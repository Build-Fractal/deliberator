# Cross-Review: scope-boundary's Review of Spec 010

**Cross-reviewer**: compliance
**Reviewing**: scope-boundary's Phase 1 review

### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Constitution Principle IV resolution strategy**
  - **scope-boundary claims**: The constitution Principle IV tension should be resolved by either (a) adding a note to the Known Antipatterns section acknowledging Principle IV predates the catalog and should be reviewed in a follow-up spec, or (b) adding a "When This Does NOT Apply" exception in the antipattern for the specific STATUS.md usage Principle IV mandates. scope-boundary frames this as a P2 recommendation and explicitly notes "the implementation correctly did not modify Principle IV without a spec" (Missed Opportunities, first item; Actionable Recommendation 2).
  - **compliance claims**: Constitution Principle IV must be amended directly — specifically, the STATUS.md mandate should be removed or reworded to reference deriving status from speckit artifacts. compliance frames this as a P1 blocking defect and cites the constitution's own governance clause (L196-199) to argue that the constitution's MUST overrides the catalog's correction, making the antipattern "effectively dead letter" if left unresolved (Actionable Recommendation 2; Off-Base Assumptions, first item).
  - **Why this is dangerous**: These two positions prescribe mutually exclusive remediation strategies. scope-boundary's approach preserves Principle IV intact and adds an acknowledgment note — meaning STATUS.md maintenance remains a MUST-level constitutional mandate. compliance's approach removes or rewrites the mandate — meaning Principle IV's text changes substantively. If the synthesis adopts both, implementors receive two conflicting instructions: amend Principle IV (compliance) and do not amend Principle IV without a spec (scope-boundary). The ambiguity is especially sharp because scope-boundary explicitly validates the decision not to modify Principle IV, while compliance explicitly calls for modifying it.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Both reviews agree the tension is real and high-impact. The cooperative path is scope-boundary's procedural concern (changes to constitutional principles require a spec) combined with compliance's substantive concern (the MUST-level conflict makes the antipattern dead letter). Resolution: create a follow-up spec (as scope-boundary suggests) whose explicit scope is to amend Principle IV (as compliance demands). This decouples the procedural discipline from the substantive fix. In the interim, a note in the Known Antipatterns section should acknowledge the conflict, as scope-boundary recommends, so agents encountering both documents have a resolution path.

- **T006 traceability: scope violation vs. reasonable extension**
  - **scope-boundary claims**: T006 (constitution update) "goes beyond the spec's stated scope" since spec.md L109 lists only SKILL.md and templates as integration points. scope-boundary flags this as a "missed opportunity" for traceability and recommends either amending the spec's Assumptions section or adding an acceptance scenario for the constitution update (Missed Opportunities, third item; Actionable Recommendation 3).
  - **compliance claims**: T006 is recorded as a verified pass in the Task Completion Verification Matrix (T006 row: "Pass"). compliance's Alignment section states "The constitution's 'Known Antipatterns' section references antipatterns/catalog.md and describes the SKILL.md enforcement mechanism, satisfying T006" (Alignment, sixth bullet). compliance does not flag T006 as a scope issue.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: scope-boundary identifies T006 as formally ungrounded in the spec, while compliance accepts T006 as fully satisfied without noting the traceability gap. If the synthesis follows compliance's position, T006 passes without qualification, establishing a precedent that implementation tasks can extend beyond spec-defined integration points without formal grounding. If the synthesis follows scope-boundary's position, T006 needs retroactive spec coverage — but compliance's verification matrix already marks it as "Pass" with no caveats.
  - **Suggested resolution**: scope-boundary is correct on the traceability point — the spec does not list constitution.md as an integration point, so T006 lacks formal spec coverage. compliance is correct that the implementation itself is well-formed. The synthesis should note T006 as "implemented correctly but with a spec traceability gap" and recommend adding constitution.md to the spec's Assumptions as an affected artifact, as scope-boundary suggests. This preserves compliance's pass verdict while addressing scope-boundary's procedural concern.

- **Priority classification of the broken example path**
  - **scope-boundary claims**: The broken example path (`specs/001-antipattern-steering/` instead of `specs/010-antipattern-steering/`) is the "single factual error in the implementation" and is classified as P1. However, scope-boundary frames it primarily through FR-004 (self-contained entries) and SC-004 (real incident references), treating it as a factual error that undermines the entry's credibility (Executive Summary; Actionable Recommendation 1).
  - **compliance claims**: The broken path is also classified as P1, but compliance additionally uses it to downgrade FR-002 to "Partially implemented" and T003 and T011 to "Fail" status in the verification matrices (Requirement Verification Matrix; Task Completion Verification Matrix). compliance treats the broken path as a systemic validation failure — not just a typo — because T011 explicitly claims to have validated cross-references but did not catch the error (Missed Opportunities, first item; Actionable Recommendation 3).
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Both reviews agree the path is broken and P1, but they differ on the blast radius. scope-boundary treats it as a localized factual error in the catalog and tasks.md. compliance treats it as evidence of a deeper validation process failure (T011 claimed completion despite not actually validating paths). If the synthesis only fixes the path (scope-boundary's framing), the underlying validation gap persists. If the synthesis also redesigns the validation process (compliance's framing), it may be over-scoped for a P1 fix.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Both framings are valid simultaneously. The immediate fix is scope-boundary's: correct the path in catalog.md and tasks.md. The structural fix is compliance's: T011's validation methodology should require actual path-existence checks, not just textual review. The synthesis should prescribe both — the path fix as P1, the validation process improvement as P2 — since they operate at different levels and do not conflict.

### Tensions

- **Scope of "blocking defect" classification**
  - **scope-boundary's position**: scope-boundary identifies only the broken example path as a factual error. The constitution Principle IV tension is categorized as a "Missed Opportunity" — high-impact but not a blocking defect. scope-boundary explicitly states the implementation "correctly did not modify Principle IV without a spec" (Missed Opportunities, first item).
  - **compliance's position**: compliance classifies both the broken path and the Principle IV contradiction as blocking defects. compliance's Executive Summary states both are "blocking defects" that should be resolved "before declaring this spec complete" (Executive Summary).
  - **Nature of tension**: scope-boundary applies a procedural lens — blocking defects are things the implementation got wrong, while tensions with pre-existing documents are missed opportunities. compliance applies a functional lens — anything that would cause agents to receive contradictory MUST-level instructions is a blocking defect regardless of where the contradiction originates. These are not mutually exclusive framings, but they produce different priority hierarchies for the same issue.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis must decide whether "blocking" means "this implementation has a bug" (scope-boundary's standard) or "an agent using this system will encounter a failure" (compliance's standard). Both are legitimate. The cooperative resolution is to adopt compliance's functional standard for the defect classification while preserving scope-boundary's procedural insight that the fix requires its own spec.

- **Completeness of the verification approach**
  - **scope-boundary's position**: scope-boundary's review contains no verification matrices. The review operates through narrative analysis of each alignment point, missed opportunity, and recommendation. Evidence is cited by line number but not tabulated (Alignment section; Missed Opportunities section).
  - **compliance's position**: compliance includes four detailed verification matrices: Requirement Verification (FR-001 through SC-004), Task Completion (T001 through T011), Catalog Entry Field Validation, and Summary Index Consistency Check (all in Detailed Requirement Verification Matrix and subsequent sections).
  - **Nature of tension**: The two reviews arrive at similar conclusions through different methodologies. scope-boundary's narrative approach catches context-dependent issues (like the constitution tension) more naturally. compliance's matrix approach catches systematic gaps (like FR-002 being only "Partially implemented") more reliably. Neither approach alone covers both dimensions.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should use compliance's matrices as the structural backbone for the final verdict, enriched with scope-boundary's contextual analysis. Specifically, scope-boundary's Missed Opportunities items should be mapped into compliance's matrix as additional rows or notes, ensuring nothing falls through the cracks between the two formats.

- **Deprecation example gap: actionable or not**
  - **scope-boundary's position**: scope-boundary notes "No deprecation example in seed" as a low-impact missed opportunity, observing that "User Story 3 acceptance scenario testing would benefit from a deprecated example to verify the Summary Index exclusion behavior" (Missed Opportunities, fifth item).
  - **compliance's position**: compliance does not flag the absence of a deprecated entry. The Requirement Verification Matrix marks FR-009 as "Implemented" based on the Maintenance section documenting the deprecation workflow (Requirement Verification Matrix, FR-009 row).
  - **Nature of tension**: scope-boundary applies a testing-completeness lens — the deprecation mechanism is defined but untested because there is no deprecated entry to exercise it. compliance applies a requirements-satisfaction lens — FR-009 says entries "MAY be deprecated" and the mechanism for doing so exists, so the requirement is met. The tension is between "requirement implemented" and "requirement verified," which are different standards.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should note FR-009 as "implemented but not exercised." This is not a defect — FR-009 uses MAY, not MUST — but scope-boundary's point about testing the Summary Index exclusion behavior is valid for future catalog growth. A note in the synthesis acknowledging this gap is sufficient without upgrading it to a recommendation.

- **SC-002 and SC-003 measurability**
  - **scope-boundary's position**: scope-boundary does not explicitly discuss SC-002 or SC-003 measurability. The review's focus is on scope boundaries and factual correctness, not on success criteria testability.
  - **compliance's position**: compliance marks SC-001 as "Not measurable," SC-002 as "Not measurable," and SC-003 as "Not testable at current scale" (Requirement Verification Matrix). compliance recommends defining measurement mechanisms for SC-001 (Recommendation 5) and clarifying SC-003's "performance" threshold (Recommendation 6).
  - **Nature of tension**: scope-boundary's silence on SC measurability is not a disagreement — it reflects the different mandates of the two roles. However, the synthesis must decide whether unmeasurable success criteria constitute a compliance gap (compliance's framing) or are simply out of scope-boundary's review mandate (which focuses on whether the implementation exceeded or fell short of scope).
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should incorporate compliance's SC measurability findings without attributing them to scope-boundary. These are compliance-specific observations that scope-boundary neither supports nor contradicts.

- **Summary Index keyword-sync durability**
  - **scope-boundary's position**: scope-boundary recommends adding an explicit Maintenance section bullet requiring verification that Summary Index keywords exactly match entry keywords, noting that "mismatches break keyword-based retrieval" (Actionable Recommendation 5).
  - **compliance's position**: compliance notes the bidirectional sync requirement (data-model.md L33) as a missed opportunity for automated validation but focuses the recommendation on adding a "field-count validation" checklist to the Maintenance section (Missed Opportunities, fourth item; Actionable Recommendation 7).
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews identify the same durability risk — manual sync between the index and entries will break as the catalog grows — but prescribe different mitigations. scope-boundary wants a keyword-specific sync reminder. compliance wants a broader field-validation checklist. These are complementary, not conflicting, but implementing both could produce redundant Maintenance section content.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should recommend a single, consolidated Maintenance checklist that covers both keyword sync (scope-boundary's concern) and field-count validation (compliance's concern), rather than two separate additions that partially overlap.

### Safe Agreements

- **Broken example path is the highest-priority factual defect**
  - **Shared position**: Both reviews identify the `specs/001-antipattern-steering/examples/redundant-cache/` path as incorrect (should be `specs/010-antipattern-steering/examples/redundant-cache/`), classify it as P1, trace the error to propagation from design documents (tasks.md) into the implementation, and cite SC-004 and FR-004 as the violated requirements. scope-boundary: Executive Summary and Actionable Recommendation 1. compliance: Executive Summary, Actionable Recommendation 1, Requirement Verification Matrix (FR-002, SC-004), Task Completion Matrix (T003, T011).
  - **Combined evidence**: scope-boundary verifies the correct path exists on the filesystem. compliance traces the propagation chain through tasks.md L38, L64, L77. Together, the evidence confirms: (1) the correct directory exists at `specs/010-antipattern-steering/examples/redundant-cache/`, (2) the error originated in the task definitions, (3) T011's cross-reference validation did not catch it, and (4) the fix is mechanical — find-and-replace `001` with `010` across catalog.md and tasks.md.
  - **Confidence level**: High. Both reviews independently identified this as the single most important factual fix. The combined evidence is comprehensive and the remediation is unambiguous.

- **Constitution Principle IV creates a real, high-impact tension with the seed antipattern**
  - **Shared position**: Both reviews identify constitution.md Principle IV (L73-75, mandating STATUS.md maintenance) as contradicting the `redundant-cache` antipattern (catalog.md L27-33, identifying STATUS.md as a redundant cache). Together, these analyses establish that the tension is both procedurally and functionally significant: it was right not to fix it within spec 010's scope, but it must be fixed soon or the antipattern system's foundational entry is undermined.
  - **Confidence level**: High. Both reviews arrive at the same finding through independent analytical frameworks (scope discipline vs. requirements compliance), and both rate the impact as their second-highest concern.

Together, the evidence confirms the implementation stayed within its stated boundaries. The one scope extension (T006, constitution update) was additive and non-destructive.
