## Dispute 1: Heading Governance Symmetry

### Arbiter's Recommended Language

The arbiter recommended: "describe the data flow as directional (template-owner is upstream producer), describe the governance as bilateral (SKILL.md line 683 governs), and note as an observed pattern (not a formal rule) that new headings typically originate from template content."

### My Position: Accept with Minor Refinement

I accept the arbiter's recommendation. It captures the substance of my position without overreaching into "first-mover authority" language that engine-owner legitimately objected to.

**Why I accept**: My Round 1 flexibility statement already said I could accept "bilateral with template-first origination pattern." The arbiter's formulation is substantively identical -- it describes the origination pattern as observed reality rather than formal governance authority. The key elements I needed are preserved:

1. The data flow is directional (template produces, engine consumes) -- explicitly stated.
2. The historical record shows new headings originating from template content (spec 004's `## Disputed Boundaries` and `### [` sub-headings are the clearest example) -- acknowledged as observed pattern.
3. The governance protocol is bilateral per SKILL.md line 683 -- preserved without dilution.

**My refinement**: I propose one small addition to the arbiter's language. The arbiter says "new headings typically originate from template content." I would add a brief explanatory clause: "because templates are where mode-specific analytical structure is defined." This is not an assertion of authority -- it is an explanation of why the observed pattern exists. Without the explanatory clause, a future reader might assume the pattern is coincidental rather than structural. The pattern holds because game-theoretic framing (which determines what headings are needed) lives exclusively in templates, which is agreed territory.

**Proposed final language for the boundary map**:

> Dispute headings: template-owner is the upstream producer; engine-owner is the downstream consumer via the Dispute-Parsing Subsystem. The governance protocol is bilateral per SKILL.md line 683 -- neither side changes unilaterally. As an observed pattern, new headings typically originate from template content, because templates are where mode-specific analytical structure is defined. The engine evaluates feasibility and updates the Dispute-Parsing Subsystem accordingly. Either side may propose heading changes; both must coordinate before implementation.

**What I will not accept**: Any formulation that describes the origination pattern as purely coincidental or that implies the engine is equally likely to originate new headings. The structural reason (game-theoretic framing is template-only) makes template origination the expected case, not a historical accident. However, I accept that engine-originated heading changes are possible (e.g., if a parsing optimization required heading restructuring) and that the governance protocol does not privilege either side's proposals.

### Concession Verification

This position is consistent with my Round 1 concessions:
- I continue to not claim "source of truth" governance authority.
- I continue to not claim unilateral change rights.
- I accept SKILL.md line 683 as the governing rule.
- I do not use the phrase "first-mover authority."

## Dispute 2: Variable Proposal Direction

### Arbiter's Recommended Language

The arbiter recommended: "the variable availability contract permits either side to propose new variables. In practice, template-owner is the typical demand-side initiator (consumption requirements surface in template development). The engine evaluates feasibility and implements."

### My Position: Accept Fully

I accept the arbiter's recommendation without modification. It is precisely what I asked for in my Round 1 flexibility statement: "bilateral negotiation provided it acknowledges template-owner as the typical demand-side initiator."

**Why I accept**: The arbiter correctly identified that the substantive disagreement was rhetorical, not operational. Engine-owner's own Round 1 flexibility statement said: "I am open to template-owner proposing new variables through the shared interface contract. The engine evaluates feasibility and implements; the template motivates the need." That is the request-fulfill pattern I described, using engine-owner's own words. The arbiter's formulation simply makes this explicit in the boundary map without privileging either side's framing.

**Proposed final language for the boundary map**:

> Variable availability contract: the variable set is jointly maintained. The engine documents and provides variables; templates consume only documented variables. Neither side unilaterally adds or removes variables. The engine commits to deprecation cycles for removals; templates commit to consuming only documented variables. Either side may propose new variables. In practice, template-owner is the typical demand-side initiator, because consumption requirements surface during template development. The engine evaluates feasibility and implements. This is bilateral governance with an observed demand-side pattern.

**What I will not accept**: Any formulation that removes the "typical demand-side initiator" acknowledgment entirely, or that implies variable proposals are equally likely to originate from either side. The structural reason is clear: templates discover consumption gaps when building mode-specific analytical frameworks, which is where new variable needs surface. The engine may also identify variable opportunities (e.g., recognizing that a computed intermediate value would be useful to expose), and I accept that the governance protocol should accommodate this.

### Concession Verification

This position is consistent with my Round 1 concessions:
- I do not claim exclusive proposal rights.
- I accept "bilateral negotiation" as the governance model.
- I accept "jointly maintained" as the overall framing.
- I do not characterize the engine's role as subordinate.
