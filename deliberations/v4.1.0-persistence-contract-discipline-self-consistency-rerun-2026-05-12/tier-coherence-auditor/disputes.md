# Remaining Disputes

### Remaining Disputes

- **Dispute: Temporal vs Membership Universality for Deadlines**
  - **My claim**: Modified Recommendation 1 from my revision distinguishes between temporal universality (applying to all products retroactively) and membership universality (applying to all products upon admission). The universal 2026-12-01 deadline should apply to products existing at ratification, with future siblings receiving admission-time deadlines per § 7 provisions.
  - **Opposing position(s)**: Purist's Recommendation 2 maintains that "universal means uniform application, period" and that ANY product-specific accommodations, even beneficial ones like acceleration or admission-time deadlines, fundamentally undermine what "universal" means constitutionally.
  - **Why I will not concede**: The logical impossibility of retroactive deadlines for non-existent products remains valid constitutional concern. A principle cannot be "universal" if it contains logical contradictions. The temporal vs membership distinction preserves universality within temporal scope while avoiding constitutional impossibility.
  - **Counter-argument to their position**: Purist's position creates the absurdity that future conversus siblings must either (a) inherit impossible retroactive obligations for dates before they existed, or (b) the amendment can never apply to future siblings, making it non-universal by purist's own standard. The temporal scope distinction solves both problems.
  - **Proposed resolution path**: Synthesizer must choose between logical impossibility (purist's position) vs temporal scope preservation of universality (my position). The spec language should clarify temporal applicability bounds.

- **Dispute: Enforcement-Constitutional Coordination vs Sequential Priority**
  - **My claim**: New Recommendation 1 from my revision - enforcement mechanism design and tier coherence validation should be addressed as complementary P1 concerns in parallel, with enforcement mechanism strength supporting the tier placement's constitutional adequacy.
  - **Opposing position(s)**: Precedent-auditor's New Recommendation 1 argues for sequencing constitutional validity before procedural robustness, stating "constitutional validity questions (does the principle meet basic constitutional standards) must be resolved before procedural infrastructure questions (what enforcement mechanisms should be built)."
  - **Why I will not concede**: Precedent-auditor correctly identified these as mutually informing domains but incorrectly concluded they should be sequential. Tier placement adequacy and enforcement mechanism strength are constitutionally interdependent - a principle's tier placement partly depends on whether it can be mechanically enforced at that tier's scope.
  - **Counter-argument to their position**: Sequential treatment creates artificial prioritization where coordinated treatment preserves the structural relationship. Constitutional adequacy and enforcement robustness inform each other; treating them as separable creates incomplete analysis of either domain.
  - **Proposed resolution path**: Add explicit language requiring parallel analysis of tier placement and enforcement mechanism design, with recognition of their mutual dependency rather than artificial sequencing.

### Final Position Statement

**Non-Negotiables**:

- **Retroactive deadline logical impossibility resolution**: The temporal vs membership universality distinction (Modified Recommendation 1) addresses a genuine logical contradiction that cannot be resolved by ignoring it. Constitutional principles cannot contain logical impossibilities.

- **Evidence base verification**: New Recommendation 2 requiring explicit verification of spec v3 L8 and L15 evidence citations is essential because factual disputes about evidence scope undermine constitutional foundation assessment.

- **Cross-tier compliance attestation**: Recommendation 4 providing verification that the amendment satisfies Tier 2 CONSTITUTION.md L475-488 weakening prohibition is required constitutional documentation that strengthens rather than duplicates existing analysis.

**Flexibility**:

- **Enforcement-constitutional coordination timing**: While I advocate for parallel treatment (New Recommendation 1), I am flexible on the specific coordination language if it preserves the core requirement that tier placement analysis and enforcement mechanism design inform each other rather than proceeding in artificial isolation.

- **Constitutional Inclusion Criteria analysis expansion**: Recommendation 7 could be modified in scope or location within § 8 as long as it provides more thorough demonstration of gate compliance than currently exists.

- **Suite membership definition placement**: Recommendation 3 could be located in different sections (§ 2 goals, § 3 non-goals, or § 8 verification) as long as the boundary definition function is preserved.
