### Recommendation 1: Clarify retroactive deadline handling

- **Original position**: Future products joining post-2026-12-01 should receive admission-time deadlines rather than retroactive 2026-12-01 obligation to resolve "constitutional impossibility: retroactive deadlines for products that didn't exist when the deadline was set."
- **Disposition**: Modified
- **Explanation**: purist's cross-review raised a fundamental challenge about what "universal" means in constitutional context. Their argument that "universal means uniform application, period" and that accommodations undermine universality has merit. However, the logical impossibility of retroactive deadlines for non-existent products remains valid. I modify this to distinguish between temporal universality (applying to all products retroactively) and membership universality (applying to all products upon admission). The recommendation becomes: "Clarify that the universal 2026-12-01 deadline applies to all products that exist as of the amendment's ratification. Future siblings joining post-deadline receive admission-time deadlines per the forward-sibling provisions in § 7, which preserves universality within the temporal scope while avoiding logical impossibility."

## New Recommendations

### New Recommendation 1: Coordinate enforcement mechanism with tier placement analysis

- **Priority**: P1
- **Triggered by**: precedent-auditor's cross-review identifying the tension between "enforcement priority vs tier placement priority" and their observation that "both concerns should be addressed as complementary P1 concerns."
- **Proposed change**: Add explicit coordination in the spec that enforcement mechanism design and tier coherence validation should be addressed as complementary P1 concerns, with enforcement mechanism strength supporting the tier placement's constitutional adequacy.
- **Rationale**: precedent-auditor correctly identified that constitutional adequacy (tier placement) and operational robustness (enforcement) are mutually informing domains that require parallel rather than sequential treatment. My original focus on tier placement analysis alone was incomplete without considering enforcement coordination.

### New Recommendation 2: Verify evidence base citations explicitly

- **Priority**: P1
- **Triggered by**: Both purist and precedent-auditor raising "factual disagreements about evidence scope" and the tension around whether concrete conversus-enhanced persistence artifacts are documented.
- **Proposed change**: Add explicit verification in tier placement analysis that spec v3 L8 and L15 evidence citations include conversus-enhanced consumer surfaces alongside conversus-oss and spec-kit-orc, with specific line-number references to resolve the factual disagreement.
- **Rationale**: The cross-reviews revealed factual uncertainty about what evidence actually exists in the spec. This verification is prerequisite to confident tier placement analysis - if evidence scope is disputed, the constitutional foundation is uncertain.

## Position Summary

I withdrew 0 recommendations, modified 3, and maintained 4, while adding 2 new recommendations that emerged from cross-review tensions. The most significant change in my thinking is recognizing that enforcement mechanism design and tier coherence validation are complementary P1 concerns rather than separate domains, prompted by precedent-auditor's analysis of their mutual dependency.

My highest-priority surviving recommendation remains clarifying retroactive deadline handling (modified Recommendation 1), as this addresses the most fundamental constitutional inconsistency: v3's universal deadline cannot logically apply to products that didn't exist when the deadline was set. The modification preserving universality through temporal scope boundaries while avoiding logical impossibility represents a principled resolution that satisfies both constitutional theory and practical implementation.

The cross-review process strengthened rather than undermined my core position that v3's tier placement is constitutionally sound but requires clarifications on forward sibling obligations and evidence base verification to be fully robust.
