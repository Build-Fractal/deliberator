### New Recommendations

- **Reclassify version bump from MINOR (v2.6.0) to PATCH (v2.5.1)** (Priority: P2)
  - **Triggered by**: Gate-skeptic's cross-review of CPC, Dangerous Contradiction 1 ("Version classification: PATCH vs. MINOR"). My original review accepted the 2.6.0 MINOR classification without scrutiny; gate-skeptic's cross-review explicitly identified this as an off-base assumption on my part: "cross-principle-coherence accepts the 2.6.0 (MINOR) classification without challenge. Its recommendations treat the amendment as a legitimate MINOR expansion."
  - **Proposed change**: Reclassify this amendment as v2.5.1 (PATCH) for the XVI headline rewrite component. The SIR's own framing — "the structural substrate already living in XVI's body IS mechanically verifiable" — confirms the rewrite reorganizes existing content rather than introducing material expansion. The constitution's Versioning rule assigns MINOR to "new principles or material expansions" and PATCH to "clarifications." Elevating existing body content to headline status is a clarification. If the Governance gate text amendment (Modified Rec 2 — defining path (c)) is treated as a materially new normative addition to the Governance section, that addition may independently warrant MINOR — in which case the amendment package would be v2.6.0 for the governance addition, with the XVI headline rewrite explicitly noted as PATCH-within-MINOR.   - **Rationale**: Accepting MINOR for content reorganization sets precedent that any grandfathered-principle amendment promoting existing body sentences to headline status constitutes material expansion. The version classification is not cosmetic — it determines what future amendments can claim under this precedent, particularly for Principles VI and X (also failing under the gate, also candidates for the same remediation path). Gate-skeptic's Recommendation 3b provides the correct diagnostic: identify whether a specific new normative requirement justifies MINOR; if none can be identified, correct the classification before ratification.

  - **Triggered by**: Gate-skeptic's cross-review of CPC (Dangerous Contradiction 2, "Primary diagnosis of the plain-language pairing failure") and Safe Agreement 4 ("'plain-language explanation' requires structural definition for the principle to be self-contained"). ### Position Summary

I modified six of my original seven recommendations (Recs 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7), maintained one unchanged (Rec 3), withdrew zero, and added two new recommendations (version reclassification at P2, structural definition of "plain-language explanation" at P1). The pattern of modification was not random: almost all modifications involved accepting a sequencing constraint, a scope constraint, or a prerequisite I had missed — not a fundamental change in direction. The underlying concerns I identified in Phase 1 survived; what changed was the order and completeness in which they must be addressed.

The most significant change in my thinking was accepting gate-skeptic's argument that the structural definition of "plain-language explanation" is not a missed opportunity to note in passing but a prerequisite that must precede the enforcement mode clause and the schema lint. My original Phase 1 review identified the XV/XVI enforcement conflict as P1 and treated it as self-sufficient. Gate-skeptic's Dangerous Contradiction 2 exposed the flaw: an enforcement mode clause for a condition that cannot be mechanically identified is aspirational rather than operational. The two cross-reviews' independent convergence on this — WP from Criterion 2 falsifiability, gate-skeptic from schema lint implementability — is the strongest single piece of evidence produced by the Phase 2 process, and it correctly demotes my original P1 (enforcement mode alone) to a joint P1 package (structural definition + enforcement mode, in that order). I also accept gate-skeptic's version classification argument: I had no basis for accepting MINOR without scrutiny, and the SIR's own framing of the rewrite as reorganizing existing content is self-defeating evidence for a MINOR bump.

My highest-priority remaining recommendation is the joint package of New Rec B (structural definition of "plain-language explanation") and Modified Rec 1 (enforcement mode clause) — the two must ship atomically as a single change to XVI's third bullet. This package addresses the only requirement among the three headline claims (parameter pinning, shape determinism, plain-language output pairing) that currently lacks both a structural definition in the principle body and body-level enforcement sub-bullets. It is the one location where the amendment's gate compliance claim ("mechanically verifiable, falsifiable") is not yet supportable from the principle text alone, creating precisely the self-referential compliance gap the v2.4.0 gate was designed to prevent. Unlike the governance amendments (Modified Recs 2, 7) and the Verification block (Modified Rec 4), which can be sequenced as complementary additions, the structural definition and enforcement mode gap makes requirement (c)'s headline claim actively unsupportable from the body text — the same structural condition that made the pre-v2.6.0 headline fail Criterion 1.
