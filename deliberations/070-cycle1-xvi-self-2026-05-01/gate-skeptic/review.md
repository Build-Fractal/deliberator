### Executive Summary

The v2.6.0 amendment to Principle XVI claims to close the grandfathering gap identified in v2.4.0 by reorganizing XVI's existing structural substrate (parameter pinning, shape determinism, plain-language output pairing) from body content into headline status, simultaneously demoting the original "user MUST understand" framing to a derived design-intent gloss. The strategy is correct in outline: the three structural requirements that have lived in XVI's body since v2.3.2 are genuinely more mechanically verifiable than the original headline, and elevating them produces a cleaner constitutional claim. The problem is execution. The design-intent paragraph that explains *why* the structural requirements matter smuggles the original falsifiability gap back into the principle's normative territory through three parenthetical glosses — "auditable," "no surprise math," and "no bare numbers" — each of which partially re-introduces a reader-relative predicate that Criterion 1 rejected in the original headline. One of the three ("no bare numbers") is genuinely mechanical; the other two are not.

The more structurally significant problem is version classification. The SIR's own framing defeats the MINOR claim: "The structural substrate already living in XVI's body IS mechanically verifiable, falsifiable, and distinct." If the substrate already lived there, the rewrite adds no new normative content — it reorganizes existing content from body to headline. Under the constitution's own versioning rules, reorganization-without-addition is a PATCH clarification, not a MINOR expansion. The Criterion 3 self-assessment compounds this: it correctly argues that the substrate is distinct from other principles, but that argument has been true since v2.3.2; it is an argument about the existing body, not about what the rewrite adds. Applying Criterion 3 to a within-principle reorganization answers the wrong question.

The precedent concern is the longest-tailed risk. The v2.4.0 governance disclosure anticipated two remediation paths for grandfathered principles: keep them grandfathered, or migrate them to operational guidance. Option A establishes a third path — rewrite the headline to pass the gate retroactively — without governance sanction. Any future grandfathered principle that fails Criterion 1 can now cite Option A as justification for an identical maneuver: locate mechanically-verifiable body content, promote it to headline, declare the gap closed. The constitution has no defense against this because the Governance section does not define when path (c) is legitimate. The single most important recommendation is to excise the interpretive glosses ("auditable," "no surprise math") from the design-intent paragraph and add a governance definition of path (c) that constrains its future use.

### Alignment

- **Structural requirements as constitutional headline** (SIR, Criterion 1 self-assessment block): The rewrite correctly identifies that parameter pinning, shape determinism, and plain-language output pairing are each more tractable for mechanical enforcement than "user MUST understand." The constitution's Criterion 1 standard explicitly accepts that "the check does NOT have to exist at amendment time" — what matters is sketchability. For parameter pinning, the existing body already cites spec 014 FR-012/SC-004 and the re-entrant `GapFiller.fill()` CI lint; the sketchability bar is met. Promoting these requirements to headline status is the correct structural move. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, Criterion 1]

- **Demotion of "user MUST understand" to derived property** (Principle XVI, opening paragraph): The original headline claim is repositioned as design intent, not constitutional requirement. This is the correct architectural move: the Criterion 1 problem was the word "MUST" attached to an interpretive outcome ("understand"). Removing MUST from the interpretive claim and attaching MUST to the three structural requirements structurally closes the original gap, regardless of whether the execution of that move is clean. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Principle XVI headline]

- **Preservation of Falsification sub-bullet** (XVI body, Clarification v2.3.2): The falsification clause ("A future PR that re-resolves parameters mid-deliberation, or that lets parameter values drift during a single optimization run, violates this principle") is preserved unchanged. For parameter pinning specifically, this satisfies Criterion 2 without interpretation: the violation is structurally detectable via diff-shape (production-source edit to `GapFiller.fill()` call sites). [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, XVI body, Clarification v2.3.2 — Falsification sub-bullet]

- **Parameter-pinning enforcement chain is concrete** (XVI body, Clarification v2.3.2): The enforcement path for parameter pinning is the most fully specified of the three requirements. The existing body references `objective.yml` persistence, spec 014 FR-012/SC-004 acceptance criteria, `SourceProvenance.filled_by` at `construction.py:262-279`, and a filed follow-up for the CI lint. An engineer can sketch the check from the principle text alone: assert that `GapFiller.fill()` is called at most once per `objective.yml` artifact lifetime; assert that subsequent reads come from `objective.yml` not from re-invocation. This is the strongest of the three enforcement paths. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, XVI body, Clarification v2.3.2 — Enforcement sub-bullet]

### Missed Opportunities

- **Removing interpretive glosses from the design-intent paragraph entirely**: The design-intent paragraph introduces three parenthetical glosses — "(auditable)," "(no surprise math)," "(no bare numbers)" — to explain why the structural requirements achieve the design intent. The constitution's Criterion 1 gate applies to principle wording, not to labeled sections; parenthetical language in a "design-intent" paragraph is still constitutional text and still fails Criterion 1 if it is reader-relative. The amendment could have written: "user-provided parameters stay pinned (persisted to `objective.yml` and re-loaded by process), the assembled objective function is byte-identical given identical inputs, and every numerical recommendation field has an adjacent string-typed explanation field" — three mechanical paraphrases of the structural requirements, not three interpretive glosses. *Impact: high.* [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Principle XVI opening paragraph]

- **Defining "plain-language explanation" structurally**: The plain-language output pairing requirement relies on a single example ("87% of agents are at their best possible position given others' positions") rather than a structural definition. An engineer implementing the schema lint needs to know what field shape counts as a valid pairing — is `equilibrium_score_note: "a measure of agent satisfaction"` sufficient? Is minimum character count relevant? The amendment could have added: "An explanation field is a string-typed output field whose name is `{numerical_field}_explanation` and whose content expresses the numerical value's meaning in domain terms, not in numerical terms." This would allow the schema lint to check presence and naming convention mechanically, and a content audit to verify the domain-terms requirement. Without this, the quality dimension of "plain-language" is permanently interpretive. *Impact: high.* [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Principle XVI third bullet]

- **Filing follow-up tickets for parity test and schema lint before ratification**: The SIR claims Criterion 1 is satisfied for shape determinism (b) and plain-language pairing (c) because enforcement mechanisms are "concretely sketchable." But (b) and (c) have no filed follow-up tickets, unlike (a) which cites specific spec numbers and a filed lint follow-up. The sketchability standard is not binary: the constitution's gate distinguishes between "an engineer can sketch the check in one paragraph" (acceptable) and "the check is filed, numbered, and tracked" (stronger). For (a), the stronger bar is met. For (b) and (c), only the weaker bar is claimed. The amendment should have filed GitHub issues for the parity test and schema lint before ratification, then cited the issue numbers in the SIR. *Impact: medium.* [SIR, Criterion 1 self-assessment]

- **Reclassifying as PATCH with explicit governance rationale**: The version bump to 2.6.0 (MINOR) signals that the rewrite is a "material expansion." But the SIR's own language ("the structural substrate already living in XVI's body") identifies it as a reorganization. The amendment missed the opportunity to classify correctly as PATCH and explain why headline-reorganization-of-existing-body-content is the right category — which would also have preempted the precedent concern by naming this as a limited, PATCH-class operation rather than a MINOR expansion. *Impact: medium.* [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Governance — Versioning]

- **Adding path (c) to the Governance section**: The v2.4.0 disclosure described two grandfathered-principle remediation paths. Option A establishes a third path without defining it. The amendment missed the opportunity to add a one-paragraph governance definition: "Path (c): structural amendment that elevates mechanically-verifiable body content to headline status. Path (c) is a PATCH amendment; the gate applies only to normative requirements newly introduced by the structural amendment, not to grandfathered body content." Without this, path (c) is used once without definition, creating an ad hoc precedent. *Impact: medium.* [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, prospective-only paragraph]

- **Replacing "auditable" with a machine-readable referent**: "Auditable" in the design-intent paragraph ("user-provided parameters stay pinned (auditable)") is doing ambiguous work. The existing body at Clarification v2.3.2 already says what machine-readable auditability means: `SourceProvenance.filled_by` at `construction.py:262-279`, a sanctioned form of explicit keyed persistence. The amendment could have written "(via `SourceProvenance.filled_by`)" in place of "(auditable)" — a direct reference to the mechanical artifact rather than a quality that requires a reader to assess. *Impact: low.* [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, XVI body — Clarification v2.3.2, Observability sub-bullet]

- **Cutting "no surprise math" entirely**: "Template shapes are stable (no surprise math)" is the weakest of the three glosses. "Surprise" is defined relative to a reference population of readers — what surprises a non-mathematician does not surprise a game-theorist. The design intent the gloss is trying to express (that template shapes do not shift unpredictably) is already captured by the structural requirement "shape determinism" and the Clarification v2.3.2 prohibition on cross-run shape variance. The gloss adds no information and introduces a reader-relative predicate. It should be cut with no replacement. *Impact: low.* [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Principle XVI opening paragraph]

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **"Design-intent" labeling neutralizes Criterion 1 problems in that paragraph**: The SIR assumes that positioning the original "user MUST understand" framing as a "derived property" in a design-intent paragraph removes it from the scope of the Criterion 1 gate. This is incorrect. The gate's standard — "at least one form of automated check MUST be feasible such that a future PR violating the principle would fail the check" — applies to the principle's wording, not to labeled sections within the principle. A design-intent paragraph that includes "auditable" and "no surprise math" is still constitutional text. The gate does not exempt interpretive language from Criterion 1 based on the paragraph's declared purpose; it checks whether the wording, taken as a whole, is mechanically verifiable. Labeling a paragraph "design intent" does not partition it from the rest of the principle for gating purposes. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, Criterion 1]

- **Criterion 3 can be satisfied by a within-principle reorganization**: The SIR's Criterion 3 self-assessment argues that the structural substrate "covers ground no other principle covers." This is true of the existing body content (which has been present since v2.3.2 and is correctly distinct from Principles VII, VIII, IX, XI, XV, XXII, XXIV). But Criterion 3 asks whether the *amendment* introduces content distinct from existing principles — for a within-principle reorganization, this question is malformed. The principle is not being compared to other principles for distinctness; it is being reorganized internally. The SIR answers a question about the body content's distinctness from peer principles, which has been true since v2.3.2, as if it were a question about the rewrite's distinctness, which is a category error. Criterion 3 was designed to block duplicate principles; it has no defined behavior when applied to a within-principle structural reorganization. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, Criterion 3; SIR, Criterion 3 self-assessment]

- **The v2.4.0 grandfathering framework anticipated Option A**: The SIR implicitly treats headline-rewriting as a legitimate remediation path under the v2.4.0 governance framework. The v2.4.0 disclosure (preserved in the prior SIR block) explicitly described two paths: keep grandfathered, or migrate to operational guidance. The disclosure further stated "migration of any grandfathered principle to operational guidance is a separate, intentional act governed by the same amendment process." Option A is neither path (a) nor path (b) — it is a third path the disclosure did not define, sanction, or bound. The absence of governance definition means there is no constraint on when path (c) applies: any grandfathered principle could cite Option A as precedent for an identical maneuver, regardless of whether its body content is as mechanically grounded as XVI's v2.3.2 clarification. The assumption that the existing framework covers Option A is incorrect. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, prior SIR block (v2.3.2 → v2.4.0), Grandfathering disclosure paragraph]

### Actionable Recommendations

1. **Remove "auditable" and "no surprise math" from design-intent paragraph** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: "user-provided parameters stay pinned (auditable), template shapes are stable (no surprise math), and outputs are paired with explanations (no bare numbers)"
   - **Proposed change**: Replace with mechanical paraphrases: "user-provided parameters stay pinned (persisted to `objective.yml` via `SourceProvenance.filled_by`), the assembled objective function is byte-identical given identical template and pinned parameter inputs, and every numerical recommendation field in plugin output has an adjacent string-typed explanation field."
   - **Rationale**: "Auditable" is reader-relative unless anchored to a machine-readable artifact; `SourceProvenance.filled_by` provides that anchor. "No surprise math" is irreducibly reader-relative — "surprise" has no mechanical referent. The third gloss ("no bare numbers") is already mechanical (field-presence check); retaining it as-is is acceptable. The paraphrases replace interpretive language with structural claims that mirror the three structural requirements already named in the headline. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Principle XVI opening paragraph; Governance — Criterion 1]
   - **Risk if ignored**: A future gate audit will correctly identify "auditable" and "no surprise math" as Criterion 1 failures in the design-intent paragraph. The amendment will be reopened under the same rationale it was supposed to close. The grandfathering gap will have been replaced with a smaller but structurally identical gap.

2. **File and cite follow-up tickets for parity test (b) and schema lint (c) before ratification** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: SIR claims Criterion 1 is satisfied for shape determinism and plain-language pairing because checks are "concretely sketchable." No issue or ticket numbers are cited for (b) or (c), unlike (a) which cites spec 014 FR-012/SC-004 by name.
   - **Proposed change**: File GitHub issues for (b) "assembly parity test: assert byte-identical objective function output given identical template ID and pinned parameter set" and (c) "plugin recommendation schema lint: assert every numerical output field has an adjacent string-typed `{field}_explanation` field." Add issue numbers to the SIR's enforcement cross-references: "(b) parity test: issue #NNN; (c) schema lint: issue #NNN."
   - **Rationale**: The constitution's Criterion 1 gate is calibrated against "can an engineer sketch the check" not "has the check been filed." But the existing body's enforcement chain for parameter pinning (a) satisfies a higher bar: filed, numbered, cross-referenced. Claiming the same Criterion 1 status for (b) and (c) without filed tickets creates an asymmetry that will be perceived as a weaker commitment. Symmetry with (a) strengthens the case. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, SIR — Criterion 1 self-assessment; Governance — Criterion 1]
   - **Risk if ignored**: The asymmetric enforcement commitment — (a) filed and tracked, (b) and (c) promised but untracked — mirrors exactly the pattern the v2.4.0 grandfathering disclosure described for pre-gate principles: "would be verifiable, in principle." Future auditors will note the asymmetry.

3. **Reclassify version bump as PATCH or justify MINOR substantively** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: Version bump from 2.5.0 to 2.6.0 (MINOR). SIR states "MINOR — Principle XVI Option A in-place refactor."
   - **Proposed change**: Either (a) reclassify as 2.5.1 (PATCH) with explicit rationale: "PATCH — headline reorganization of existing body content; no new normative requirements introduced beyond v2.3.2 body"; or (b) identify the specific new normative requirement introduced by the rewrite and justify MINOR on those grounds. Candidate for (b): if the design-intent paragraph's mechanical paraphrases (per Recommendation 1) introduce a new normative obligation not stated in the v2.3.2 body, that would justify MINOR.
   - **Rationale**: The constitution defines MINOR as "new principles or material expansions" and PATCH as "clarifications." The SIR states the substrate "already lived in XVI's body." If the content already existed, the version bump is wrong. The existing versioning rules do not define a "reorganization" category; reorganization-without-new-content is closest to PATCH. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Governance — Versioning; SIR headline]
   - **Risk if ignored**: Miscategorizing a PATCH as MINOR signals that editorial reorganization of existing body content counts as material expansion. This precedent will be cited in future amendments to justify MINOR bumps for similarly editorial changes.

4. **Add a structural definition of "plain-language explanation"** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: "Plugin recommendations MUST include plain-language explanations alongside numerical outputs. 'Equilibrium quality: 0.87' is insufficient; '87% of agents are at their best possible position given others' positions' is required."
   - **Proposed change**: Add after the example: "A compliant explanation is a string-typed field in the plugin's recommendation output schema whose content (a) does not repeat the numerical value as its only information, (b) expresses the value's meaning in terms of the optimization domain (agent positions, convergence, satisfaction) rather than the numerical range, and (c) is machine-readable by the schema lint as a string field adjacent in schema order to its paired numerical field."
   - **Rationale**: Criterion 2 requires the principle wording to flag a violating PR without requiring reviewer interpretation. "87% of agents are at their best possible position" is one compliant instance; it does not define the boundary. A reviewer encountering "Equilibrium quality: 0.87 (a measure of agent satisfaction)" must interpret whether that passes — the principle gives no rule. The structural definition allows the schema lint to check (c) mechanically and provides reviewers with criteria (a) and (b) that are falsifiable without interpretation. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Principle XVI — third bullet; Governance — Criterion 2]
   - **Risk if ignored**: The plain-language pairing requirement is the least specified of the three structural requirements and the most likely to generate disagreement in future code review. Without a definition, every PR that adds a plugin recommendation will require human judgment about what "plain-language" means.

5. **Add path (c) to Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: The Governance section describes paths (a) (keep grandfathered) and (b) (migrate to operational guidance) implicitly through the v2.4.0 disclosure. Path (c) (headline reorganization to elevate mechanically-verifiable body content) is established by this amendment without governance definition.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to the prospective-only paragraph in Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria: "Grandfathered principles failing Criterion 1 may alternatively be remediated by structural amendment (path c): elevating mechanically-verifiable body content to headline status while demoting the non-verifiable claim to design-intent prose. Path (c) amendments are PATCH unless they introduce new normative requirements not present in the prior body. The gate applies to any new normative requirements introduced by a path (c) amendment; body content present before v2.4.0 is not re-audited by the gate."
   - **Rationale**: The v2.4.0 disclosure's two-path description ("keep grandfathered" or "migrate to operational guidance") does not cover Option A. Governance precedents established without explicit definition accumulate as ad hoc case law. A governance definition of path (c) bounds its future use (PATCH only, gate applies to new requirements) and prevents it from becoming a general license to rewrite grandfathered headlines without genuine constitutional improvement. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, prospective-only paragraph; prior SIR block, Grandfathering disclosure]
   - **Risk if ignored**: The first unconstrained use of path (c) sets the precedent for all subsequent uses. Future committees will apply it to grandfathered principles VI and X (Scripts Over Markdown, Zen of Python Output) by identifying their most mechanically-grounded sentences, promoting those to headlines, and declaring the gate satisfied — without any governance constraint distinguishing legitimate reorganization from constitutionalization-by-relabeling.

6. **Cut "no surprise math" entirely** (Priority: P3)
   - **Current state**: Design-intent paragraph gloss: "template shapes are stable (no surprise math)."
   - **Proposed change**: Delete "(no surprise math)" without replacement. If Recommendation 1 is adopted, the mechanical paraphrase of shape determinism already conveys the structural claim; the gloss is redundant and its only distinctive contribution is the reader-relative predicate "surprise."
   - **Rationale**: "No surprise math" is the weakest text in the rewrite — it has no mechanical referent, no falsification clause, and no enforcement path. Its deletion makes the design-intent paragraph strictly cleaner without losing any normative content. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, Principle XVI opening paragraph; Governance — Criterion 1]
   - **Risk if ignored**: Retention of "no surprise math" is a low-severity ongoing liability: any future gate audit will flag it, creating editorial churn at the cost of constitutional stability.

7. **Disclose the gate-scope question for within-principle reorganizations in the SIR** (Priority: P3)
   - **Current state**: The SIR's Criterion 3 self-assessment applies the distinctness criterion to the structural substrate's distinctness from peer principles — a question whose answer has been true since v2.3.2 — without acknowledging that Criterion 3 has no defined behavior for within-principle reorganizations.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to the SIR's Criterion 3 self-assessment: "Note: Criterion 3 was designed to prevent duplicate principles; its application to a within-principle reorganization is not defined by the gate's text. This SIR interprets Criterion 3 as asking whether the body content being elevated to headline status covers ground distinct from other principles, rather than whether the reorganization itself adds distinct content. This interpretation is disclosed for future governance reference."
   - **Rationale**: Unexplained application of a criterion to a case the criterion was not designed for creates ambiguity in the precedent. The disclosure converts an implicit assumption into an explicit governance record, allowing future committees to decide whether the interpretation was correct. [`CONSTITUTION-v2.6.0-candidate.md`, SIR — Criterion 3 self-assessment; Governance — Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, Criterion 3]
   - **Risk if ignored**: The unarticulated Criterion 3 interpretation propagates silently as precedent. Future amendments will apply it without the benefit of the reasoning, potentially broadening or misapplying it.
