### Position Summary

Of the seven original recommendations, one is withdrawn in its prior form (implicitly: the informal conditional-retention approach to L790 that cross-principle-coherence cited as "Rec 8"), three are modified (Recs 1, 2, 4), one is substantially modified in scope and mechanism (Rec 6), and three survive unchanged in substance (Recs 3, 5, 7). Two new recommendations emerge from cross-review analysis that represents genuine Phase 1 blind spots: the L790 self-attestation sentence as an explicit P1 removal target, and the XV enforcement-mode carve-out as an independent structural fix rather than a component of Rec 1.

The most significant change in my thinking is the XV enforcement-mode conflict. My original review noted XVI's plugin output constraint was "consistent with XV" without examining what happens at runtime when a plugin fails to comply. Cross-principle-coherence's identification of this as a foreseeable production failure mode — a future implementor cites XVI's MUST as authority for schema-level rejection, triggering a core execution block that is a direct XV violation — is a genuine blind spot, not a framing dispute. More critically, my original Rec 1's wholesale paragraph replacement, if adopted without the XV clause, makes this conflict worse rather than better: a more mechanically precise MUST with no enforcement-mode coordination is strictly worse than the current candidate. This changed my overall verdict from "Rec 1 as written is P1" to "Rec 1 and the XV clause are jointly P1 and must be adopted together."

My highest-priority surviving recommendation remains the governance definition of path (c) in the Governance section (Rec 5, combined with the PATCH version classification of Rec 3). This matters more than any individual wording fix because it determines the precedent for all subsequent grandfathered-principle remediation. Both reviews confirm the gap independently, and without explicit governance text constraining path (c) to PATCH and limiting the gate's scope to new normative requirements, the amendment establishes ad hoc case law that future committees for Principles VI and X will cite without any of the constraints this amendment's drafters implicitly observed. The wording fixes — L790 removal, "no surprise math" replacement, XV coordination, structural definition of "plain-language" — are high-value repairs to this specific amendment. The governance definition is the repair that makes the amendment's constitutional contribution durable.
