### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Criteria compliance content in the principle body: add structured Verification block vs. remove meta-content entirely**
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims**: Recommendation 4 (P2): "Add a `**Verification (v2.6.0):**` block inside Principle XVI's body (after the headline paragraph), listing the three mechanical checks… SIRs are preserved for audit trail but are comment blocks; the principle body is what future readers and agents load as constitutional text. Keeping verification evidence only in the SIR makes compliance audits dependent on reading comment blocks."
  - **wording-precision claims**: Recommendation 1 (P1): "Remove self-attestation sentence from principle body entirely. The full criteria self-assessment already exists at SIR L34-82… No principle in I-XXVIII contains a self-attestation of criteria compliance in its normative body text. The SIR is the established home for this content."
  - **Why this is dangerous**: The two recommendations embody opposite architectural principles about where criteria compliance evidence belongs. If CPC's Rec 4 is adopted, a structured Verification block is added to the body — more compliance metadata, not less. If WP's Rec 1 is adopted first and the sentence removed, a synthesis that also applies CPC's Rec 4 adds back compliance content WP explicitly excluded. Without resolution, one implementation partially undoes the other's underlying principle. The failure mode is a principle body that oscillates between "no compliance content" and "structured compliance block" across amendment cycles, with no canonical rule settling the question.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Resolve the architectural question first: does criteria compliance evidence have *any* sanctioned place in principle bodies? Both reviews agree the *current* state — an informal self-attestation sentence without structured enforcement documentation — is unacceptable. Both recommendations converge if the answer is "yes, but structured only": remove the unstructured self-attestation sentence (WP's Rec 1) AND add a structured Verification block (CPC's Rec 4). If the answer is "no compliance content in principle bodies," remove the sentence and leave compliance evidence in the SIR only. Neither review explicitly states which architectural rule governs; the synthesis must settle that question first, then the individual recommendations follow.

- **Gate compliance verdict: satisfied as submitted vs. internally inconsistent**
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims** (Alignment, "Constitutional Inclusion Criteria self-assessment substantive"): "The SIR includes explicit Criterion 1/2/3 self-assessments with concrete mechanical-check sketches for each structural requirement — spec 014 FR-012/SC-004 lint for parameter pinning, parity test for shape determinism, schema lint for plain-language pairing. Each check is concrete enough that an engineer reading the principle can sketch the check in one paragraph, satisfying the gate's stated standard (Governance, Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, Criterion 1)."
  - **wording-precision claims** (Off-Base Assumptions, third assumption + Recommendation 8): "The present-tense body assertion conflicts with the body's own honest caveat and the SIR's future-oriented framing… 'is mechanically verifiable' must be supportable by the principle body itself, not only by the SIR header comment. SIR comments are amendment-time metadata; a reader encountering the ratified constitution without the SIR cannot verify the claim from the body text for requirements (2) and (3)."
  - **Why this is dangerous**: CPC's alignment finding supports ratifying the amendment with the SIR satisfying the Governance gate. WP's Rec 8 requires either removing or temporally qualifying the L790 sentence before ratification because the body uses present-tense "is mechanically verifiable" while the same body acknowledges enforcement artifacts are "filed as a follow-up." If synthesis accepts CPC's alignment finding without WP's qualification, the amendment ratifies with an internal inconsistency: a normative present-tense claim the body itself qualifies as future-pending. This creates a self-referential compliance dispute at the next amendment cycle.
  - **Suggested resolution**: These are compatible once disambiguated. CPC's finding is about the SIR satisfying the Governance gate's *processual* requirements (is this amendment structurally sound?). WP's concern is about the principle body's *internal consistency* (does this sentence accurately describe the current state of enforcement artifacts?). Both can coexist if WP's Rec 1 is adopted: removing the self-attestation sentence from the body leaves CPC's finding intact (the SIR still satisfies the gate) and eliminates the body inconsistency WP identified. Neither review is overridden; the sentence's removal resolves both concerns simultaneously.

- **Scope of the "unchanged body" — who audited what**
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims** (Recommendation 6, P3): "In XVI's v2.3.2 clarification, replace 'cross-run variance in the assembled objective function's *shape*' with 'cross-run variance in the assembled objective function's *structure*' and update the inline parenthetical." This recommendation targets the v2.3.2 Clarification block, which the SIR explicitly states is UNCHANGED from v2.5.0.
  - **wording-precision claims** (audit scope framing): The audit charter covers "the headline + opening paragraph rewrite, not the pre-existing substrate." WP's Recommendation 3 targets the opening paragraph's "template shapes are stable" gloss (within the declared rewrite scope), not the v2.3.2 Clarification block's "assembled objective function's *shape*" language.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: CPC recommends a change to the v2.3.2 Clarification block — a section WP explicitly excluded from audit scope. If synthesis adopts CPC's Rec 6, it modifies a body section that WP did not review under wording-precision criteria. The two reviews together do not constitute a complete audit of that specific change. WP's Recommendation 3 and CPC's Recommendation 6 both replace "shape" language in Principle XVI, but in different locations and for different reasons: WP's change corrects a template-mutability misreading; CPC's change corrects a cross-principle terminological collision with IX. A synthesis that adopts both creates two separate "shape" → "structure" replacements in different sections, each reviewed by only one panel member.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Before adopting CPC's Rec 6, either commission a targeted wording-precision review of the v2.3.2 clarification text change, or move CPC's Rec 6 to a separate follow-up amendment scope. The v2.3.2 block was declared UNCHANGED in this amendment's SIR; modifying it under v2.6.0 ratification expands the amendment's scope beyond its stated boundary. Alternatively, bundle both "shape" → "structure" replacements into a single P3 follow-up pass that receives full dual-perspective review.

### Tensions

- **Enforcement chain visibility vs. body purity**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position** (Missed Opportunities, "Registry-emission enforcement chain is SIR-only" + Recommendation 5): "The enforcement chain exists only in the SIR comment block, not in the principle text. XV's Clarification v2.3.1 establishes the registry as the extension interface; XVI's plain-language requirement relies on that registry-emission validation but does not say so, leaving the enforcement chain invisible to readers of the principle body."
  - **wording-precision's position** (Recommendation 1): Criteria compliance metadata belongs in the SIR, not the principle body. The self-attestation sentence should be removed precisely because it imports SIR-level metadata into normative text.
  - **Nature of tension**: CPC wants more enforcement and compliance visibility in the principle body (surface the registry-emission enforcement chain). WP wants less compliance metadata in the principle body (remove the self-attestation sentence). Both diagnose the same root problem — compliance content is currently in the wrong place — but their directions of correction pull opposite ways. This is not a contradiction if the two reviews are targeting different types of content, but the distinction requires explicit articulation to prevent synthesis from treating them as canceling signals.
  - **Coordination needed**: Distinguish between *enforcement mechanism cross-references* (CPC wants these in the body — a parenthetical describing how XV's registry-emission enforces XVI's requirement) and *criteria compliance attestations* (WP wants these in the SIR — the claim that requirements satisfy the Constitutional Inclusion Criteria gate). A principle body can cross-reference XV's registry-emission mechanism without asserting its own criteria-gate compliance. CPC's Rec 5 (parenthetical cross-referencing XV) is compatible with WP's Rec 1 (remove self-attestation sentence) if the parenthetical is categorized as an enforcement mechanics reference, not a compliance metadata assertion. The synthesis must explicitly make this categorical distinction.

- **"No surprise math" as P1 vs. unexamined**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: Does not flag "no surprise math" (L788) as a wording problem anywhere in the review. The alignment section ("VII↔XVI bilateral carve-out preserved") confirms the v2.3.2 Clarification is verbatim preserved and the cross-run variance permission is intact, but the opening paragraph's gloss is not evaluated against that permission.
  - **wording-precision's position** (Missed Opportunities + Recommendations 2 and 3, both P1): "'No surprise math' does not accurately characterize a guarantee that is conditional on parameter pinning having already occurred. A user relying on 'no surprise math' as a behavioral guarantee will be surprised by this permitted cross-run variance." The v2.3.2 Clarification at L848-852 explicitly permits two separate invocations on identical input to produce different `objective.yml` files and therefore different mathematical objectives.
  - **Nature of tension**: WP treats this as P1 — a falsifiable inaccuracy in the principle body that generates compliance disputes when cross-run variance is observed. CPC does not surface it, which in synthesis could be treated as agreement or as a scope gap. If the synthesis weights both reviews equally and averages their prioritization, WP's P1 finding gets deprioritized, which would be an incorrect aggregation: CPC's silence on this point is a scope limitation, not a rebuttal.
  - **Coordination needed**: CPC should explicitly acknowledge whether the cross-run variance permitted by v2.3.2 makes "no surprise math" accurate or misleading as a plain-language gloss. If CPC agrees it overpromises (the gloss does not scope to post-pinning assembly), WP's P1 priority is reinforced from a cross-principle coherence perspective. If CPC disagrees (arguing the gloss is implicitly scoped to assembly determinism by context), that constitutes a genuine dispute synthesis must resolve before ratification. CPC's silence is not a safe neutral; synthesis should prompt an explicit position.

- **Headline-rewrite precedent: fix the governance layer vs. fix the body layer**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position** (Missed Opportunities + Recommendations 2, 7, both P2): "The Governance gate does not explicitly cover headline rewrites of grandfathered principles, creating a new precedent category the SIR does not explicitly acknowledge." Recommends updating the Governance gate text (Rec 2) and documenting the precedent explicitly in the SIR's governance log entry (Rec 7) — both governance-layer changes.
  - **wording-precision's position** (Off-Base Assumptions, first and second assumptions): Notes the same governance ambiguity — the SIR's "opening paragraph restructured" scope claim does not cover the net-new self-attestation sentence — but frames it as a wording precision concern about body internal consistency, not a governance process concern requiring gate text changes.
  - **Nature of tension**: CPC proposes fixing the governance layer (amend the gate text to name headline rewrites as a category, update the SIR). WP proposes fixing the body text (remove the self-attestation sentence, qualify the present-tense claim). Both responses are valid but address different layers. The tension is in sequencing and scope: the governance fix (CPC) should arguably precede the body fix (WP), because the gate text is what determines whether a body's compliance claim is even appropriate. But WP's body fix is P1 and CPC's governance fix is P2. Accepting WP's P1 priority and acting on the body first is not wrong, but it defers the governance layer issue to a future amendment cycle where the precedent has already been set de facto.
  - **Coordination needed**: Sequence the fixes by dependency, not by priority. The body's internal inconsistency (WP's P1) does not depend on gate text resolution and can be addressed immediately. The governance precedent (CPC's P2) should be bundled into the next governance amendment, with an explicit note in the v2.6.0 SIR acknowledging the unresolved precedent gap in the meantime. This allows WP's time-sensitive P1 fix to proceed without blocking on governance process work.

- **"Shape" terminology: opening paragraph vs. v2.3.2 Clarification block**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position** (Missed Opportunities + Recommendation 6, P3): "'Shape' is used in two incompatible senses across Principles IX and XVI." Targets the v2.3.2 Clarification block: replace "assembled objective function's *shape* (parameter names, template selection, gap-identifier set)" with "assembled objective function's *structure*."
  - **wording-precision's position** (Missed Opportunities + Recommendations 2 and 3, both P1): "'Template shapes are stable' reads as a claim about template mutability, not assembly determinism." Targets the opening paragraph's "template shapes are stable (no surprise math)" gloss with a proposed replacement: "assembly structure is deterministic given the same template and pinned parameters."
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews want to reduce or correct "shape" usage in XVI, but they target different locations for different reasons. CPC targets the v2.3.2 block for cross-principle terminological collision with IX; WP targets the opening paragraph for template-mutability misreading. A synthesis that adopts one without the other produces a document where "shape" terminology is inconsistent *within* XVI: corrected in one location, uncorrected in another. Additionally, the two reviews propose different replacements ("structure" in both cases, but the surrounding contexts differ), and the interaction between a fixed opening paragraph and an unfixed v2.3.2 block — or vice versa — has not been audited.
  - **Coordination needed**: Bundle both "shape" corrections into a single pass: (a) opening paragraph "template shapes are stable" → WP's proposed replacement for the opening paragraph; (b) v2.3.2 "assembled objective function's *shape*" → CPC's proposed "structure." These are complementary, not conflicting, but need to be coordinated so the resulting document is terminologically consistent across all XVI sections. Given CPC's Rec 6 targets the UNCHANGED body (see Dangerous Contradiction 3), bundle the coordination with the scope resolution for that contradiction.
