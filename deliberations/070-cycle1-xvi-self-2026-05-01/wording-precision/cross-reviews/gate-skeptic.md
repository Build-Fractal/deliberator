### Dangerous Contradictions

- **"no surprise math": Delete vs. Replace**
  - **gate-skeptic claims**: "(no surprise math) should be cut entirely" — "Its deletion makes the design-intent paragraph strictly cleaner without losing any normative content." gate-skeptic explicitly proposes no replacement text, arguing the structural requirement for shape determinism already conveys the claim without any gloss. (gate-skeptic Recommendation 6, Priority P3)
  - **wording-precision claims**: "Replace with conditional gloss" — "assembly is reproducible given the same inputs (no structural drift after pinning)" or "assembly is stable given pinned parameters." The fix must be a replacement, not a deletion, because the absence of any gloss leaves the paragraph's design-intent framing incomplete. (wording-precision Recommendation 2, Priority P1)
  - **Why this is dangerous**: The two recommendations are mutually exclusive. Applied together, they cannot both be true of the same text — either something appears after "template shapes are stable" or nothing does. Worse, the underlying diagnoses differ: gate-skeptic sees a Criterion 1 failure (reader-relative predicate — "surprise" has no mechanical referent), while wording-precision sees an accuracy failure (the gloss overpromises by implying no mathematical variation across invocations when the v2.3.2 Clarification at L848-852 explicitly permits cross-run variance in resolved parameter values). A synthesis that adopts gate-skeptic's "delete" without acknowledging wording-precision's accuracy concern leaves the structural requirement's scope still ambiguous — no gloss and no correction. A synthesis that adopts wording-precision's "replace" without gate-skeptic's Criterion 1 analysis might produce a conditional gloss that still contains a reader-relative predicate ("no structural drift" is arguably as reader-relative as "no surprise math").
  - **Suggested resolution**: The two diagnoses are compatible if treated as additive constraints. The replacement gloss must satisfy both: it must be conditional on pinning (wording-precision's accuracy requirement) AND avoid reader-relative language (gate-skeptic's Criterion 1 requirement). A compliant candidate: "assembly is deterministic given the same template and pinned parameters" — conditional scope (post-pinning only), no reader-relative predicate, mirrors the body's "deterministic assembly" vocabulary from stage 3. Gate-skeptic's diagnosis motivates the constraint on the replacement text; wording-precision's diagnosis motivates having a replacement at all. Both should yield on their stated preference (delete / specific proposed wording) in favor of the compliant candidate.

- **Priority divergence on "no surprise math": P3 vs. P1**
  - **gate-skeptic claims**: Cutting "no surprise math" is explicitly Priority P3 — "a low-severity ongoing liability." Gate-skeptic's P1 recommendation focuses on the full paragraph replacement, within which cutting "no surprise math" is a component. The prioritization signal is that the paragraph-level fix matters more than the individual gloss's deletion. (gate-skeptic Recommendations 1 and 6)
  - **wording-precision claims**: "No surprise math" is Priority P1 — equivalent in urgency to removing the self-attestation sentence.   - **Why this is dangerous**: Priority divergence of this magnitude — P1 vs. P3 for the same item — would produce incompatible sequencing decisions in a ratification workflow that processes recommendations in priority order. If gate-skeptic's ordering is adopted, "no surprise math" might remain through a PATCH cycle where only P1 and P2 items are addressed. If wording-precision's ordering is adopted and synthesis editors begin from the P1 list, they will encounter gate-skeptic's Recommendation 1 (also P1) proposing a whole-paragraph rewrite — and the priority of any individual gloss within that rewrite becomes moot. The real integration failure is that an editor following gate-skeptic's P1 recommendation (full paragraph rewrite) will not process "no surprise math" as a separate P1 item, potentially producing a paragraph that retains the accuracy problem while eliminating the Criterion 1 problem.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Collapse the priority question by framing "no surprise math" as a sub-item of the paragraph-level P1 fix rather than an independent item at any priority level. Both reviews agree the paragraph needs fixing; they disagree on what drives the fix. The combined position — paragraph rewrite is P1, and any replacement text for "no surprise math" must satisfy both accuracy and Criterion 1 constraints — resolves the sequencing conflict.

  - **gate-skeptic's position**: "Auditable" and "no surprise math" fail because they are "reader-relative predicates that Criterion 1 rejected in the original headline."   - **Nature of tension**: Gate-skeptic's Criterion 1 lens would flag any phrase whose referent cannot be mechanically assessed. A phrase could satisfy wording-precision (accurately characterizes the body's guarantee) while still failing gate-skeptic (uses reader-relative vocabulary).
