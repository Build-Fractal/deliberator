### Systemic Contradictions

- **Compliance metadata has no constitutionally-sanctioned home in principle bodies**
  - **Manifests in**: Contradiction 1 above (remove sentence vs. add structured block); WP Rec 4 conditionality logic (enforcement sub-bullets as support for a body claim vs. standalone documentation); GS's design-intent paragraph wholesale-replacement approach eliminating all metadata-adjacent prose atomically; CPC's Rec 4 structured Verification block proposal.
  - **Root cause**: The Governance section's Extension blocks paragraph names a `Verification:` block as a required format for certain Extension blocks, but this format is not defined elsewhere in the constitution and no prior principle body uses it. The gap is that the constitution has no rule governing whether, in what format, and at what location, criteria compliance evidence may appear in principle bodies at all. The ambiguity produced divergent architectural preferences (WP: SIR only; CPC: body required) that were resolvable only by a synthesis inference from the Extension blocks paragraph.
  - **Implication for spec**: Governance should explicitly define two categories of body-level content: (a) normative requirements (bound by Constitutional Inclusion Criteria), and (b) structured metadata blocks (Verification, Origin) whose format is specified in the Governance section and exempt from normative-content analysis. The absence of this distinction forces each amendment to re-litigate where compliance evidence belongs.

- **Constitutional authority partitioning between XVI and XV over plugin output**
  - **Manifests in**: Unresolved design of the XV enforcement-mode carve-out; CPC Off-Base Assumption (jurisdiction question); GS Phase 3 acknowledgment that adopting a stronger MUST without the XV clause is "strictly worse than the current candidate"; WP New Rec and GS New Rec B both converging on the same gap from different analytical angles.
  - **Root cause**: Principle XVI developed its plain-language output pairing requirement in the v2.3.2 body clarification block, evolving independently of Principle XV's plugin isolation framework. The v2.3.2 MUST was written to constrain plugin output content without simultaneously establishing which enforcement mechanism governs violations. XV's warn-and-continue model applies but is not referenced from XVI's body, creating a silent cross-principle conflict that neither XVI nor XV's text resolves.
  - **Implication for spec**: Any principle that imposes behavioral obligations on plugins — plugin output format, plugin timing, plugin side effects — should be required by the amendment process to explicitly cross-reference XV's enforcement machinery and declare whether violations are (a) warn-and-continue, (b) blocked, or (c) silently tolerated. This cross-reference pattern should be formalized as a drafting convention in the Governance section or in an operational guidance document.

- **Declared "UNCHANGED" sections create amendment scope traps**
  - **Manifests in**: CPC Dispute 2 (v2.3.2 "shape" terminology fix in declared-UNCHANGED block); WP Modified Rec 3 (coordinate fix requires v2.3.2 scope); WP/cross-reviews/CPC Dangerous Contradiction 3 (scope of "unchanged body" — who audited what); CPC Modified Rec 6 Option A/B scope decision.
  - **Root cause**: The SIR's "UNCHANGED" scope declaration creates a useful audit boundary but produces a trap: when a change in the declared-rewrite zone (the opening paragraph) requires coordination with terminology in the declared-UNCHANGED zone (the v2.3.2 block), either the scope must be explicitly expanded (requiring updated SIR acknowledgment and extended audit) or the fix must be deferred (leaving the document in a transitional inconsistent state). The current process has no defined procedure for declaring a bounded scope expansion mid-amendment.
  - **Implication for spec**: The amendment process should either (a) define a procedure for "scope expansion discovery" — when a declared-UNCHANGED section requires modification to maintain internal consistency with a declared-rewrite section, the SIR should include a formal scope-expansion notice with explicit dual-perspective review of the expanded section — or (b) explicitly require pre-ratification consistency checks between declared-rewrite and declared-UNCHANGED sections, so that required v2.3.2 terminology fixes are caught and scoped before the SIR is finalized.

- **The v2.4.0 gate's category taxonomy does not cover within-principle reorganization**
  - **Manifests in**: GS Rec 5 / CPC Rec 2 (path (c) governance gap); GS Rec 3 / CPC New Rec A (version classification as PATCH for reorganization); GS Rec 7 (Criterion 3 inapplicability); GS Dispute 2 (blocking priority for governance definition); the gate's Extension blocks paragraph vs. the SIR's framing of the amendment as a "restructuring."
  - **Root cause**: The v2.4.0 gate was designed for two use cases: new principles (full gate applies, all three criteria) and Extension/Clarification blocks on existing principles (Criterion 1 required, Criterion 3 not applicable). Headline rewrites of grandfathered principles — promoting existing body content to headline status while demoting a non-verifiable claim to design-intent prose — fit neither category. The absence of a defined path (c) means every future grandfathered-principle remediation using this approach must re-derive its legitimacy from first principles or cite this amendment as ad hoc precedent, neither of which is constitutionally robust.
  - **Implication for spec**: The Governance section requires an explicit path (c) definition (already converged on by GS and CPC) that constrains future use: PATCH classification, no new normative requirements, full gate applies to any new requirements introduced alongside the reorganization. Without this definition, the mechanism established by v2.6.0 becomes a general-purpose bypass for Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, rather than a bounded remediation tool for grandfathered principles with mechanically verifiable existing body content.

- **Design-intent prose in principle bodies has no defined normative status**
  - **Manifests in**: GS Rec 1 (purpose-clause framing as structural problem); WP Rec 7 (evaluate whether design-intent paragraph belongs in Origin note); GS/cross-reviews/WP Tension (governance-first vs wording-first triage); WP Final Position Statement flexibility point (design-intent paragraph in Origin note conditional on WP-7 P3 evaluation).
  - **Root cause**: The constitution has no rule distinguishing normative requirements from explanatory commentary within a principle body. Origin notes are the established location for design rationale ("game engine vision — specs 012-019"), but the design-intent paragraph at L785-789 is not framed as an Origin note — it is in the body proper. This creates interpretive risk: the clause "is achieved through these structural requirements" could be read by future implementors as a purpose test (an implementation that achieves user understanding through different means satisfies the principle even if it violates the structural requirements), effectively resurrecting the epistemically unverifiable claim the amendment sought to retire. No existing principle body contains this pattern; its constitutional status is undefined.
  - **Implication for spec**: Either (a) the design-intent paragraph should be relocated to the Origin note, where purpose-clause prose is constitutionally established and does not carry normative weight, or (b) the Governance section should define what counts as explanatory commentary vs. normative text in a principle body, with the design-intent paragraph's status explicitly declared under whichever definition is adopted.
