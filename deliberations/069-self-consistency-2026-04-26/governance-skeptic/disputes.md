### Remaining Disputes

#### Dispute: Structural shape of criterion 1 (AND-coupling vs. partial-OR substitution)

- **My claim**: Criterion 1 (mechanical verification capability) MUST remain AND-coupled with criteria 2 and 3. The sketch-or-rubric loosening I accepted in my Recommendation 4 withdrawal addresses *what counts as a check*, not *whether one is required*. Removing the requirement (strictness-skeptic R-1's "criterion 2 AND criterion 3 AND (criterion 1 OR demonstrated peer-review-rubric operationalization)") opens a category-wide bypass for any principle whose author claims their proposal is form-constraint or meta-principle in nature. The closed-list narrowing strictness-skeptic added in their revision (VI, X, XVI, XVII, XVIII as the only relaxed-criterion-1 principles) is a real improvement but is grandfathered evidence about the *past*, not a forward filter — it does not constrain which future principles can claim membership in those categories without a separate amendment, and the gate's purpose is to constrain the future.
- **Opposing position(s)**: Strictness-skeptic (R-1 KEEP with sequencing clarification, R-3/R-7 collapsed to closed list): the gate as written excludes form-constraint and taste-level principles by construction; admitting Principles VI, X, XVI to the constitution after rejecting them prospectively is the structurally informative fact that requires loosening criterion 1's coupling. Practitioner (New Recommendation #1, against strictness-skeptic): rejects R-1 as "theory-first loosening that imports new vagueness," prefers evidence-triggered calibration via the precedent log; this position is closer to mine but framed as deferral, not principled rejection.
- **Why I will not concede**: Strictness-skeptic's closed-list move (revised R-3) makes their proposal weaker, not stronger, against my objection. A closed list of five existing principles is an admission that *we cannot articulate the category in a forward-applicable way* — which is precisely criterion 2's complaint about scope vagueness. The closed list operationalizes "form constraint" as "one of {VI, X, XVI, XVII, XVIII}," which is a tautology, not a category. Practitioner's New Recommendation #1 is the correct response to the same evidence: the gate is provisional; if it rejects three legitimate proposals, the precedent log triggers re-examination. That mechanism (which I adopted as NR-1 in Phase 3) preserves criterion 1's substantive bar without committing to a structural change before evidence exists. Conceding to R-1 now means accepting strictness-skeptic's premise that VI, X, XVI being "rejected by the new gate" is a *defect of the gate* rather than a *fact about those principles' position on the rigor-vs-taste spectrum*. The grandfathering clause exists precisely to acknowledge that older principles were authored under looser standards and that the constitution can choose to tighten its forward filter without retroactively delegitimizing them.
- **Counter-argument**: Strictness-skeptic's strongest reply is that excluding the entire "taste-level invariant" category from future constitutional admissions makes the constitution decreasingly able to express what distinguishes it from a linter ruleset. This is a real concern but it is a concern about constitutional *content*, not constitutional *gate calibration*. If the maintainers determine that conversus needs more taste-level invariants, the answer is to amend the gate (recalibrating criterion 1) — explicitly, with worked-example justification — not to design the gate to admit a category it cannot define forward.
- **Proposed resolution path**: Synthesis adopts my AND-coupling (gate text unchanged from candidate v2.4.0 on this point) AND practitioner's New Recommendation #1 (precedent log triggers calibration review when ≥3 amendments rejected on criterion 1 alone) AND my NR-1 (calibration review at next MINOR if ≥3 rejections within a MAJOR cycle). The two retrospective-recalibration triggers are compatible — practitioner's is keyed to log content, mine is keyed to rejection count; both feed the same recalibration moment. Strictness-skeptic's closed-list move is recorded in the deliberation as a *flagged area for the calibration review's first iteration* but is not adopted in the v2.4.0 gate text. This sequences the strictness-skeptic concern behind evidence rather than pre-resolving it via structural concession.

#### Dispute: Acceptance bar for the verification deliberation (substantive zero-disputes vs. documented-disputes)

- **My claim**: The verification deliberation that gates MINOR and MAJOR amendments MUST require zero unresolved disputes from the deliberating panel before merge. Documented-but-unincorporated disputes (practitioner's amended Recommendation #5) are insufficient — they create a paper trail that satisfies audit but does not exercise the deliberation's bug-catching function. The v2.3.1 fix that was the original justification for this recommendation succeeded because the verification deliberation's disputes blocked merge of a defective text; if the same deliberation had been allowed to proceed with disputes documented in the Sync Impact Report, the bug would have shipped.
- **Opposing position(s)**: Practitioner (amended Recommendation #5): "every dispute has either been incorporated into the amendment or documented in the Sync Impact Report with a rationale for non-incorporation"; cites my own DC-2 from Phase 2 (zero-disputes is engineerable via panel composition) and strictness-skeptic's DC-2 (variance-reducer). Strictness-skeptic (Phase 2 DC-2 carried into revision): zero-disputes locks in deliberating agents' disposition.
- **Why I will not concede**: Practitioner is citing my own concern back at me, but my concern about panel-composition gameability does not survive practitioner's New Recommendation #3 (panel composition disclosure with required differentiation between proposal and verification panels). With NR-3 in place, the gameability concern is mitigated by transparency, and the original objection to a substantive-zero-disputes bar dissolves. Practitioner's documented-disputes alternative trades a substantive bug-catching contract for a procedural audit-trail contract; those are not equivalent goods. The v2.3.1 evidence is asymmetric — we have one example of zero-disputes catching a real bug, and zero examples of documented-disputes-with-rationale catching a real bug. The burden of proof is on the looser proposal.
- **Counter-argument**: Practitioner's strongest reply is the framing that single-veto failure modes are anti-democratic and inconsistent with Principle V (warnings, not blocks). I read Principle V as governing *output validation* (malformed conversus output), not *constitutional amendment process* — the two are different scopes and Principle V does not transfer. Practitioner's secondary reply (that documented disputes preserve the bug-catching benefit because reviewers will read the Sync Impact Report) is empirically unsupported; constitutional amendments accumulate documentation faster than they accumulate readers, and "documented in the SIR" reliably converges to "ignored after 90 days."
- **Proposed resolution path**: Synthesis preserves substantive zero-disputes as the verification deliberation's acceptance bar AND adopts practitioner's NR-3 (panel composition disclosure) AND adds a documented-but-binding-rationale escape hatch *only* for disputes that the verification panel itself unanimously categorizes as "out of scope for this amendment" (i.e., disputes pointing at a pre-existing constitutional defect, not a defect introduced by the proposed amendment). This preserves bug-catching for amendment-introduced defects without trapping amendments behind orthogonal critiques. The escape hatch is narrow enough to defeat the panel-stacking concern (the panel cannot route a real defect through "out of scope" without unanimity), wide enough to defeat the orthogonal-critique-traps-amendment concern.

#### Dispute: Source-of-truth and maintenance model for the precedent log (derived index vs. authoritative log)

- **My claim**: The precedent log proposed by practitioner (Recommendation #8, amended) MUST be derivable from the per-amendment Sync Impact Report self-assessments at build time. Maintaining a separate authoritative log in `deliberations/governance-decisions.md` violates Principle XI (single source of truth) — the Sync Impact Reports already carry the data. Practitioner's amendment to make the log "derived" is the right move, but the *operational* question of who runs the build and where the derived artifact lives is unresolved and matters for whether the log functions as a recalibration trigger or rots.
- **Opposing position(s)**: Practitioner (amended Recommendation #8): the log is rebuilt on demand by the speckit pipeline; the constitution does not maintain it. Strictness-skeptic (SA-2 endorsement): the log pairs with retrospective review at each MINOR — implies the log is queried at MINOR amendments, not on demand.
- **Why I will not concede**: This is a narrower dispute than the other two and may resolve in synthesis without further argument, but it is real because the recalibration triggers in my NR-1, practitioner's NR-1, and strictness-skeptic's R-10 all depend on the log being accurate and queryable *at amendment time*. "Rebuilt on demand" without a designated build moment means the log might not exist when the calibration trigger fires. The strictness-skeptic / practitioner SA-2 pairing (rebuild at each MINOR) is the right operational answer but is not in any of the three Phase 3 revisions as a concrete commitment.
- **Counter-argument**: Practitioner could reasonably argue that "rebuild on demand" is sufficient because a calibration review is itself a deliberate moment that can include the rebuild step. This is true but underspecifies whose responsibility it is — without a designated owner, the rebuild does not happen.
- **Proposed resolution path**: Synthesis adopts practitioner's amended Recommendation #8 (derived from SIRs) AND specifies that the rebuild MUST run as part of every MINOR or MAJOR amendment's PR-time CI (the same CI that runs the form-check lint from practitioner's amended Recommendation #2). The CI step writes the rebuilt log to `deliberations/governance-decisions.md` as a build artifact, makes it visible in the PR diff, and triggers the recalibration-review obligation if the log shows ≥3 rejections on the same criterion (NR-1 / practitioner NR-1) or ≥3 override invocations on the same criterion (strictness-skeptic R-10 sequenced behind override). The log is then *automatically maintained* and *automatically triggers* recalibration without any human action — which is the operational answer the dispute requires.

### Final Position Statement

#### Non-Negotiables

1. **AND-coupling of the three criteria**. The gate text in candidate v2.4.0 is correct as-is on this point. Criterion 1 (mechanical verification capability) remains required, not substitutable. Loosening to a partial-OR before evidence accumulates substitutes theory-first restructuring for evidence-triggered recalibration. The grandfathering clause already absorbs the corpus-asymmetry concern; the precedent log + retrospective trigger (NR-1 + practitioner NR-1) absorbs the future-recalibration concern. Both mechanisms must land; the structural change must not.

2. **Substantive zero-disputes acceptance bar for the verification deliberation gating MINOR/MAJOR amendments**, with a narrow unanimous-out-of-scope escape hatch and panel-composition disclosure (practitioner NR-3) as the gameability mitigation. Documented-but-unincorporated disputes are insufficient — they convert a substantive bug-catching contract into a procedural audit-trail contract, and the v2.3.1 evidence supports the substantive form, not the procedural one.

3. **Form-checking CI lint is mandatory for the Sync Impact Report self-assessment**, with the precedent log derived as a CI build artifact at every MINOR/MAJOR amendment (the synthesis of governance-skeptic Recommendation 2 modified, practitioner Recommendation 2 amended, and practitioner Recommendation 8 amended). Without a build moment, the recalibration triggers are dependent on a log that may not exist at trigger time, and the entire calibration-by-evidence machinery rots.

#### Flexibility

1. **Extension semantics (Recommendation 3 modified)**. My carve-outs (subset-of-scope test for criterion 3, Clarification-class headers exempt from full self-assessment) are conservative; if synthesis prefers strictness-skeptic's R-3/R-7 closed-list framing OR practitioner's stricter "extensions face the full gate" framing, I do not block — provided the chosen formulation does not silently freeze v2.4.0 by making criterion 3 unsatisfiable for legitimate refinements. Either of the alternatives, with appropriate carve-outs for the XV ↔ XXVII coordination pattern, is acceptable.

2. **Cooling-off period and panel-composition disclosure (practitioner NR-2 and NR-3)**. I did not propose either in my own revision; both are good additions and I will accept either or both into v2.4.0. NR-3 is more important than NR-2 because it directly addresses the gameability critique that motivated my dispute on the verification-deliberation acceptance bar.

3. **Precise wording of criterion 2's interpretation rule (practitioner Recommendation 7 adopted with extension)**. The "scope determination MUST NOT require interpretation; application within scope MAY" reframing is a clean improvement over the candidate's wording. The added sentence to criterion 3 about explicit cross-reference coordination (XV/XXVII pattern) is also adoptable. I have no preferred phrasing beyond clarity; synthesis chooses.

The revision process has resolved more than it has left open. Three disputes survive — one structural (criterion 1 coupling), one procedural (verification-deliberation acceptance bar), one operational (precedent-log build moment) — and each has a concrete proposed resolution path that does not require any role to fully concede. Five convergences are unanimous and ready for synthesis. v2.4.0 is shippable with the convergent fixes plus the proposed resolution paths on the three disputes; the structural debate (strictness-skeptic R-1) is correctly deferred to evidence rather than pre-resolved by concession.
