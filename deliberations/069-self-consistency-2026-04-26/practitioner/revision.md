# Practitioner Revision — Constitution v2.4.0 Self-Consistency

**Persona**: Pragmatist (future amendment author)
**Phase 3 revision (iteration 1)**
**Position carried forward**: the v2.4.0 gate is directionally correct and operationally fragile; my original review focused on enforcement scaffolding (template, CI lint, routing table, precedent log, sync impact report, verification deliberation, PATCH carve-out, criterion 2 wording, expedited path, XVII cross-link). Cross-reviews from governance-skeptic and strictness-skeptic have moved several of my positions and hardened others. This revision adjudicates each original recommendation against their critiques, withdraws or amends what cannot survive, and adds a small number of new recommendations that emerged from the cross-review collisions themselves.

---

### Recommendation Dispositions

**Recommendation #1 — Self-assessment template with worked examples in the spec template.**

*Status*: **Amended.** Strictness-skeptic's T-1 (worked-example skew) and governance-skeptic's Tension #1 (template location) both land. The original recommendation anchored on Principle XXVI's meta-test pattern as the criterion 1 example; that is the easiest possible pass and would set the implicit bar far above what most legitimate amendments can clear. The amended version: (a) host the template inside the **Sync Impact Report** (per governance-skeptic's location argument, which is correct — Sync Impact Reports already live in git history attached to the constitution, and creating a parallel "amendment spec template" violates Principle XI), (b) include three worked examples per criterion — one mechanical-pass (XXVI), one peer-review-rubric-pass (a hypothetical form constraint along the lines of strictness-skeptic's HF-3), and one fail example, and (c) include explicit "N/A — justified because" branches for the optional criteria if any partial-OR loosening lands downstream. This preserves the goal (calibration) without enshrining over-strictness.

**Recommendation #2 — CI lint requiring the self-assessment template to be filled in.**

*Status*: **Amended and conditioned.** Strictness-skeptic's DC-1 is the sharpest critique in either cross-review and I largely accept it: hardening a mis-calibrated gate before the calibration debate resolves creates a one-way ratchet that will take a MAJOR-tier fight to unwind. But strictness-skeptic's "defer until calibration lands" is too strong — a template with no enforcement is functionally identical to no template, and governance-skeptic's Safe Agreement #1 (both reviews independently identified this as P1) confirms enforcement is needed. The amended version: ship the lint as a **form check only** (grep for the four required Sync Impact Report sub-headings — Mechanical Verification, Falsifiable Scope, Distinctness, and an explicit Routing Decision if any criterion is N/A). The lint MUST accept "N/A — justified because {rationale}" as a passing value for any criterion. The lint does NOT evaluate the substance of the answers; that remains reviewer work. This satisfies governance-skeptic's enforcement bar, defangs strictness-skeptic's ratchet objection (lint enforces form, not the contested semantics), and resolves my own DC-1 with strictness-skeptic about the lint enforcing a now-loosened structural rule.

**Recommendation #3 — Routing table for operational-guidance destinations.**

*Status*: **Amended.** Strictness-skeptic's T-2 is correct that the routing destinations themselves are underspecified for form constraints and meta-principles — those don't fit `AGENTS.md`, `SKILL.md`, or domain-specific reference documents cleanly, and routing them to operational guidance is a demotion. Governance-skeptic's Tension #4 + Safe Agreement #3 are also correct that the routing table is needed but `SKILL.md` deserves disambiguation as runtime-enforced. The amended version: build the routing table **with an explicit "constitutional remand" branch** — if a proposed principle fails the gate but does not fit any operational-guidance destination cleanly (form constraint, meta-principle, taste invariant), it is remanded back for re-drafting rather than routed to a poor-fit destination. This converts the four-way choice into a five-way decision tree with a safety valve, and prevents the operational-guidance sink from becoming a wastebasket.

**Recommendation #4 — PATCH carve-out from the three-criterion self-assessment.**

*Status*: **Withdrawn.** Both cross-reviewers landed on this and both were right. Governance-skeptic's Dangerous Contradiction #1 (PATCH carve-out creates an exploitable bypass — authors will reclassify borderline expansions as PATCH to dodge the gate) and strictness-skeptic's DC-3 (carve-out is structurally backward — relaxes the gate where it does no work and leaves it active where it actually rejects valuable content) are independent objections that converge on the same conclusion. I withdraw the recommendation. Replacement: rather than version-bump-based carve-out, the Sync Impact Report self-assessment MUST accept "N/A — typo fix / cross-reference update / wording refinement" as a valid one-line answer for amendments that don't add or redefine principles. The form is filled in; the content is appropriately lightweight. This is what governance-skeptic explicitly endorsed as the "cost of firing scales with materiality" pattern.

**Recommendation #5 — Codify the verification deliberation as MUST with zero-disputes acceptance bar.**

*Status*: **Amended.** Both cross-reviewers correctly identified the over-promotion. Governance-skeptic's Dangerous Contradiction #2 (zero-disputes is engineerable via panel composition) and strictness-skeptic's DC-2 (variance-reducer that locks in deliberating agents' disposition) are different framings of the same structural problem. Strictness-skeptic's resolution is the better one: "all disputes documented and addressed in the Sync Impact Report" rather than "zero disputes blocks merge." The amended version: MINOR and MAJOR amendments MUST run a verification deliberation, but the acceptance bar is "every dispute has either been incorporated into the amendment or documented in the Sync Impact Report with a rationale for non-incorporation." This preserves the bug-catching benefit (the v2.3.1 fix the original recommendation cited) without creating a single-veto failure mode and without inverting Principle V's "warnings, not blocks" stance.

**Recommendation #6 — Add a v2.4.0 sync impact report to the top of the document.**

*Status*: **Strengthened.** This was the cleanest agreement across all cross-reviews (governance-skeptic Safe Agreement #2, strictness-skeptic SA-4) and survives intact. Strictness-skeptic's T-3 added an important escalation: the sync impact report MUST explicitly enumerate the grandfathered principles that fail the new gate (per spec 069 §5 — Principles VI, X, XVI). The original recommendation framed this as a documentation fix; the strengthened version makes calibration evidence permanent and visible to future amendment authors, not buried in a deliberation-folder reference. Adopt as strengthened.

**Recommendation #7 — Rewrite criterion 2's "without requiring interpretation" wording.**

*Status*: **Adopted with extension.** Strictness-skeptic's T-4 confirmed the wording fix (scope determination MUST NOT require interpretation, application within scope MAY) but flagged that a sharper criterion 2 makes the AND-coupling with weaker criteria 1 and 3 more wasteful. Governance-skeptic's Tension #2 added that the rewrite still arguably rejects XV/XXVII coordination cases. The extension: add a sentence to criterion 3 alongside the criterion 2 fix — "Coordination with an existing principle via explicit cross-reference (per the XV/XXVII pattern) is a permitted form of distinctness; near-duplication with no coordination is rejected." This closes both gaps in one edit. I do NOT, however, accept strictness-skeptic's structural R-1 (AND→partial-OR) — see New Recommendation #1 below for my counter-position on the structural debate.

**Recommendation #8 — Precedent log at `deliberations/governance-decisions.md`.**

*Status*: **Amended.** Governance-skeptic's Tension #4 raised a genuine Principle XI concern: maintaining two sources of truth (per-amendment Sync Impact Report + cross-amendment precedent log) for the same decisions risks drift. Strictness-skeptic's SA-2 endorsed the recommendation and proposed pairing it with retrospective review at each MINOR. The synthesis governance-skeptic specified — "Sync Impact Report is authoritative; precedent log is a derived index" — is the right relationship, and it is implementable as a build-time rebuild rather than hand-maintained. The amended version: the precedent log is **derived** from each amendment's Sync Impact Report self-assessment (a script that aggregates by criterion across all sync impact reports in git history). The constitution does not maintain it; the speckit pipeline rebuilds it on demand. This satisfies Principle XI by collapsing the two sources, satisfies my original audit-trail goal, and incorporates strictness-skeptic's SA-2 retrospective-review pairing as a natural consequence of having a queryable index.

**Recommendation #9 — Specify the expedited amendment path for security/regulatory.**

*Status*: **Amended with rollback authority.** Governance-skeptic's Dangerous Contradiction #3 caught a real gap: as drafted, "license-compliance issues" and "regulatory changes" are author-defined open categories, and the expedited path becomes a one-way door because findings only "land as a follow-up amendment" (which is what would happen anyway). The fix is exactly what governance-skeptic specified: post-hoc verification deliberation within 14 days **WITH explicit rollback authority** — if the post-hoc deliberation finds material issues, the expedited amendment is reverted (not patched in a follow-up). The amended version adds: "Verification findings of material issues trigger automatic rollback to the pre-expedited text, after which a normal-process amendment may re-propose the change." This closes the bypass while preserving the incident-response use case.

**Recommendation #10 — Cross-link the gate from Principle XVII (Content Classification).**

*Status*: **Amended (direction reversed).** Governance-skeptic's Dangerous Contradiction #4 is technically correct that the constitution's reference topology flows newer → older; a forward reference from XVII (grandfathered) to a Governance subsection (new) inverts that topology. I accept the correction. The amended version: cross-reference flows **from the gate to XVII** ("Constitutional Inclusion Criteria inherits XVII's Content Classification vocabulary; routing decisions to operational guidance follow XVII's execution-logic vs. contribution-guidelines distinction"). Strictness-skeptic's T-5 raised a separate and interesting consequence — XVII's clean two-way split makes the gate's fuzzier four-way fan-out visibly miscalibrated by comparison. I think this is a feature, not a bug: the gate's calibration *should* be visibly testable against XVII's, and any future amendment author who notices the asymmetry has a legitimate basis for proposing a tightening of the routing table. Adopt the corrected direction, accept the downstream pressure.

### New Recommendations

**New Recommendation #1 — Reject strictness-skeptic's AND→partial-OR restructure (R-1) but adopt the calibration evidence requirement.**

Strictness-skeptic's structural fix (criterion 2 required, criteria 1 and 3 substitutable via OR) is the most contested proposal across the three reviews. My cross-review of strictness-skeptic flagged DC-1 (R-1 weakens enforcement) and DC-3 (R-5 calibration vs. grandfathering tension); both stand on revision. The deeper objection is that R-1 imports two new categorical judgments (form-vs-behavioral, meta-principle-vs-not) that the gate itself fails criterion 2 on — strictness-skeptic's fix recreates the vagueness it claims to cure. The right response to legitimate form constraints is not to weaken criterion 1 globally but to (a) ship the gate as currently drafted with my amended enforcement scaffolding (template, lint, sync impact report, derived precedent log), (b) require each amendment that fails criterion 1 to document the failure mode in the Sync Impact Report, and (c) trigger a calibration review of criterion 1 specifically when the precedent log shows ≥3 amendments rejected by criterion 1 alone (no other criterion failing). This is evidence-first loosening rather than theory-first restructuring. It preserves the gate's coherence in the near term and creates a mechanical trigger for re-examination when real evidence accumulates.

**New Recommendation #2 — Codify a 14-day amendment-PR cooling-off period for new principles (MINOR amendments).**

Both cross-reviews surfaced concerns about deliberation-skew effects (governance-skeptic on tamper-able verification deliberations, strictness-skeptic on self-consistency methodology locking in disposition). Neither proposed a structural mitigation. The cleanest one I can offer: MINOR amendments adding new principles MUST sit at the PR stage for 14 calendar days minimum after the verification deliberation completes, with a clearly-marked "calibration window" during which any contributor MAY raise objections that trigger a re-deliberation with a *fresh* agent panel. PATCH amendments and MAJOR principle removals are exempt (different risk profile — PATCH is reversible, MAJOR has higher inherent friction). This adds wall-clock-time as a forcing function for fresh-eyes review, consistent with how distributed open-source governance handles charter changes.

**New Recommendation #3 — Add a "deliberation panel composition disclosure" requirement to the Sync Impact Report.**

Governance-skeptic's DC-2 ("zero disputes is engineerable via panel composition") and strictness-skeptic's OB-4 ("self-consistency methodology produces consistent over-strictness") both point at deliberation-panel selection as an unmonitored variance source. The fix is small: every Sync Impact Report for a MINOR or MAJOR amendment MUST document (a) the personas/agents in the proposal deliberation, (b) the personas/agents in the verification deliberation (which MUST differ from the proposal panel by at least one persona), and (c) any persona that recused or was recused. This makes panel composition auditable across amendments and creates back-pressure against panel-stacking. Cost is one paragraph per amendment; benefit is structural integrity of the deliberation-as-evidence claim.

### Position Summary

The cross-reviews moved my position in three meaningful ways. First, governance-skeptic's structural critiques — particularly the PATCH carve-out's exploitability (DC-1 against my #4) and the verification-deliberation acceptance-bar over-promotion (DC-2 against my #5) — were correct, and I have withdrawn or amended both recommendations. The PATCH carve-out withdrawal is the larger concession; replacing it with a lightweight "N/A — typo fix" answer in the self-assessment achieves the same goal (no procedural overhead on trivial changes) without creating a class-wide bypass. Second, strictness-skeptic's calibration-vs-enforcement sequencing argument (DC-1 against my #2) was partly right and partly wrong; I have amended the CI lint to enforce form only, leaving the contested semantic calibration to reviewer judgment until the precedent log accumulates evidence. This satisfies the "don't ratchet a mis-calibrated gate" concern without abandoning enforcement entirely. Third, governance-skeptic's reference-topology correction (DC-4 against my #10) caught a real error in the cross-link direction, and I accept it.

What I retained against pressure: the substantive scaffolding remains intact — template embedded in Sync Impact Report, form-checking CI lint, routing table with constitutional-remand safety valve, derived precedent log, v2.4.0 sync impact report with grandfathering disclosure, criterion 2 wording fix with criterion 3 coordination extension, expedited path with rollback authority, XVII cross-link in the corrected direction, and verification deliberation with documented-disputes acceptance bar. The single largest unresolved disagreement is with strictness-skeptic on the AND→partial-OR restructure: I reject R-1 as theory-first loosening that imports new vagueness, and propose instead an evidence-triggered calibration review (New Recommendation #1) that activates only when the precedent log shows ≥3 criterion-1-only rejections. This gives strictness-skeptic's calibration concern a mechanical hearing without committing to a structural change before evidence exists.

Bottom line on the revision: ship the gate with the amended enforcement scaffolding, the corrected cross-link direction, the lightweight PATCH-equivalent self-assessment, the rollback-authority expedited path, and the new panel-composition disclosure + cooling-off period. Defer the structural AND→OR debate until the precedent log has data. This is a smaller, more cautious package than my original review proposed, and a more rigorous one than strictness-skeptic's calibration-first sequencing would produce. It survives both cross-reviews' dangerous-contradiction objections while preserving the original goal: a gate that a future amendment author can actually use without phoning a maintainer.
