### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Enforcement Mechanism Coordination vs Individual Precision**
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims**: "Establish Enforcement Coordination" (Priority P3) recommending to "Document which enforcement mechanisms are responsible for which principles" and coordinate across principles to prevent "enforcement gaps and redundant tooling."
  - **wording-precision claims**: "Define real-world cost precisely" (Priority P1) and other precision-focused recommendations that emphasize making individual principles operationally unambiguous through specific definitions rather than coordination frameworks.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If enforcement coordination becomes the primary mechanism for resolving ambiguity, it creates external dependencies that could undermine principle-level precision. A principle that relies on coordination documents for clarity is not self-contained and could become unenforceable if coordination breaks down.
  - **Suggested resolution**: wording-precision should yield on systematic coordination while cross-principle-coherence should yield on requiring coordination as a substitute for precision. Individual principles must be precise enough to enforce independently, with coordination serving as an optimization layer rather than a dependency.

- **Amendment Complexity Preferences**
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims**: Multiple P2 recommendations for systematic frameworks: "Create Principle Interaction Index," "Establish Precedence Framework," and "Document Amendment Risk Assessment."
  - **wording-precision claims**: Focus on tactical precision fixes with specific operational definitions, warning against "Constitutional Completeness Metrics" (P3) that "No mechanism exists to identify when the constitutional framework is 'complete'" but treating this as lower priority than immediate precision issues.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Adding systematic frameworks before fixing fundamental wording precision issues could institutionalize current ambiguities. Conversely, focusing only on tactical fixes without systematic frameworks could create a coordination debt that becomes harder to resolve as the constitution grows.
  - **Suggested resolution**: cross-principle-coherence should acknowledge that systematic frameworks require precise foundational definitions to be effective. wording-precision should recognize that some coordination frameworks may be necessary to maintain precision at scale. Sequence should be: fix critical precision issues first (P1), then add systematic frameworks (P2) that build on precise foundations.

- **Documentation Scope and Precision Trade-off**
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims**: "Formalize Interaction Documentation Pattern" (P1) requiring "explicit interaction documentation for any new principle that touches domains covered by existing principles, with a standardized format."
  - **wording-precision claims**: "Add scope exclusion statements" (P3) recommending "Out of scope:" subsections but at lower priority, focusing instead on making positive requirements more precise rather than expanding documentation scope.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Standardized interaction documentation could dilute precision if it becomes formulaic rather than substantive. Conversely, precise individual principles without explicit interaction clarity could create unexpected conflicts.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Interaction documentation should be required but must meet precision standards. Each interaction section must include operationally precise boundary definitions, not just acknowledgment that interactions exist.

### Tensions

- **Systematic vs Tactical Remediation Philosophy**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: Takes a systematic approach with "Principle Interaction Index" and "Amendment Risk Assessment Framework" to prevent future issues through comprehensive governance structures.
  - **wording-precision's position**: Takes a tactical approach targeting specific precision failures like "real-world cost" and "source code changes" boundaries with concrete operational definitions.
  - **Nature of tension**: Systematic approaches risk creating governance overhead without solving immediate enforceability problems, while tactical approaches risk fixing symptoms without addressing underlying coordination problems.
  - **Coordination needed**: Establish clear sequencing where tactical precision fixes inform the design of systematic frameworks, ensuring that governance structures solve actual precision problems rather than theoretical coordination issues.

- **Constitutional Evolution Speed**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: Recommends multiple P2 priority systematic changes that would significantly expand constitutional infrastructure ("interaction index," "precedence framework," "amendment impact assessment").
  - **wording-precision's position**: Focuses heavily on P1 and P2 precision fixes to existing principles with minimal constitutional expansion, treating systematic additions as P3 priority.
  - **Nature of tension**: Rapid systematic expansion could destabilize the constitutional framework before current precision issues are resolved, while slow tactical evolution could leave coordination gaps unaddressed as the constitution grows.
  - **Coordination needed**: Agree on constitutional change budget per amendment cycle - how many systematic changes can be absorbed while maintaining precision in existing principles.

- **Enforcement Philosophy**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: Emphasizes "Enforcement Mechanism Coordination" to prevent "enforcement gaps and redundant tooling" through systematic documentation of responsibilities.
  - **wording-precision's position**: Emphasizes making individual principles enforceable through precise operational definitions like measurement units and boundary specifications.
  - **Nature of tension**: Coordination-based enforcement assumes that gaps exist between well-defined principles, while precision-based enforcement assumes that ambiguous principles cannot be effectively coordinated.
  - **Coordination needed**: Determine whether enforcement problems are primarily precision problems (unclear what to enforce) or coordination problems (clear requirements but unclear who enforces).

- **Future-Proofing vs Present Clarity**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: Focuses on preventing future "amendment risk" and "principle drift" through systematic frameworks that govern constitutional evolution.
  - **wording-precision's position**: Focuses on resolving current enforceability gaps through specific definitions, treating future-proofing as lower priority than immediate operational clarity.
  - **Nature of tension**: Future-proofing mechanisms require current precision to be effective, while current precision fixes may not scale without systematic frameworks.
  - **Coordination needed**: Establish whether current precision issues are blocking effective governance or whether governance frameworks are needed to maintain precision gains.

- **Documentation Minimalism vs Comprehensiveness**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: Advocates for comprehensive documentation including interaction indexes, precedence frameworks, and amendment risk assessments.
  - **wording-precision's position**: Advocates for precise but minimal documentation, warning against "constitutional bloat" and emphasizing that precision should reduce rather than increase documentation burden.
  - **Nature of tension**: Comprehensive documentation may improve coordination but reduce accessibility, while minimal documentation may improve clarity but leave coordination gaps.
  - **Coordination needed**: Define documentation efficiency metrics that balance comprehensiveness with precision - what constitutes "just enough" documentation for effective constitutional governance.
