### Tensions

- **Process Content Extraction Scope**
  - **external-scholar's position**: "Extract §12-13 (compound debt acknowledgment and methodological lessons) as these are historical self-flagellation" while preserving "§11 (override-with-rationale scope restriction) as it establishes a binding procedural rule"

  - **Nature of tension**: external-scholar wants to extract historical content while preserving operationally necessary rules; naive-reader wants to strengthen verification mechanisms. Both aim for better operational clarity but through different approaches - removal vs strengthening.

- **Constitutional Content Philosophy**

  - **Nature of tension**: external-scholar seeks constitutional minimalism (current requirements only), naive-reader seeks constitutional completeness (requirements plus guidance). Both valid approaches to constitutional clarity but with different information density preferences.
  - **Coordination needed**: Establish criteria for what constitutes "current requirements" vs "implementation guidance" and agree on where the boundary lies between constitutional text and supplementary guidance.

- **Implementation Failure Prevention Strategy**
  - **external-scholar's position**: Focus on "doctrinal coherence and practical implementability through careful distinction between essential requirements and procedural scaffolding"

  - **Nature of tension**: external-scholar addresses failure through structural clarity (better organization of requirements), naive-reader through demonstration clarity (showing how requirements work). Both address implementation failure but at different stages of the implementation process.
  - **Coordination needed**: Sequence the approaches - external-scholar's structural clarity creates the foundation, naive-reader's practical examples demonstrate application of that foundation.
