### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Constitutional Content Philosophy**
  - **naive-reader claims**: "Constitutional principles can include universal deadlines when the deadline itself is part of the normative requirement... universal deadlines can be constitutionally legitimate when they serve the principle's universality claim across a defined temporal scope" (from their disposition of recommendation 8)
  - **external-scholar claims**: "Extract specific deadlines to implementation section, keeping principle requirements date-independent" (original recommendation 8, though I later withdrew this)
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If we simultaneously accept that deadlines belong in constitutional text AND that they should be extracted for doctrinal purity, we create inconsistent amendment principles. Future amendments won't know whether to embed temporal constraints or extract them.
  - **Suggested resolution**: naive-reader's substantive argument is stronger here. I should accept that universal deadlines serving universality claims can be constitutionally legitimate, while maintaining that implementation-specific deadlines should be extracted.

- **Process Archaeology Treatment**  
  - **naive-reader claims**: §11 (override-with-rationale scope restriction) establishes "a binding procedural rule" that should be preserved, while §§12-13 are "historical self-flagellation" that should be extracted (modified recommendation 1)
  - **external-scholar claims**: "Extract sections 11-13 to preserve doctrinal clarity" with §11 being "process archaeology" (original recommendation 1, though I later modified this)
  - **Why this is dangerous**: We both want to extract §§12-13 but disagree about §11. If §11 stays as binding procedure while §§12-13 are extracted as history, we need clear criteria for what makes procedural content constitutionally legitimate versus historical record.
  - **Suggested resolution**: naive-reader's distinction between "binding procedural rules" and "historical self-flagellation" provides the needed criteria. Accept their boundary: §11 creates ongoing obligations (keep), §§12-13 document past events (extract).

- **Implementation Template Approach**
  - **naive-reader claims**: "Provide both precise term definitions within the constitutional text AND worked examples/implementation templates that demonstrate those definitions in practice" (new recommendation)
  - **external-scholar claims**: "Provide technology-neutral examples of adequate specification while acknowledging that cross-product consumer contracts may require format translation layers" (modified recommendation 3)
  - **Why this is dangerous**: I advocated for minimal examples to preserve technology neutrality; they advocate for comprehensive templates to ensure implementability. These approaches could produce contradictory guidance about how much detail belongs in constitutional text.
  - **Suggested resolution**: naive-reader's insight that "definitional precision and practical guidance address different implementation failure modes" is correct. Both layers are needed, but constitutional text should carry essential constraints while templates demonstrate application.

### Tensions

- **Doctrinal Purity vs Practical Implementability**
  - **naive-reader's position**: Emphasized getting the spec implementable with "definitional precision" and concrete mechanisms (recommendations 1, 3, new recommendation on templates)
  - **external-scholar's position**: Emphasized doctrinal extraction and constitutional hygiene (recommendations 1, 2, 7)
  - **Nature of tension**: Constitutional adequacy and operational clarity pull in different directions - too much extraction creates implementability gaps, too much detail creates constitutional bloat.
  - **Coordination needed**: Establish clear criteria for what level of detail belongs in constitutional text versus implementation guidance, using naive-reader's "essential definitional constraints" boundary.

- **Cross-Product Integration Scope**
  - **naive-reader's position**: Modified recommendation 4 to include "cross-product coordination protocols to ensure both clear boundaries and manageable change processes"
  - **external-scholar's position**: Modified recommendation 4 to acknowledge "cross-product integration needs" but emphasize "technology neutrality" 
  - **Nature of tension**: Operational coordination requires concrete specificity while constitutional principles should be format-agnostic. Both are valid concerns operating at different abstraction levels.
  - **Coordination needed**: Constitutional text specifies coordination requirements (what must be coordinated) while implementation guidance specifies coordination mechanisms (how to coordinate).

- **Priority Assessment Methodology**
  - **naive-reader's position**: Withdrew implementation planning guidance after recognizing "priority level" misassessment exposed by cross-reviews (recommendation 7 disposition)

  - **Coordination needed**: Align on whether constitutional adequacy or operational deliverability takes precedence when they conflict.
