### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Plain-language requirement — Remove vs. Fix-in-Place**
  - **skeptic-mathematical claims**: Recommendation 1 (P1): "Remove requirement 3 from the Principle XVI headline and body. Relocate it to a dedicated section in AGENTS.md or an operational guidance document." The rationale is that plain-language fails Governance Criterion 2 (falsifiable scope) — "Determining whether 'Equilibrium quality: 0.87' satisfies or violates the requirement demands reviewer interpretation."
  - **skeptic-cross-principle claims**: Recommendations 1 and 5 both presuppose plain-language STAYS in XVI. Rec 1 proposes adding: "A plugin recommendation that includes numerical output without a conforming plain-language explanation is treated as invalid plugin output under Principle XV." Rec 5 proposes adding: "Plain-language pairing is a usability requirement, not a safety-critical path under Principle XXIV." Both amendments are substantive edits to XVI's plain-language content — they cannot be applied to text that has been removed.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If both recommendation sets are adopted without resolution, the synthesis document contains: (1) a header removing plain-language from XVI alongside (2) detailed language specifying how plain-language should be treated under XV and XXIV. An implementor reading both faces directly contradictory instructions — delete the requirement vs. refine the requirement with specific failure semantics. The failure mode is a synthesis document that simultaneously says to remove and to amend the same text.
  - **Suggested resolution**: This requires the synthesis to answer a threshold question first: does plain-language fail Criterion 2 (mathematical's diagnosis) or only Criterion 1 plus compositional clarity (cross-principle's diagnosis)? The SIR's grandfathering disclosure already names XVI's plain-language as failing the gate — supporting mathematical's Criterion 2 diagnosis. Cross-principle's framing (Criterion 1 gap + XV/XVI composition) identifies additional problems but does not refute the Criterion 2 failure; they are additive diagnoses. Synthesis should adopt mathematical's removal recommendation. Cross-principle's XV/XVI composition concern then becomes a downstream note: if plain-language moves to operational guidance, XV's warning-and-continue semantics should be stated explicitly in that guidance. Cross-principle yields on location; mathematical accommodates the composition clarification in the receiving document.

- **Stage-3 in restructured XVI — Keep as headline invariant vs. Collapse into VII+VIII cross-reference**
  - **skeptic-mathematical claims**: Recommendation 4 proposes restructuring the headline to: "conversus's optimization pipeline MUST satisfy two invariants across its three stages: (1) parameter pinning... (2) shape determinism..." This restructuring keeps stage-3 as a named invariant referenced in the headline. The proposal presupposes stage-3 remains in XVI as structural content worth naming.
  - **skeptic-cross-principle claims**: Recommendation 2, option (b): "Remove stage-3 as a standalone structural requirement in XVI and replace it with a cross-reference: 'Stage 3 applies Principles VII and VIII to the optimization domain: same template, same pinned parameter set → structurally identical assembled function. No additional normative requirement beyond VII and VIII is introduced.'" If option (b) is adopted, mathematical's restructured headline becomes incoherent — it references a stage-3 invariant that the body text has declined to specify.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: The two restructuring proposals are mutually exclusive execution paths. Mathematical's restructured headline requires a stage-3 invariant. Cross-principle's option (b) removes the stage-3 invariant. Adopting both without resolution produces a headline that enumerates "two invariants across three stages" while the body text says stage-3 has no independent normative content — an incoherence in a governance document consumed as execution truth.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Cross-principle's option (a) (name a concrete CI lint or contract test for stage-3 bit-identical assembly) is fully compatible with mathematical's restructuring. The synthesis should adopt option (a) — which both adds the Criterion 1 verification artifact cross-principle requires and preserves the stage-3 structural claim mathematical's headline restructuring needs. Cross-principle's option (b) should be recorded in the SIR as a Criterion 3 composition acknowledgment (per cross-principle's rec 4) rather than executed as a structural removal.

- **Diagnosis of plain-language failure mode — Criterion 2 (falsifiable scope) vs. Criterion 1 (verification artifact)**
  - **skeptic-mathematical claims**: Off-Base Assumptions section ("Assumption: the plain-language output requirement is constitutionally verifiable") and Missed Opportunities section ("Plain-language output pairing has no minimum-sufficiency test"): the diagnosis is Criterion 2 failure. "If a reviewer must reason 'well, X might be okay if Y,' the principle is too vague for the constitution." Even a schema-level required field would not fix this because field content — whether a given explanation is adequate — cannot be validated mechanically.
  - **skeptic-cross-principle claims**: Missed Opportunities ("Plain-language pairing has no schema-level enforcement") and Recommendation 5: the diagnosis is Criterion 1 failure (no verification artifact) plus unresolved XXIV scope. The implicit assumption is that adding a schema-level required field in the plugin output schema could satisfy Criterion 1 and rescue the requirement constitutionally.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Cross-principle's recommended fix (schema-level required field + XXIV declaration) does not address mathematical's identified problem. A required field validates field presence, not field adequacy. "OK" or "high equilibrium" in a plain-language field passes schema validation but fails mathematical's interpretation-dependent adequacy test. If synthesis adopts cross-principle's schema-enforcement recommendation as the fix for plain-language without also addressing mathematical's scope critique, the result is a false fix — the requirement has a nominal verification artifact but remains constitutionally non-falsifiable. Implementations would build schema validators that provide no meaningful enforcement.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The synthesis should acknowledge both diagnoses as correct and additive: plain-language fails both Criterion 1 (no feasible adequacy verification path) and Criterion 2 (adequacy is interpretation-dependent). Schema enforcement can address presence but not adequacy, which means Criterion 1 is not satisfied by schema enforcement alone for a requirement where the normative content is adequacy. This compound failure strengthens mathematical's removal recommendation. Cross-principle should acknowledge that its schema-enforcement path is insufficient for the adequacy problem, not merely the presence problem.

### Tensions

- **Enforcement granularity — soften pending MUST vs. add contract test to close the gap**
  - **skeptic-mathematical's position**: Recommendation 5 ("Distinguish shipped enforcement from planned enforcement"): the principle uses present-tense MUST for an enforcement mechanism that is future work. The proposed fix is to soften language — replace "is required" with "is pending" and add explicit notice that enforcement depends on manual verification until the contract test ships.
  - **skeptic-cross-principle's position**: Recommendation 2 ("Add a stage-3 verification artifact to XVI or collapse stage-3 into VIII"): the absence of a stage-3 verification artifact is itself the defect. Rather than softening the MUST, the recommendation is to close the gap — either name a concrete CI lint or contract test (option a), or remove the standalone stage-3 requirement (option b). The SIR note (rec 4) similarly proposes forward-looking acknowledgment, not softening.
  - **Nature of tension**: These pull in opposite directions on how to handle missing enforcement. Mathematical's approach acknowledges the gap transparently by making the present-vs-future tension explicit through language softening. Cross-principle's approach closes the gap operationally by requiring an artifact. For a governance document consumed as execution truth, both approaches have merit: softening is honest about current state; adding the artifact converts aspiration into operational reality. The tension is between transparency-about-incompleteness and urgency-toward-completeness.
  - **Coordination needed**: These are compatible if applied to different stages. Cross-principle's option (a) (name the stage-3 contract test) can be adopted to close the stage-3 enforcement gap, making mathematical's present/future tension moot for that specific point. For stage-2 enforcement (GapFiller.fill() re-resolution contract test, which is also future work per the SIR), mathematical's rec 5 softening appropriately applies. The two recommendations coexist by applying cross-principle's approach to stage-3 (add named test) and mathematical's approach to stage-2 (soften pending language).

- **Dual-taxonomy restructuring vs. stage-3 distinctness acknowledgment**
  - **skeptic-mathematical's position**: Recommendation 4 ("Reconcile two organizational frameworks"): proposes restructuring the headline to explicitly reference the pipeline, making the stage-to-requirement mapping prominent. Stage-3 is named as a structural component of the headline: "two invariants across its three stages."
  - **skeptic-cross-principle's position**: Recommendation 4 ("Document Criterion 3 analysis for XVI stage-3 in the SIR"): proposes adding to the SIR that "stage-3 is included for completeness of the three-stage pipeline description, not as an independent normative requirement." This frames stage-3 as descriptive infrastructure rather than a normative claim worthy of headline prominence.
  - **Nature of tension**: Mathematical's restructuring treats stage-3 as a real invariant worth naming in the headline. Cross-principle's SIR addition characterizes stage-3 as a pipeline completeness description — not independent, not a normative claim in its own right. A reader who internalizes both would see the headline asserting "two invariants across three stages" while the SIR says stage-3 isn't independently normative. This is not a logical contradiction but it creates interpretive friction: is stage-3 headline content or footnote content?
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should determine whether stage-3's role is a structural claim (mathematical's framing) or a completeness description (cross-principle's framing). These can coexist if the headline is carefully scoped: "two invariants operating across the three stages: [stage-1 is deterministic by construction; stage-2 requires pinning discipline; stage-3 applies Principles VII and VIII — no additional normative requirement introduced]." This acknowledges pipeline completeness without elevating stage-3 to an independent invariant, satisfying both reviews without forcing either to yield entirely.

- **Template registry stability — explicit prohibition vs. implicit dependency**
  - **skeptic-mathematical's position**: Recommendation 6 (P2, "Add explicit template registry stability requirement"): "The byte-identity guarantee has an implicit dependency on template registry stability. Unstated dependencies in constitutional text create exactly the 'silent failures' Principle X warns against." Recommends adding: "The template registry MUST be treated as immutable for the duration of a deliberation run."
  - **skeptic-cross-principle's position**: Does not address template registry stability. The cross-principle review focuses on the capability registry (via XI's Registry-First Declaration extension and XV's clarification) but does not consider whether the template registry requires independent stability requirements.
  - **Nature of tension**: This is an asymmetry, not a direct conflict, but it creates integration friction. Cross-principle's Recommendation 7 (IX cross-reference for stage-2 contract tests) and Recommendation 2 (stage-3 contract test) both depend on the execution context being stable between assemblies within a run. If the template registry is permitted to change during a run because no prohibition exists, the behavioral properties that cross-principle's contract tests are designed to verify become unreliable — a correctly pinned parameter could produce different output if the template changed between the first and second assembly invocation.
  - **Coordination needed**: Mathematical's rec 6 (template registry stability) is a precondition for the behavioral guarantees cross-principle's contract test recommendations are designed to verify. Synthesis should adopt mathematical's rec 6 early in the implementation ordering — it closes an implicit dependency that would otherwise undermine the integrity of cross-principle's enforcement recommendations. No yield is required from either side; the sequencing is the resolution.

- **XVII content classification of plain-language — execution logic vs. contribution guidance**
  - **skeptic-mathematical's position**: Recommendation 1 implicitly classifies plain-language output pairing as contribution guidance or authoring standards for plugin developers — hence the relocation target is AGENTS.md. This aligns with Principle XVII's classification of "contribution guidelines (naming conventions, template authoring rules)" as belonging in AGENTS.md rather than SKILL.md/constitutional principles.
  - **skeptic-cross-principle's position**: Recommendation 1 proposes adding behavioral execution language to XVI's plain-language bullet ("treated as invalid plugin output under Principle XV... core deliberation continues"). This classifies plain-language enforcement as execution logic — it specifies runtime behavior during deliberation. Principle XVII says execution logic (behavior that occurs at runtime during deliberation) belongs in SKILL.md and constitutional principles, not AGENTS.md.
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews implicitly classify the same requirement in different XVII categories. If plain-language enforcement is execution logic (cross-principle's framing), XVII supports keeping it in XVI/SKILL.md. If it is a contribution guideline (mathematical's framing), XVII supports moving it to AGENTS.md. The two reviews disagree about which XVII category applies, which is the upstream question determining the correct relocation target.
  - **Coordination needed**: The Criterion 2 failure (mathematical) provides a path through this tension. If plain-language adequacy cannot be evaluated without interpretation, it cannot function as execution logic — execution logic requires deterministic evaluation at runtime. A runtime agent cannot determine whether an explanation is adequate without the same interpretation-dependent reasoning the principle fails to specify. This means XVII's own classification logic, properly applied, supports mathematical's position (AGENTS.md). Cross-principle's fix-in-place approach would need to acknowledge that the XVII classification question cuts against it.

### Safe Agreements

- **XVI/XV composition creates an unresolved and dangerous behavioral ambiguity**
  - **Shared position**: Both reviews independently identify that XVI's MUST for plain-language pairing and XV's warning-and-continue plugin failure semantics produce contradictory behavioral contracts for the same triggering event: a plugin that emits numerical output without plain-language pairing. Neither review identifies an existing constitutional resolution to this composition.
  - **Combined evidence**: mathematical (Missed Opportunities, "No enforcement hook for XVI/XV composition"): "What is the status of numerical-only plugin output — is it a warning, a blocking failure, or a silent passthrough? The constitution specifies neither... The MUST in XVI is unenforceable without contradicting XV." Cross-principle (Off-Base Assumptions, "Assumption that XV and XVI compose without conflict"; Recommendation 1): "XV's warning-and-continue model is the only behavior consistent with plugin isolation. XVI's MUST creates a normative requirement... they establish contradictory behavioral contracts for the same event (plugin emits non-conforming output)." Independent discovery of the same structural conflict in two adversarial reviews is strong evidence the gap is real and not an artifact of interpretation. Note that the two reviews differ on remedy (remove vs. resolve), which is addressed under Contradiction 1 above, but agree completely on diagnosis.
  - **Confidence level**: High — the composition question has a determinate answer (XV's isolation model governs plugin failure behavior at runtime; the deliberation cannot block on plugin output) that any implementation would have to answer. The gap is structurally verifiable.

- **Stage-3 deterministic assembly is a composition of VII and VIII, not an independent normative requirement**
  - **Shared position**: Both reviews independently conclude that stage-3 "deterministic assembly" — given a template and fully-pinned parameter set, the output is bit-for-bit identical — is logically implied by composing Principle VII (same inputs → structurally identical output) with Principle VIII (prefer template-driven/mechanical behavior over LLM inference).   - **Combined evidence**: mathematical (Off-Base Assumptions, "Assumption that stage-3 determinism is distinct from VIII"): "stage-3 deterministic assembly is VIII + VII applied to the objective function domain... Their composition directly implies: given the same template (mechanical, rule-based) and the same pinned parameter set (same inputs), the assembled output is structurally identical. Cross-principle (Off-Base Assumptions, "Assumption that stage-3 determinism is distinct from VIII"; Recommendation 2): "stage-3 assembly determinism is an application of Principles VII and VIII to the optimization domain and would fail Criterion 3 if proposed as a standalone principle...   - **Confidence level**: High — the VII+VIII composition argument is independently derived and technically sound.   - **Confidence level**: Medium — the agreement is explicit in mathematical's review and implicit in cross-principle's framing.
