### Executive Summary

The constitution is a governance document that accumulates normative invariants for a prompt-orchestrated deliberation engine. This review audits the cluster of principles that govern determinism, reproducibility, transparency, and template-driven behavior — specifically Principles VII, VIII, IX (behavior-over-shape extension), XI (Registry-First Declaration extension), XV, and XVI — for structural overlap, terminological collision, and composition gaps. The review finds that the constitution is largely well-disciplined: the v2.3.2 clarification block on XVI shows evidence that the authors have already negotiated at least one VII/XVI tension. However, three genuine problems remain that the existing text does not resolve: a restatement-level overlap between XVI stage-3 and VIII that the distinctness gate (Governance §Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, Criterion 3) would reject if applied to stage-3 alone; a hard composition gap between XVI's "MUST include plain-language pairing" and XV's "plugin failure MUST NOT block core execution"; and a terminological collision between IX's use of "shape" (prohibited) and XVI's use of "shape determinism" (required) that will confuse readers who encounter the two principles in sequence. The most important recommendation is to resolve the XVI/XV composition gap explicitly, because it is the one gap that causes a silent behavioral ambiguity that no other principle covers.

### Alignment

- **VII/XVI carve-out is explicit** (XVI Clarification v2.3.2, paragraphs 1-4): The constitution explicitly acknowledges the tension between VII's "structurally identical output" and XVI's stochastic stage-2, resolving it by naming the assembled objective function as the determinism boundary. This is the correct resolution and is clearly marked. The carve-out is a genuine structural distinction, not a workaround.

- **Distinctness gate applied retroactively** (Governance §Constitutional Inclusion Criteria, Criterion 3, worked examples): The gate's worked example — "a principle proposing 'all template variables MUST be lowercase' fails Criterion 3" — demonstrates that the authors already understand the difference between refinement-within-a-principle and new-principle. This precedent is the correct lens for evaluating whether XVI stage-3 is distinct from VIII.

- **Pinning discipline adds genuine content** (XVI, "Clarification v2.3.2," bullet 2): The requirement that resolved parameter values "MUST be persisted to `objective.yml`" and "MUST NOT be re-invoked for parameter resolution within the same run" covers ground that neither VII nor VIII addresses. VII requires same-input/same-output determinism; it says nothing about within-run parameter re-resolution being prohibited. This is a real enforcement discipline VIII does not imply.

- **Plugin output schema requirement is correctly homed** (XVI, third bullet under plain-language output): Plain-language pairing for plugin recommendations is an output-content requirement, not a registry-availability requirement (XI) or a data-flow/isolation requirement (XV). XVI is the correct home for this requirement given that it is about mathematical transparency, not capability exposure or failure semantics.

- **"Shape" term in IX is self-delimiting** (IX, behavior-over-shape extension, "Operational test" paragraph): The IX extension defines "shape test" precisely as "checks only field presence, type, or non-null status WITHOUT also constraining the value's meaning." This definition is narrow enough that readers who also encounter XVI's "shape determinism" (which is about bit-identical assembled output) should be able to distinguish them. The surrounding context does most of the work.

### Missed Opportunities

- **No enforcement hook for XVI/XV composition**: The plain-language pairing requirement in XVI is a MUST. Plugin Isolation (XV) says plugin failure "MUST NOT block core execution — warnings only" and that "malformed output is better than no output" (echoed in V). What is the status of numerical-only plugin output — is it a warning, a blocking failure, or a silent passthrough? The constitution specifies neither a schema-level enforcement mechanism (as XXIV would require for safety-critical paths) nor a warning-and-continue behavior. The gap means the MUST in XVI is unenforceable without contradicting XV. Impact: **high**.

- **Stage-3 "bit-for-bit identical" standard lacks a verification artifact**: Principle XXIV requires safety-critical paths to have a "contract test reproducing the failure scenario." The Governance gate (Criterion 1) requires a mechanical verification path. XVI names stage-3 determinism as a structural requirement but does not name a contract test or CI lint for it. The only enforcement reference is the follow-up TODO for spec 014 FR-012/SC-004. By contrast, stage-2 pinning has `SourceProvenance.filled_by` as an auditable hook. Stage-3 has nothing. Impact: **high**.

- **"Shape determinism" and "shape test" are the same word with opposite valences**: In IX, "shape" is the property a test MUST NOT rely on exclusively (it is prohibited as a sole assertion). In XVI, "shape determinism" is a required property of an assembled artifact. A reader who has internalized IX's prohibition on shape-centric reasoning will encounter XVI's "shape determinism" requirement and experience friction. A one-word disambiguation (e.g., "structural determinism" or "assembly-form determinism" in XVI) would eliminate this. Impact: **medium**.

- **Criterion 3 (Distinctness) analysis of XVI stage-3 is missing from the SIR**: The SIR comment block for XVI (v2.3.2) documents Criterion 1 (falsification clause) and Criterion 2 (determinism-scope scope) but does not demonstrate that stage-3 deterministic assembly is distinct from VIII. The Governance gate (Criterion 3) requires this. Composing VIII ("prefer template-driven behavior over inference") with VII ("structurally identical output") already yields: "for template-driven artifacts, same template + same config → same output." Stage-3 determinism is exactly that composition. The SIR should either argue why stage-3 is not captured by this composition, or acknowledge that stage-3 is an application of VIII+VII to the optimization domain and belongs in an VIII extension rather than in XVI as a standalone structural requirement. Impact: **medium**.

- **Plain-language pairing has no schema-level enforcement**: XVI requires plain-language pairing for plugin recommendations. XXIV (Safety-Critical Defense-in-Depth) requires schema-level required fields, parser-level validation, and a contract test for safety-critical paths. Whether plain-language pairing is safety-critical is never declared. If it is, XXIV's three-layer defense applies and is missing. If it is not, the MUST is aspirational. The constitution needs to declare which category this falls into. Impact: **medium**.

- **VII's "narrowed by Principle XVI" parenthetical is the only cross-reference, and it points the wrong direction**: VII says "structurally identical output (narrowed by Principle XVI for LLM gap-filling output)." This parenthetical tells readers who are reading VII that XVI narrows it. But readers who are reading XVI are not told to read VII first — the XVI text just states the carve-out. Bidirectional cross-referencing would reduce the risk that readers of XVI miss the VII baseline requirement they are narrowing. Impact: **low**.

- **Principle IX's "behavior-over-shape" extension is invoked by domain-specific principles (XXIV, XXVI) but XVI never references it**: XVI requires that stage-2 LLM gap-filling produce auditable, reproducible results via `SourceProvenance.filled_by`. This is a behavioral requirement (what the code *does*) rather than a shape requirement. The test discipline for this requirement would fall under IX's behavior-over-shape extension — but XVI does not reference IX. A contract test for stage-2 pinning that only asserts `SourceProvenance.filled_by is not None` would be a shape test that IX prohibits. XVI should cross-reference IX's extension to make this explicit. Impact: **low**.

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **Assumption that stage-3 determinism is distinct from VIII**: XVI (stage-3 paragraph) treats "deterministic assembly: given a template and a fully-pinned parameter set... identical bit-for-bit" as a new structural requirement. This assumes distinctness from VIII. The correct understanding is that stage-3 deterministic assembly is VIII + VII applied to the objective function domain. VIII says: "prefer mechanical template-driven behavior over LLM inference... Orchestration decisions are rule-based, not inferred." VII says: "same inputs → structurally identical output." Their composition directly implies: given the same template (mechanical, rule-based) and the same pinned parameter set (same inputs), the assembled output is structurally identical. Stage-3 does not add a new normative requirement; it applies existing ones to a specific artifact. The distinctness gate (Governance, Criterion 3) would reject stage-3 as a standalone principle. It survives as part of XVI only because XVI contributes other genuinely distinct content (the stochastic-stage-2 carve-out, the pinning discipline, the plain-language pairing requirement). The SIR should acknowledge this rather than treating stage-3 as independently distinct.

- **Assumption that XV and XVI compose without conflict**: XVI (plain-language pairing bullet) assumes that its MUST requirement for plugin output format is compatible with XV's plugin failure semantics. The correct understanding is that they conflict: XV defines plugin failure as a warning-and-continue scenario, which means malformed or non-conforming plugin output (including numerical-only output without plain-language pairing) must be warned-and-continued per XV. But XVI's MUST implies that numerical-only output is a violation, not a warning scenario. The two principles establish contradictory behavioral contracts for the same event (plugin emits non-conforming output). This is not a minor ambiguity; it determines whether a plugin that strips plain-language output causes a deliberation failure or a warning.

- **Assumption that the XVI v2.3.2 SIR's "conservative-wording rule" validates the carve-out from VII**: The SIR states "per the conservative-wording rule, the self-consistency wording is adopted." This implies the carve-out from VII is settled by the verification deliberations. But the conservative-wording rule governs wording choices within a proposed amendment — it does not perform the Criterion 3 distinctness check. The carve-out's distinctness from VII (i.e., that the XVI clarification adds content VII does not imply) was not explicitly argued in the SIR. The constitution assumes the carve-out satisfies Criterion 3 because it was verified; the verification deliberations in fact focused on word-level disputes (tightened-standalone rewrite, drop XXIV citation) rather than distinctness from VII.

### Actionable Recommendations

1. **Resolve the XVI/XV composition gap with explicit failure-semantics** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: XVI (plain-language pairing bullet): "Plugin recommendations... MUST include plain-language explanations alongside numerical outputs." XV: "A plugin that crashes, times out, or returns invalid data emits a warning. The core deliberation completes normally."
   - **Proposed change**: Add to XVI's plain-language pairing bullet: "A plugin recommendation that includes numerical output without a conforming plain-language explanation is treated as invalid plugin output under Principle XV — the output is preserved with a warning and core deliberation continues. The pairing requirement is enforced at the plugin output schema boundary (Principle XXIV layer 1), not by blocking core execution." Add a symmetric cross-reference in XV: "Pairing requirements for plugin output are governed by domain-specific principles (see Principle XVI for optimization plugin output); non-conforming output is invalid output for the purposes of this principle."
   - **Rationale**: XV's warning-and-continue model is the only behavior consistent with plugin isolation. XVI's MUST creates a normative requirement; the resolution is that the MUST is enforced at schema validation (XXIV layer 1), not at execution, with failure handled as XV specifies.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Two normative requirements govern the same event with contradictory behavioral implications. Implementors will make incompatible choices. Future deliberations reviewing plugin output handling will produce conflicting rulings.

2. **Add a stage-3 verification artifact to XVI or collapse stage-3 into VIII** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: XVI (stage-3 paragraph) declares "deterministic assembly" as a structural requirement. Governance (Criterion 1) requires a concrete mechanical verification path. No verification artifact is named for stage-3.
   - **Proposed change**: Either (a) name a concrete CI lint or contract test for stage-3 bit-identical assembly in XVI's Clarification v2.3.2 enforcement block, or (b) remove stage-3 as a standalone structural requirement in XVI and replace it with a cross-reference: "Stage 3 applies Principles VII and VIII to the objective function domain: same template, same pinned parameter set → structurally identical assembled function. No additional normative requirement beyond VII and VIII is introduced."
   - **Rationale**: Option (a) satisfies Governance Criterion 1. Option (b) acknowledges the composition and eliminates redundant normative content, reducing the risk that future amendments propose conflicting requirements for stage-3. The pinning discipline (stage-2) already has `SourceProvenance.filled_by` as an auditable enforcement hook; stage-3 should have the same.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Stage-3 is an unverifiable normative requirement. A PR that violates bit-identical assembly would have no CI check to catch it. The verification artifact gap also means the constitution fails its own Criterion 1 for XVI.

3. **Rename "shape determinism" in XVI to eliminate the IX collision** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: XVI (stage-3 paragraph and requirements list): "shape determinism" as a required property. IX (behavior-over-shape extension): "shape test" as a prohibited-when-sole-assertion category. "Shape" carries opposite valences in the two principles.
   - **Proposed change**: Replace "shape determinism" with "assembly-form determinism" or "structural determinism" throughout XVI. Update the requirements list item to read: "**assembly-form determinism** — given a template and a fully-pinned parameter set, the assembled objective function is byte-identical on every assembly."
   - **Rationale**: The terminological collision is not a safety problem, but it creates unnecessary cognitive load for readers who have internalized IX's prohibition on shape-centric reasoning. Renaming is a pure patch (zero content change) that eliminates the collision.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Readers will encounter "shape determinism is required" (XVI) after "shape tests are prohibited" (IX) and must spend effort determining whether these are intentionally different uses. Comments, questions, and amendment proposals will repeat this disambiguation work indefinitely.

4. **Document Criterion 3 analysis for XVI stage-3 in the SIR** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: XVI's SIR (v2.3.2) documents Criteria 1 and 2 implicitly (falsification clause, determinism-scope scope) but does not argue that stage-3 deterministic assembly is distinct from VIII+VII.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to the XVI SIR (or the next relevant SIR): "Criterion 3 assessment for stage-3 deterministic assembly: stage-3 assembly determinism is an application of Principles VII and VIII to the optimization domain and would fail Criterion 3 if proposed as a standalone principle. It survives as content within XVI because XVI's other contributions (stage-2 stochastic carve-out, pinning discipline, plain-language pairing) are genuinely distinct. Stage-3 is included for completeness of the three-stage pipeline description, not as an independent normative requirement."
   - **Rationale**: The distinctness gate (Governance, Criterion 3) applies prospectively. Acknowledging in the SIR that stage-3 is a composition application (not an independent principle) preempts future amendment proposals that would re-raise this question.
   - **Risk if ignored**: A future amendment author or verifier will independently discover the VIII+VII composition and raise it as a Criterion 3 violation during deliberation. The response will require the same analysis now, but under adversarial conditions.

5. **Declare plain-language pairing as XXIV-governed or exclude it from XXIV scope** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: XVI requires "MUST include plain-language explanations alongside numerical outputs." XXIV (Safety-Critical Defense-in-Depth) applies to "safety-critical paths — synthesis verdict generation AND provider protocol implementation." Plain-language pairing is not declared as safety-critical or excluded from XXIV.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to XVI's plain-language pairing bullet: "Plain-language pairing is a usability requirement, not a safety-critical path under Principle XXIV. Enforcement is by schema-level required field in the plugin output schema (XXIV layer 1 applied voluntarily); parser-level and contract-test layers (XXIV layers 2 and 3) are recommended but not required for this requirement." Alternatively, if pairing is safety-critical: "Plain-language pairing is a safety-critical requirement under Principle XXIV. The three-layer defense applies: required field in plugin output schema, parser-level validation, and a contract test reproducing a no-plain-language numerical-only output."
   - **Rationale**: The ambiguity about XXIV scope creates an implementation gap. If pairing is safety-critical, XXIV mandates a contract test that does not yet exist. If it is not, the MUST is aspirational and enforcement is voluntary. Either way, the declaration resolves the implementation question.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Implementors will independently reach different conclusions about XXIV scope. The resulting implementation will either over-enforce (blocking plugin output without a XXIV contract test) or under-enforce (no schema validation because "it's not XXIV territory").

6. **Add a bidirectional cross-reference between VII and XVI** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: VII contains "(narrowed by Principle XVI for LLM gap-filling output)" as a parenthetical. XVI does not reference VII.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to XVI's opening paragraph or to the Clarification v2.3.2 block: "Principle XVI operates within Principle VII's reproducibility framework. The assembled objective function satisfies VII's same-inputs/same-output requirement. The stage-2 stochastic gap-filling carves an explicit exception: parameter *values* may differ across runs; the assembled *shape* is stable per stage-3. Readers of this principle should read Principle VII first."
   - **Rationale**: Principles that narrow other principles must be legible in both directions. A reader who encounters XVI first has no pointer to VII and may miss the baseline requirement being specialized.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Low individual cost, but accumulated over future amendments: reviewers who read XVI without VII context will propose redundant amendments re-establishing the relationship.

7. **Add an IX cross-reference to XVI's enforcement block for stage-2 contract tests** (Priority: P3)
   - **Current state**: XVI (Clarification v2.3.2, enforcement bullet): "Principle XXIV applies — a contract test reproducing the re-resolution failure pattern is required." IX's behavior-over-shape extension is not referenced.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to the enforcement bullet: "Contract tests for stage-2 pinning MUST assert behavioral properties (Principle IX extension): asserting `SourceProvenance.filled_by is not None` alone is a shape test (prohibited under IX); the test MUST also verify that the same parameter identifier resolves to the same value within a run, and that a second resolution attempt within the same run raises or returns the pinned value, not a new LLM call."
   - **Rationale**: Without this cross-reference, a contract test for stage-2 pinning will naturally be written as a shape assertion (`filled_by is not None`). This passes CI but does not verify the actual pinning behavior — it is exactly the class of shape test IX prohibits. The cross-reference converts an easy mistake into a deliberate choice.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Stage-2 contract tests will be written as shape tests. The pinning discipline will have nominally-passing CI coverage that does not actually test that parameters are pinned across within-run re-resolution attempts.
