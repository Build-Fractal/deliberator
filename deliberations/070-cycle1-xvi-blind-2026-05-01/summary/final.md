### Dangerous Contradictions Found

**Resolved Contradictions**

1. **Plain-language requirement: Remove vs. Fix-in-Place**

   SM argued in Phase 1 that the plain-language output pairing requirement fails Governance Criterion 2 (falsifiable scope) because determining whether a given explanation is "adequate" requires reviewer interpretation — a determination no automated check can make (skeptic-mathematical/review.md, Missed Opportunities, first item; Off-Base Assumptions, second item). SM's Phase 1 position was categorical excision to AGENTS.md.

   SCP argued in Phase 1 that the requirement should remain in XVI with in-place patching: import XV's warning-and-continue semantics, add schema-level enforcement for field presence, and declare whether the requirement falls under Principle XXIV's three-layer defense (skeptic-cross-principle/review.md, Recs 1 and 5).

   The cross-review exposed the contradiction as dangerous because implementing both simultaneously would produce XVI text treating non-conforming output as an invalid-plugin signal under XV while AGENTS.md treated the same requirement as non-normative authoring guidance (skeptic-mathematical/cross-reviews/skeptic-cross-principle.md, "Dangerous Contradictions — Plain-language requirement: excise vs. patch-in-place"). The cross-review explicitly identified the threshold condition: the patch path survives only if a minimum-sufficiency test can be written that makes "adequate plain-language explanation" mechanically evaluable.

   **SCP conceded.** In Phase 3, SCP acknowledged that schema enforcement validates field *presence*, not field *adequacy*, and concluded that no minimum-sufficiency test could be written — making the Criterion 2 failure structural, not fixable by enforcement tier (skeptic-cross-principle/revision.md, Rec 1 modified). Both agents converged on excision as the constitutionally sound default, with XV warning-and-continue semantics explicitly stated in whatever document receives the requirement.

   **Resolution**: Excision of plain-language requirement from XVI headline and body. Relocation to AGENTS.md or equivalent operational guidance. Receiving document must explicitly state: non-conforming plugin output does not block core deliberation, is preserved with a warning, and is handled per Principle XV's warning-and-continue model.

2. **Stage-3 enforcement: Soften pending MUST vs. Add verification artifact**

   SM argued in Phase 1 (Rec 5) that the enforcement sub-bullet's present-tense "is required" for the stage-2 contract test conflicts with the "filed as a follow-up" status — aspirational enforcement presented as operational. ### Systemic Contradictions

- **Dual-taxonomy organizational structure in XVI**
  - **Manifests in**: SM-4 (reconcile organizational frameworks), SM disputes D1 (stage-3 headline status), SCP-2 (stage-3 verification artifact), SM Missed Opportunities second item ("Stage 1 has no headline structural requirement").
  - **Root cause**: Principle XVI presents three structural requirements in its headline (parameter pinning, shape determinism, plain-language output pairing) and a three-stage pipeline in its body (symbolic parsing, LLM gap-filling, deterministic assembly). These two frameworks have different organizational structures: the requirements don't map 1:1 to the stages, stage-1 has no corresponding headline requirement, and stage-3 (the only deterministic stage) maps to requirement 2 — not requirement 3. The dual taxonomy forces readers to mentally reconcile two competing schemas for the same content.
  - **Implication for spec**: The headline should be organized around the pipeline, not a separate requirement enumeration. After excision of the plain-language requirement (which has no stage correspondence) and acknowledgment that stage-3 is a VII+VIII composition, the headline reduces to one independently normative requirement: parameter pinning at stage-2. The body should then describe all three stages explicitly, noting stage-1's determinism-by-construction, stage-2's pinning discipline, and stage-3's VII+VIII composition with its own verification artifact in the enforcement block.

- **Grandfathered content conflicting with retroactively-applied constitutional gate**
  - **Manifests in**: SM-1 (unbundle plain-language), SCP-1 (XVI/XV composition), SCP-5 (XXIV scope declaration), the Governance section's grandfathering disclosure explicitly naming XVI's plain-language as a gate failure.
  - **Root cause**: The v2.4.0 Constitutional Inclusion Criteria were added after XVI was ratified. The gate's grandfathering clause preserves XVI's content at ratified status but explicitly discloses that the plain-language requirement fails Criterion 2. The clause says migration is "a separate, intentional act" — but this deliberation is exactly that act. The grandfathering creates a tension between constitutional stability (once ratified, content is stable) and constitutional quality (the gate was designed to prevent non-falsifiable content).
  - **Implication for spec**: The migration act this deliberation has produced — excision of the plain-language requirement — is the appropriate resolution of this systemic tension. The template for future similar situations is now established: grandfathered content that fails the gate can be migrated via a deliberation that explicitly identifies the receiving document, which this one has done (AGENTS.md as plugin authoring guidance with explicit XV runtime semantics).

- **Cross-principle failure semantics not declared within XVI**
  - **Manifests in**: SCP-1 (XVI/XV composition gap), SM New Rec 1 (receiving-document semantics), SM Missed Opportunities ("No enforcement hook for XVI/XV composition"), SCP cross-review of SM ("XVI's MUST creates a normative requirement... they establish contradictory behavioral contracts").
  - **Root cause**: XVI states its requirements with MUST language but never declares what happens when a plugin fails to satisfy them. XV declares warning-and-continue semantics for plugin failure generically. When XVI's MUST intersects with XV's warning-and-continue model at the same triggering event (plugin emits non-conforming output), the two principles establish contradictory behavioral contracts and no third principle arbitrates between them.
  - **Implication for spec**: Any principle that establishes requirements for plugin output must explicitly declare whether violations trigger XV's warning-and-continue semantics or constitute blocking failures. The excision of XVI's plain-language requirement from constitutional status reduces this gap, but the structural lesson applies to future plugin-output requirements: the relevant principle must cross-reference XV and declare failure semantics in-line, not leave them implicit.

- **Aspirational enforcement presented as operational in MUST language**
  - **Manifests in**: SM-5 (distinguish shipped vs. planned enforcement), SCP modified Rec 2 (stage-2 enforcement softening), SM Executive Summary ("present-tense MUST language is structurally inconsistent with the Origin note's admission that the enforcement infrastructure is 'partly under construction'").
  - **Root cause**: XVI uses MUST language for requirements whose enforcement contract tests are acknowledged as future work in the same sentence ("filed as a follow-up"). In a document consumed as execution truth by agents, MUST implies the check is operational. The simultaneous presence of MUST and "filed as a follow-up" creates an internal contradiction that agents cannot resolve without access to implementation status information external to the spec.
  - **Implication for spec**: Constitutional enforcement language should distinguish between "the requirement exists" (MUST, always appropriate if the requirement is ratified) and "the enforcement artifact exists" (requires a separate, explicitly temporal qualifier). The two-step fix — soften enforcement artifact status to "pending" with an exit condition tied to the artifact shipping — converts the present-tense/future-tense contradiction into an honest two-state model.

- **Stage-3 composition claimed as independent normative content**
  - **Manifests in**: SM Off-Base Assumptions ("Assumption that stage-3 determinism is distinct from VIII"), SCP Off-Base Assumptions (same finding, independently derived), SM-4, SCP-2, SM-N2, SCP Rec 4.
  - **Root cause**: The three-stage pipeline description creates an implicit expectation that each stage contributes independent normative content, but stage-3's bit-identical assembly guarantee is logically entailed by composing Principle VII (same inputs → structurally identical output) with Principle VIII (mechanical template-driven behavior). The safe agreement from this deliberation — reached independently by two adversarially-positioned agents — confirms that no stage-3-specific claim has been identified that VII+VIII don't already cover. The constitution's three-stage framing obscures this by presenting stage-3 as a third structural requirement alongside the independently normative stage-2 pinning discipline.
  - **Implication for spec**: The constitution should acknowledge where pipeline stages serve as descriptive completeness elements rather than independent normative contributions. The SIR mechanism is the correct vehicle for this acknowledgment. Presenting stage-3 as equivalent to stage-2 in normative weight misleads readers and creates spurious distinctness where the Governance gate would find composition.
