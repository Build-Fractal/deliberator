### Systemic Contradictions

- **Amendment taxonomy was designed for addition and never completed for removal or external modification**
  - **Manifests in**: NS Dispute 1 and NS Dispute 2 (the grandfathering/retirement policy dispute and the XIX immediate-stage classification dispute both arise from the absence of a documented removal taxonomy); BCS Dispute 2 (the MUST vs. SHOULD dispute about retroactive Origin notes arises from the absence of a documented arbiter-modification pathway); NS-Recs 1, 4, and 6 collectively (each addressing a separate dimension of the missing removal taxonomy); BCS-Recs 2 and 9 (addressing the absent arbiter-modification pathway)
  - **Root cause**: The three-criterion gate (v2.4.0) was added to the Governance section to answer "what qualifies for constitutional inclusion." It was not designed to answer "what happens when something leaves" (removal), "when a principle's scope is modified by an external deliberation process" (arbiter-ruling), or "what happens to the identifier of a removed principle" (number retirement). The gate is rigorous in one direction and silent in the other three. The asymmetry is structural: the gate was added at a specific point in the document's evolution, and removal/modification taxonomy was never added as a complement.
  - **Implication for spec**: The Governance section needs a removal taxonomy symmetric to the gate's addition taxonomy: (a) a removal checklist (what steps apply when a principle is retired, including tombstone creation, MAJOR version bump, citation audit, and retirement list update); (b) an arbiter-ruling amendment pathway (when a deliberation arbiter ruling modifies normative principle scope, what amendment record is required and how it enters the governance trail); (c) a principle number stability policy (retired numbers are permanently reserved, the no-reuse rule, and the consequence for violating it). These three additions together constitute the missing half of the amendment taxonomy.

- **Constitutional stable-interface declarations do not extend to the constitution's own governance identifiers**
  - **Manifests in**: NS-Rec-3 and NS-New-1/BCS-New-2 (the atomic bundle requirement triggered by Principle II's own atomicity clause); the observation that principle numbers are used as stable identifiers throughout PR descriptions, governance logs, and constitutional cross-references but are not listed among Principle II's stable contracts
  - **Root cause**: Principle II's stable-interface list was designed for technical artifacts consumed by the agent runtime: structural markers, template variables, the dispute-parsing subsystem, the preset schema, reference file paths, and the dispatch table. The list reflects a design-time view of what consumers exist. Principle numbers are consumed in a different register (PR descriptions, governance logs, external documentation, historical citations) — a register not represented in the original list. The omission was not deliberate; principle numbers were not yet treated as a class of stable identifier when Principle II was drafted.
  - **Implication for spec**: Adding principle numbers to Principle II's stable-interfaces list is the correct fix, but it immediately triggers Principle II's own atomicity clause. This makes the addition self-referential in a structurally interesting way: the amendment that declares principle numbers stable must itself comply with the atomicity clause it is asserting — meaning the no-reuse rule, tombstones, and grandfathering correction must all land in the same change. The implication is not a problem but a discipline: the first time the constitution declares a new class of stable identifier, it must simultaneously produce all the governance machinery that makes the declaration meaningful.

- **Governance documentation quality falls below the standard the constitution itself requires**
  - **Manifests in**: NS-Recs 1 and 2 (no-reuse rule and tombstones exist as shared understanding, not as text); BCS-Rec-2 modified and BCS-New-1 (three arbiter-originated amendments lack formal amendment records); both agents' independent citation of Principles IV and XI as violated by the undocumented gap behavior; the Safe Agreements section of both Phase 4 dispute documents confirming the same finding from different analytical starting points
  - **Root cause**: The constitution has become progressively more rigorous about requiring documentation of everything — Principles IV, XI, XIX, XXIV each demand that invariants be written down, that governance rules have authoritative locations, and that non-extractable items be enumerated explicitly. But this rigor was not applied retroactively to the Governance section itself when those principles were added. The Governance section was written before v2.4.0 and has not been updated to meet the standard the later principles establish. The document demands more of its domain rules than it demands of its own governance rules.
  - **Implication for spec**: The Governance section should be audited against Principles IV and XI as if it were any other part of the specification. Every governance rule that exists only in the authors' shared understanding — the no-reuse rule, the permanent-retirement policy, the arbiter-ruling pathway — must be documented in its authoritative location (the Governance section). The gap between "things that are true and practiced" and "things written in the Governance section" is the primary source of the disputes in this deliberation, and closing that gap is the unifying purpose of the P1 bundle.

- **Principle body text and its extensions exist in different normative registers without mutual cross-reference**
  - **Manifests in**: BCS-Rec-1 (IX's Extension makes an architectural promise about XXIII/XXIV that XXIII/XXIV do not fulfill — the promise and its claimed beneficiaries exist in different documents without pointers); BCS-Rec-3 (XXVII characterizes XV through XV's Clarification, not through XV's headline or body, without making this sourcing explicit); BCS-Rec-7 (XVI's Clarification defines "deliberation run" — a term load-bearing for VII and IX — but VII and IX contain no pointer to the definition)
  - **Root cause**: Principles have a main body (established at principle creation) and Extension/Clarification blocks added later with version tags. Blocks can reframe a principle's meaning, define new terms, or establish architectural relationships with other principles, but those relationships are not propagated as cross-references to the principles they affect. The Extension/Clarification blocks are versioned additions that modify their parent principle's effective meaning without triggering updates to other principles' bodies.
  - **Implication for spec**: Extensions that make architectural promises about named later principles (like IX's Extension claiming that XXIII and XXIV "reference this general framework") should either cite those later principles explicitly in the Extension text or trigger a reciprocal cross-reference addition in those principles when the Extension is ratified. Extensions that define terms used by earlier principles (like XVI's Clarification defining "deliberation run") should trigger forward-pointer additions in those earlier principles. The amendment process's current guidance ("document the change, rationale, and impact on existing specs") covers the impact-on-specs dimension but does not operationalize it as a cross-reference audit requirement.

- **Retroactive documentation symmetry creates an inconsistency between what is documented and how thoroughly**
  - **Manifests in**: BCS Dispute 2 (the MUST vs. SHOULD dispute about retroactive Origin notes); NS-New-2 (retroactive documentation symmetry); the parallel structure between tombstones for VI/X (retroactive documentation of number gaps) and Origin note amendments for XXIV/XXV/XXVII (retroactive documentation of arbiter-ruling gaps); the Safe Agreements section of both Phase 4 dispute documents confirming this asymmetry as a shared finding
  - **Root cause**: The constitution provides different levels of historical documentation for different types of governance events. Active principles have full Origin notes. Number gaps (VI, X) currently have no documentation. Arbiter-originated modifications (XXIV L724–727, XXV L774–775, XXVII L836–839) have the ruling embedded in normative body text but no formal amendment record. These three levels of documentation reflect an organic accretion pattern: each element was added or removed at a different point in the document's history, under different practices, by contributors with different documentation habits. No policy exists for regularizing this variation.
  - **Implication for spec**: The document needs a retroactive documentation policy — or at minimum, a one-time retroactive repair — that establishes consistent documentation for all classes of historical governance event. The principle of symmetric treatment is clear: if number gaps deserve tombstones (which both agents agree), then arbiter-ruling modifications deserve amendment records of equivalent formality. The cost is bounded (two tombstones plus three Origin note edits); the benefit is a document that applies its own governance standards consistently across its full historical record.

- **Dispute: Grandfathering clause wording — "MUST NOT be reused" vs. "subject to the gate"**
  - **Positions**: numbering-skeptic (disputes.md, Dispute 1) holds that the grandfathering clause must commit to explicit "MUST NOT be reused by any future amendment regardless of gate criteria" language. body-coherence-skeptic (revision, Rec 6 surviving; disputes.md, Convergence point 3) proposes "any future principle assigned those numbers is subject to the full three-criterion gate" and argues this text is "compatible with both a never-reuse policy and a gate-eligible policy."

The RFC/CVE discipline NS invokes is clear: retired numbers are never reused unconditionally; the slot is closed regardless of whether new content would qualify. BCS argues that the practical commitment is identical — BCS explicitly states in revision that any new principle would receive a number after XXVIII — and that the proposed text achieves the same result without the "absolutist" framing. Principle XI demands that the rule be stated directly, not derivable from an interpretation technique. Second, NS explicitly corrected this exact framing in its own Phase 3 revision, having identified it as incoherent in its original Rec 6. BCS's proposed text restores structurally the same hedging that NS withdrew as an error. Third, the two policies (never-reuse and gate-eligible) have materially different implications for the validity of historical citations: under never-reuse, "Principle VI" in any historical document refers to nothing, permanently; under gate-eligible, "Principle VI" would refer to a new principle if one is ever ratified, retroactively changing the meaning of those historical citations.
