#### Dispute: Grandfathering clause wording — "MUST NOT be reused" vs. "subject to the gate"

**Positions:**
- **numbering-skeptic** (disputes.md, Dispute 1): The grandfathering clause must commit to explicit "MUST NOT be reused by any future amendment regardless of gate criteria" language. BCS's proposed text ("any future principle assigned those numbers is subject to the full three-criterion gate") governs the *conditions* of assignment rather than prohibiting assignment — embedding an affirmative permission to assign. A text whose only route to compatibility with never-reuse is through treating a conditional as vacuously true is gate-eligible governance with a plausible-deniability reading, not neutral governance text.

- **body-coherence-skeptic** (revision, Rec 6 surviving; disputes.md, Convergence point 3): The proposed text is "compatible with both a never-reuse policy and a gate-eligible policy." BCS argues the practical commitment is identical — any new principle would receive a number after XXVIII, not VI or X — and that the text achieves this without "absolutist" framing. BCS further notes its text is compatible with adding an explicit MUST NOT clause in the Principle Number Stability subsection, while the grandfathering clause governs a more specific scenario.

**Synthesizer's assessment:** NS's position is stronger for three independent reasons: the vacuous-conditionality structural problem; that NS corrected this exact framing in Phase 3 revision and BCS's text restores the hedging NS withdrew as an error; and that the two policies have materially different implications for historical citation validity under the RFC/CVE discipline.

**Ruling:** Adopt NS's explicit "MUST NOT be reused by any future amendment, regardless of gate criteria" language for the grandfathering clause.

**Grounding citation:** Principle IV and Governance Criterion 2 jointly require this ruling. Principle IV states: "Agents MUST NOT rely on conventions not written in SKILL.md." Governance Criterion 2 states: "The principle's wording MUST be specific enough to flag a hypothetical future PR as violating, without requiring 'interpretation.'" BCS's proposed text requires a reader to determine that the conditional ("any future principle *assigned* those numbers") is vacuously true before concluding the clause is compatible with never-reuse. That determination is interpretive work Criterion 2 prohibits. Principle VIII reinforces this: governance text that can only be read correctly by applying an interpretation technique is imposing agent reasoning where a deterministic rule is available. NS's text is falsifiable on its face — a PR that reuses VI or X is immediately identifiable as a violation without interpretive work. BCS's text is not.

**Rationale:** The core of BCS's argument is that the practical commitment is identical — BCS explicitly states any new principle would receive a number after XXVIII. If that commitment is genuine and BCS does not intend to leave the door open for VI or X reuse, then BCS's proposed text is an unnecessarily indirect way to express it. The Governance Criterion 2 test asks whether a hypothetical future PR can be flagged as violating *without requiring interpretation*. A PR proposing a new principle numbered VI could construct the argument: "The grandfathering clause says any principle assigned VI is subject to the three-criterion gate; this proposed principle satisfies the three-criterion gate; therefore, assigning it VI is permitted." Under BCS's text, that argument is syntactically valid — the text does not prohibit the assignment; it governs the conditions of assignment. A governance clause that can be plausibly read to permit what it is intended to prohibit fails the falsifiability standard. NS's text closes this reading path completely.

**Rejected position:** BCS's "compatible with both" framing is not adopted. BCS's strongest argument — that its text is compatible with an explicit MUST NOT clause in the Principle Number Stability subsection — actually reinforces the ruling against it: if the explicit prohibition is what governs, then the grandfathering clause's implicit compatibility with never-reuse is doing no independent governance work. The clause that carries the prohibition should say so directly. "Compatible with" is not the same as "implements."

**Required changes:** In `CONSTITUTION-v3.0.0-blind.md`, Governance section, grandfathering clause paragraph: replace "Existing principles I-XXVII are grandfathered" with: "Existing principles (I–V, VII–IX, XI–XXVII, totaling 25 active principles at gate ratification, with XXVIII added post-gate for a current total of 26 active principles) are grandfathered.
