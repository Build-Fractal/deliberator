- **Dispute: Grandfathering clause wording — "MUST NOT be reused" vs "subject to the gate"**
  - **My claim**: Numbers VI and X are "permanently retired and MUST NOT be reused by any future amendment regardless of whether proposed content would satisfy the gate criteria." (numbering-skeptic revision, Rec 6 modification — gate-eligible language explicitly withdrawn)
  - **Opposing position(s)**: body-coherence revision, Rec 6 (surviving): the proposed grandfathering text says "any future principle assigned those numbers is subject to the three-criterion gate." body-coherence claims this text is "compatible with both a never-reuse policy and a gate-eligible policy; the recommendation itself does not foreclose either."
  - **Why I will not concede**: body-coherence's "compatible with both" framing is internally incoherent for a specific structural reason: a clause that governs what happens *when* numbers VI and X are assigned ("any future principle assigned those numbers is subject to...") embeds an affirmative permission to assign. A text that never-reuse means can only be read as compatible with never-reuse by treating it as a vacuously true conditional — it governs a scenario that can never occur. But governance text whose only route to compatibility with one of its claimed interpretations is through vacuous conditionality is not neutral; it is gate-eligible with a plausible-deniability reading attached. This is precisely the incoherence I identified in my own original Rec 6 and corrected by explicitly withdrawing the gate-eligible language. The correction was the substantive act. Adopting body-coherence's "compatible" framing re-imports the contradiction under a different label.

    The RFC/CVE discipline is the entire basis of my analytical position: stable identifiers are never reused because their citation validity depends on unconditional stability, not on fitness of proposed content. An RFC number is not "gate-eligible if a future RFC would meet the criteria for that slot." The slot is closed. Governance text that leaves the slot technically open (subject to gate) is following a different discipline than the one my review argued for, regardless of how the author characterizes that text's compatibility with the alternative.

  - **Counter-argument to their position**: body-coherence's revision acknowledges (Rec 6 disposition, framing clarification) that "any new principle elevating the multi-agent isolation content from XIX would receive a number after XXVIII, not VI." This is the substantive never-reuse commitment. If body-coherence actually intends never-reuse, the proposed text should say so directly. The argument that the existing text is "compatible with both policies" asks the synthesizer to adopt text whose intended meaning is admittedly one thing but whose literal reading supports another. That is precisely the category of ambiguity the document's own Principle IV (Documentation Is the Product) and Principle XI (Single Source of Truth) prohibit. The synthesizer should not inherit an ambiguous clause when a precise one is available.
  - **Proposed resolution path**: The synthesizer must choose between the two policy formulations. The clean "MUST NOT be reused by any future amendment regardless of gate criteria" text should be preferred because it is falsifiable on its face, requires no interpretive work, and aligns with the RFC/CVE discipline both reviews invoked. If the synthesizer determines gate-eligible is the correct policy, it should adopt explicit gate-eligible language — not text claimed to be compatible with both. A middle path (e.g., "reuse requires Supermajority ratification in addition to the three-criterion gate") is acceptable as a synthesis outcome if the synthesizer finds the never-reuse position too absolute; what is not acceptable is leaving the ambiguity unresolved.

The current text communicates finality; the note explicitly communicates conditionality. This is not a restatement of tautological amendability: any constitutional text is formally amendable, but a principle titled "Non-Extractable Core" does not currently signal that any of its items are expected to migrate. Adding that signal is a normative change to the principle's operational meaning for future implementors, regardless of whether the migration itself requires further amendment. The migration eligibility note belongs in the second stage, which lands after Governance improvements have established a strengthened amendment process under which the reclassification can be properly governed. If body-coherence accepts this scope limitation, the dispute resolves entirely — the conceptual labeling is genuinely PATCH and the sequencing dependency is satisfied. If body-coherence insists the "may be migrated" note must appear in the immediate stage, the synthesizer should classify the immediate stage as MINOR and apply the sequencing dependency accordingly.

### Final Position Statement

**Non-Negotiables**

1. The grandfathering clause must commit to explicit "MUST NOT be reused" language — not to text that is "compatible with" never-reuse while governing the conditions of reuse (numbering-skeptic revision, Rec 6 modification).
   The RFC/CVE discipline is the load-bearing rationale for all numbering recommendations. A text that specifies what happens when numbers VI and X are assigned, rather than prohibiting assignment, is gate-eligible governance dressed in neutral language. Adopting body-coherence's proposed text while labeling it as never-reuse-compatible reintroduces precisely the self-contradiction that the revision process was designed to resolve. The synthesizer should not inherit that ambiguity. The never-reuse position has a concrete, falsifiable text; body-coherence's "compatible" framing does not.

2. The atomic bundle (Recs 1, 2, 3, 4, 6) must land as a single PR — not as separable recommendations that may be accepted or deferred individually (numbering-skeptic revision, Rec 3 modification and new bundle recommendation).
   This is not a packaging preference but a Principle II obligation. Implementing Rec 3 without simultaneously implementing the no-reuse rule and tombstones is a Principle II atomicity violation performed in the act of asserting Principle II stability. The constraint is mechanical, not discretionary. Partial implementation is worse than no implementation: it produces a document that declares principle numbers as stable interfaces without the governance machinery that makes the declaration meaningful.

**Flexibility**

1. The exact wording of the tombstone entries for VI and X (numbering-skeptic revision, Rec 2 modified). What must be preserved: the word "permanently" and a MUST NOT reuse prohibition with no gate-eligible carve-out. What is flexible: the length of the tombstone, whether to include a forward reference to the Governance no-reuse rule, whether to include the RFC/CVE rationale inline or by reference, and whether to note the original principles' topics (if historically recoverable). The structural discipline matters more than the exact prose.

2. The sequencing of body-coherence's XIX immediate stage relative to the atomic bundle (Dispute 2). What must be preserved: "may be migrated" language must not appear in XIX until after the Governance improvements have landed. What is flexible: if the synthesizer determines that the immediate stage can be scoped to conceptual labeling only — Architectural invariants / Operational constants labels without any migration note — then the sequencing dependency is satisfied and the immediate stage may proceed independently. The dispute reduces entirely to scope containment. I am prepared to accept an immediate stage that is genuinely label-only; I am not prepared to accept one that includes migration eligibility language before the Governance framework for governing that migration is in place.
