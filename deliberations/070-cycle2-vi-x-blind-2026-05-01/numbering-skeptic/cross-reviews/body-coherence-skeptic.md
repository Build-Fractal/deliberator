### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Gap at VI: permanently retired number vs. natural candidate for a new principle**
  - **body-coherence-skeptic claims** (Missed Opportunities, item 8): "The gap at Principle VI (between V: Observable Deliberation and VII: Reproducibility) is the natural location where a principle governing multi-agent isolation constraints would fit. As written, these invariants are protected only by XIX's enumeration — a structural consequence of a decomposition rule, not a first-class principle."
  - **numbering-skeptic claims** (Recommendations #1–2, Off-Base Assumptions): "Principle numbers are permanent identifiers. A number assigned to a ratified principle is never reassigned, even after the principle is removed. Numbers VI and X are retired; they MUST NOT be reused by future amendments." Tombstone entries should mark VI and X as permanently reserved.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: body-coherence-skeptic's framing — describing VI as the "natural location" — creates a practical invitation to use that number when drafting a new multi-agent isolation principle. If an implementor reads body-coherence-skeptic's recommendation without also reading numbering-skeptic's tombstone proposal, they will draft a new principle VI in good faith while violating numbering-skeptic's P1 requirement. The failure mode is an amendment PR that fills VI sincerely believing one review endorses it, while another review's tombstone entry explicitly marks VI as permanently reserved — a constitutional conflict introduced during amendment, not caught until after ratification.
  - **Suggested resolution**: body-coherence-skeptic should clarify that "natural location" is a thematic observation, not a numbering recommendation. Any new multi-agent isolation principle should be placed at XXIX (or the next available number after XXVIII). numbering-skeptic's tombstone for VI should explicitly note that the thematic domain formerly associated with this position now corresponds to the new principle. The two recommendations become complementary: tombstone VI, assign new principle at XXIX, cross-reference in tombstone.

- **Governance section amendments: parallel additions without integration**
  - **body-coherence-skeptic claims** (Recommendations #2 and #9): The Governance section needs two additions — an "Arbiter rulings" sub-clause requiring that arbiter rulings modifying normative principle scope must be followed by a `/speckit.constitution` update, and a clarification that "arbiter-originated amendments: a conversus deliberation arbiter ruling that modifies the normative scope of a principle constitutes a constitutional amendment."
  - **numbering-skeptic claims** (Recommendations #1 and #4): The Governance section needs a "Principle Number Stability" subsection establishing the no-reuse rule, plus a removal checklist within the existing Amendments sub-clause covering tombstone creation, MAJOR version bump, citation audit, and retirement list update.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Both reviews propose modifications to the same Governance section, both touching the Amendments sub-clause. Without coordination, the combined text produces two parallel amendment tracks with no stated relationship. The critical gap: if an arbiter ruling retires a principle (removes it), body-coherence-skeptic's mechanism handles the ruling-to-amendment pipeline but says nothing about what happens to the retired number. numbering-skeptic's removal checklist handles number retirement but assumes developer-initiated amendments, not arbiter-originated ones. An arbiter-initiated removal falls between both mechanisms — neither fires the other.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Draft the Governance section additions jointly. The arbiter rulings sub-clause (body-coherence-skeptic) should explicitly cross-reference the removal checklist (numbering-skeptic) conditional on the ruling involving principle removal. The text should read something like: "When an arbiter ruling removes a principle, the removal checklist (see: Principle Number Stability) applies in addition to the amendment record requirement." This makes the two mechanisms additive rather than parallel.

- **P1 priority signals create conflicting amendment sequencing**
  - **body-coherence-skeptic claims** (Recommendations #1 and #2, both P1): Repairing IX Extension's cross-domain claim and formalizing arbiter rulings as amendments are the most urgent constitutional fixes.
  - **numbering-skeptic claims** (Recommendations #1–3, all P1): Documenting the no-reuse rule, adding tombstone entries, and adding principle numbers to Principle II's stable-interfaces list are the most urgent fixes.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: The two P1 sets are not in content conflict, but they share the Governance section as a modification target and signal different urgency to implementors. If treated as a single amendment PR, the reviewer faces six P1 items across two domains with no stated ordering or dependency. If treated as separate PRs, the Governance section receives conflicting piecemeal edits — body-coherence-skeptic's arbiter-rulings sub-clause and numbering-skeptic's removal checklist each independently modify the Amendments block, risking merge conflicts or structural incoherence in the resulting text. The specific failure mode: a "fix" PR that addresses body-coherence-skeptic's IX extension repair (a targeted PATCH to IX's body) would be misclassified as satisfying the combined P1 mandate when it has not touched Governance at all.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Decouple the amendment targets. body-coherence-skeptic's IX Extension fix (modifies IX's body only) and XV/XXVII characterization fix (modifies XXVII's body only) are PATCH-class and can land first without touching Governance. All Governance section additions — arbiter rulings sub-clause (body-coherence-skeptic) + number-retirement policy + removal checklist (numbering-skeptic) — should be batched into a single coordinated MINOR-class amendment PR, authored jointly.

### Tensions

- **Level of analysis: content coherence vs. governance structure**
  - **body-coherence-skeptic's position** (throughout, Missed Opportunities and Recommendations #1–9): The review operates at the level of cross-reference accuracy and principle body coherence — does XXVII's characterization of XV match XV's actual content? Does IX's extension match XXIII and XXIV's practice? These are targeted, PATCH-class repairs to specific principle bodies.
  - **numbering-skeptic's position** (throughout, Missed Opportunities and Recommendations #1–7): The review operates at the level of governance infrastructure — are amendment mechanisms documented? Are stable identifiers protected by policy? These are MINOR-class Governance section expansions.
  - **Nature of tension**: Both levels of repair are necessary, but they suggest different amendment PR structures and different urgency orderings. Content-level repairs (IX, XV/XXVII) require careful attention to how each principle is read in sequence; Governance-level repairs (numbering policy, removal checklist) require careful attention to how the amendment process itself is specified. Running both simultaneously risks scope creep into a single PR that is difficult to review atomically. Conversely, serializing them means content-level repairs land before the Governance machinery for governing those repairs exists.
  - **Coordination needed**: Agree on a landing order. The natural sequence is: (1) content-level PATCH repairs first (IX extension fix, XV/XXVII characterization, line numbers removal from XVI), then (2) Governance MINOR amendments (numbering policy, arbiter rulings formalization, removal checklist, grandfathering clause correction). The PATCH repairs do not depend on the Governance changes; the Governance changes do not depend on the PATCH repairs. Serializing them reduces review scope and allows each class of change to be evaluated independently.

- **Calibration footnote repair: exemplar replacement vs. count clarification**
  - **body-coherence-skeptic's position** (Recommendation #4): Replace XII and XIII in the exemplar list with XXVIII: "prefer the structural pattern of principles whose verification artifact is named explicitly (e.g., Principles XXIV, XXVI, XXVIII)." The fix is about which principles demonstrate the pattern.
  - **numbering-skeptic's position** (Recommendation #5): Add a parenthetical clarifying the principle count: "(The constitution currently has 26 active principles; numbers VI and X are retired.)" The fix is about the denominator ambiguity created by the numbering gaps.
  - **Nature of tension**: Both recommendations address the same calibration footnote paragraph in the Governance section, but they are orthogonal repairs. body-coherence-skeptic's fix leaves the denominator ambiguity unresolved; numbering-skeptic's fix leaves the miscited exemplars in place. Applied independently, each PR leaves a half-repaired paragraph: the exemplar list is corrected but readers still cannot determine whether the constitution has 26 or 28 principles, or vice versa. Neither review acknowledges the other's concern about this paragraph.
  - **Coordination needed**: Apply both fixes atomically in a single edit to the calibration footnote. The combined text would read: "(The constitution currently has 26 active principles; numbers VI and X are retired.) When drafting new principles, prefer the structural pattern of principles whose verification artifact is named explicitly (e.g., Principles XXIV, XXVI, XXVIII)." This is purely additive — no trade-off between the two recommendations exists; they address different sentences in the same paragraph.

- **XIX separation of invariants vs. number assignment for any elevated principle**
  - **body-coherence-skeptic's position** (Missed Opportunities item 8, Recommendation #8): XIX conflates architectural invariants (one agent per output file, context-isolated agents, phase boundaries as hard barriers) with operational constants (agent count formulas, baseline features list). These should be separated into distinct categories within XIX. Separately, body-coherence-skeptic observes that multi-agent isolation "received the strongest consensus" and that the gap at VI is its "natural location."
  - **numbering-skeptic's position** (implicitly, through the no-reuse and tombstone recommendations): Any principle elevated from XIX's invariant category to first-class status must receive a new number, not VI or X.
  - **Nature of tension**: body-coherence-skeptic's Recommendation #8 to separate invariants from operational gotchas within XIX creates conditions under which the separated invariants become candidates for promotion to a first-class principle. body-coherence-skeptic identifies VI as the thematic fit but does not make a firm recommendation about whether promotion should happen or what number it would use. numbering-skeptic's framework would assign any new principle XXIX (next available), but does not address what the separation work in XIX sets up. The tension is a staging problem: the separation work creates a decision point (promote or not?) that neither review resolves, and the number question (VI or XXIX?) depends entirely on how that decision point is resolved.
  - **Coordination needed**: body-coherence-skeptic's Recommendation #8 should be extended to explicitly address whether the "architectural invariants" category is a candidate for promotion to a standalone principle, and if so at what number. numbering-skeptic's tombstone for VI should explicitly state that multi-agent isolation, if elevated, receives XXIX. Without this extension, Recommendation #8 leaves a partially-specified outcome that a future drafter will resolve ad hoc — probably by filling VI, which is the exact scenario the tombstone is meant to prevent.

- **Retroactive documentation symmetry: tombstones vs. arbiter ruling records**
  - **body-coherence-skeptic's position** (Missed Opportunities item 3, Off-Base Assumptions item 3): Arbiter rulings embedded in normative principle body text (XXIV L724–727, XXV L774–775, XXVII L836–839) without amendment records constitute a process violation. The proposed remedy is prospective: add a Governance sub-clause so future arbiter rulings follow the amendment process.
  - **numbering-skeptic's position** (Recommendations #2 and #3): No tombstone record exists for removed principles at VI and X. The proposed remedy is retrospective: add tombstone stub entries for both gaps documenting their retired status.
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews identify cases where the constitution's current text lacks documentation of how it came to be that way. But their proposed remedies have different temporal orientations: numbering-skeptic proposes retroactive repair (add tombstones now for historical gaps), while body-coherence-skeptic proposes prospective process (prevent future arbiter rulings from being undocumented, without explicitly asking for retroactive records for the three existing arbiter rulings in XXIV, XXV, and XXVII). The asymmetry means the amended constitution would have retroactive documentation for the number gaps (VI, X) but not for the arbiter-originated modifications, leaving an incomplete historical record.
  - **Coordination needed**: Apply retroactive documentation symmetrically. Either both categories receive retroactive records — tombstones for VI/X AND explicit Origin note amendments for XXIV L724–727, XXV L774–775, and XXVII L836–839 crediting the 2026-04-25 arbiter ruling as the amendment mechanism — or both are addressed only prospectively. The retroactive approach creates a consistent document; the prospective-only approach is simpler but leaves the historical incompleteness unacknowledged. Recommend retroactive: the cost is three small Origin note edits in XXIV, XXV, and XXVII, and the benefit is that the document's historical record matches its stated governance standard.

### Safe Agreements

- **XII and XIII are miscited as verification exemplars**
  - **Shared position**: body-coherence-skeptic (Missed Opportunities, item 4): "XII says 'The linter SHOULD eventually check for dead variables'... XIII says 'The test suite SHOULD verify enum completeness: grep for string literals'... Listing XII and XIII alongside XXIV and XXVI as exemplars of explicitly named verification artifacts misrepresents the calibration target the guidance is trying to teach. Impact: medium." numbering-skeptic (Missed Opportunities, item 5): "XII (L268–269) says 'The linter SHOULD eventually check for dead variables' and XIII (L288) says 'The test suite SHOULD verify enum completeness.' These are deferred aspirations, not named artifacts. XXVIII has two concrete 'Mechanical check:' descriptors... Citing XII and XIII misleads readers about what 'explicitly named verification artifact' means."
  - **Combined evidence**: Two reviews with entirely different analytical frameworks — one focused on body-level cross-reference coherence, one focused on numbering governance — independently cited the same two line references (XII L268–269, XIII L288), identified the same linguistic indicator of failure (SHOULD vs. MUST, "eventually" vs. a concrete check), and reached the same prescriptive conclusion (remove XII and XIII from the exemplar list, add XXVIII). The convergence is structural, not coincidental: both reviews were examining the Governance calibration footnote from different entry points and arrived at the same problem. This level of independent confirmation makes the miscitation one of the most clearly correctable errors in the document.
  - **Confidence level**: High. This should be treated as a mandatory correction. The exemplar list should drop XII and XIII and add XXVIII. No further deliberation is needed.

- **"I-XXVII are grandfathered" language requires correction**
  - **Shared position**: body-coherence-skeptic (Off-Base Assumptions, item 1): "The principle numbering contains known gaps at VI and X, meaning there are 25 principles in the I-XXVII range, not 27. The grandfathering clause states the range as if it were contiguous... if someone were to introduce a new 'Principle VI' or 'Principle X' as a later addition, the claim that VI and X are 'grandfathered' would be technically supportable from the text but historically wrong." numbering-skeptic (Missed Opportunities, item 5, Recommendation #5): "the total principle count is ambiguous: 26 active principles (XXVIII minus 2 retired) or 28 (the highest assigned number)... A reader trying to assess 'how much of the constitution satisfies the verification criterion' gets two different answers depending on which denominator they use." And (Recommendation #5): "The constitution currently has 26 active principles; numbers VI and X are retired."
  - **Combined evidence**: The two reviews identify different failure modes from the same ambiguous text. body-coherence-skeptic identifies a governance classification risk: a future principle added at VI could be misread as grandfathered when it is actually new and subject to the three-criterion gate. numbering-skeptic identifies a measurement risk: coverage assessments produce inconsistent denominators depending on whether the reader counts 26 or 28 principles. Both risks are plausible, not hypothetical. Together they establish that the current language is deficient in at least two independent ways — which means fixing only one (e.g., adding a count parenthetical without enumerating the active set explicitly) would leave the other failure mode open. The amendment text must both state the active count and explicitly enumerate the gap numbers as retired.
  - **Confidence level**: High. The Governance section's grandfathering clause should be amended to read something like: "Existing principles (I–V, VII–IX, XI–XXVII, totaling 25 principles, with XXVIII added post-gate) are grandfathered. Numbers VI and X are retired and MUST NOT be reused. The constitution currently has 26 active principles."

- **The Governance section's amendment process is under-documented relative to the constitution's own rigor standards**
  - **Shared position**: body-coherence-skeptic (Missed Opportunities item 3, Off-Base Assumptions item 3, Recommendations #2 and #9): The amendment process describes one mechanism (/speckit.constitution invocation) but three principles (XXIV, XXV, XXVII) were modified via a second mechanism (arbiter rulings) without documented records. The three-criterion gate (v2.4.0) governs additions rigorously; arbiter-originated modifications and principle removal are ungoverned. numbering-skeptic (Missed Opportunities items 2–4, Recommendations #1, #3, #4, #6): No no-reuse rule, no tombstone records for retired principles, no removal checklist. "The three-criterion gate... provides a detailed checklist for addition; removal has no equivalent. The asymmetry means additions are governed but removals are not."
  - **Combined evidence**: From entirely different entry points — body-coherence-skeptic from examining how specific principles were introduced, numbering-skeptic from examining how principles are retired — both reviews reach the same structural conclusion: the Governance section's amendment process was designed for the addition case and was never completed for modification and removal. The combined picture is more complete than either review alone: the gap is not one oversight but two parallel oversights (arbiter rulings and number retirement) that share the same root cause. The three-criterion gate is the highest-quality governance mechanism in the document; the rest of the Governance section is comparatively underspecified. A single focused MINOR amendment to the Governance section would satisfy both reviews' core concerns, even if the specific sub-clause texts require integration work.
  - **Confidence level**: High. This should be treated as the primary motivation for the coordinated Governance section amendment PR described in the Dangerous Contradictions section above.
