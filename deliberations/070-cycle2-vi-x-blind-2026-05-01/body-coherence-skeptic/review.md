### Executive Summary

This constitution governs a prompt-orchestrated deliberation system called conversus, establishing 26 principles (I–XXVIII with gaps at VI and X) that span spec discipline, interface contracts, functional programming standards, testing rigor, distribution integrity, and constitutional governance. The document is internally ambitious: it not only codifies operational rules but defines its own amendment criteria, includes a versioned extension block system, and demands that every principle be mechanically verifiable. For a constitution of this scope, the overall structural coherence is high — reciprocal cross-references are mostly maintained, Origin notes are consistently formatted, and the versioned Extension block system is well-disciplined.

The critical coherence failures are concentrated in three areas: (1) a stated architectural intent in Principle IX's extension block that is flatly violated by two later principles; (2) a de facto constitutional amendment mechanism — the 2026-04-25 deliberation arbiter — that operates outside the Governance section's documented amendment process and has done so three times; and (3) Principle XXVII's characterization of Principle XV that inverts XV's own headline and body emphasis. None of these create broken pointer errors (no cross-reference points at a nonexistent principle number), but each creates a reader who follows one principle's guidance and finds it contradicted by another.

The most important recommendation is to repair the IX Extension's cross-domain promise: either remove the claim that XXIII and XXIV "reference this general framework rather than introducing parallel behavioral validation requirements per domain," or add explicit back-references in XXIII and XXIV. This is the most visible internal contradiction in the document.

### Missed Opportunities

- **IX Extension's cross-domain promise is broken by XXIII and XXIV**: L183–186 states: "Domain-specific testing requirements — distribution validation, provider robustness, synthesis correctness — reference this general framework rather than introducing parallel 'behavioral validation' requirements per domain." But XXIII (L670–699) never cites Principle IX. XXIV (L701–730) never cites Principle IX. Only XXVIII (L845–846) explicitly references IX. The extension's stated architectural intent — that domain-specific principles would delegate to IX's framework rather than re-introduce parallel requirements — is simply not honored. A reader of IX's extension expects to find back-references in XXIII and XXIV; they do not exist. **Impact: high.**

- **XV's headline-body characterization inverted by XXVII**: Principle XV's headline is "Plugin Isolation." Its main body (L324–339) is entirely about what plugins cannot do: "Plugins consume core artifacts; they MUST NOT modify them," "Data flows one direction: core produces deliberation artifacts → plugins consume them." Principle XXVII then characterizes XV as "Principle XV governs how plugins **add** capabilities" (L821). This characterization is sourced from XV's Clarification (v2.3.1, L344: "Plugins extend conversus by registering new Capability objects"), not from XV's headline or body. A reader who trusts XXVII's summary of XV and then reads XV finds a principle that emphasizes isolation and prohibition, not capability addition. The characterization is not wrong, but it is architecturally misleading. **Impact: medium.**

- **Arbiter rulings used as amendment mechanism without Governance trace**: The 2026-04-25 deliberation arbiter appears three times as a constitutional authority: extending XXIV's scope (L724–727), ruling on XXV's ordering (L774–775), and ruling XXVII should be standalone (L836–839). The Governance section (L926–927) says "Amendments: Require documentation of the change, rationale, and impact on existing specs. Use /speckit.constitution to update." None of the three arbiter rulings references an associated /speckit.constitution invocation or amendment record. This is not a broken pointer — the principles containing these rulings exist — but it is a constitutional process gap: the constitution describes one amendment mechanism and the evidence points to a second mechanism (arbiter ruling) operating in parallel without the same traceability requirements. **Impact: medium.**

- **Governance exemplars for verification artifacts include two SHOULD-only principles**: L974–978 says: "When drafting new principles, prefer the structural pattern of principles whose verification artifact is named explicitly (e.g., Principles XI, XII, XIII, XXII, XXIV, XXVI)." But XII says "The linter SHOULD eventually check for dead variables" (L268–269, emphasis on "SHOULD eventually" — a deferred aspiration, not a named artifact). XIII says "The test suite SHOULD verify enum completeness: grep for string literals" (L288 — again SHOULD, no artifact path). XXIV (L719–722) and XXVI (L796–799) do have concrete artifact descriptions. Listing XII and XIII alongside XXIV and XXVI as exemplars of explicitly-named verification artifacts misrepresents the calibration target the guidance is trying to teach. **Impact: medium.**

- **Hardcoded implementation line numbers in constitutional text**: L445–447 reads: "pinning is auditable via `SourceProvenance.filled_by` (`conversus/schemas/construction.py:262-279`)." A constitution is a document whose claims survive implementation changes. Line numbers do not. Any insertion or deletion in `construction.py` before line 262 silently invalidates this constitutional reference without any structural detection mechanism. The class name `SourceProvenance` and the attribute `filled_by` are stable names (subject to Principle II contracts); the line numbers are not. Principle II (L18–41) covers stable interfaces for behavioral contracts but does not protect internal file:line references embedded in constitutional prose. **Impact: medium.**

- **"deliberation run" defined in XVI but load-bearing for VII and IX**: The term "deliberation run" is given a precise definition at L439–443 ("the lifetime of one `objective.yml` artifact; retries that reuse the same artifact are part of the same run..."). Principle VII's guarantees (L87–99) — particularly "Re-running a conversus with the same config overwrites cleanly. No accumulated state" — implicitly depend on understanding what constitutes a "re-run" vs. a continuation of the same run. Principle IX (L135–137) says "No mutable global state. Configuration is loaded once and passed explicitly" — again, the scope of "once" depends on the run boundary. No glossary exists in the constitution, and the embedded definition in XVI's clarification block is unlikely to be encountered by a reader of VII or IX who hasn't already read XVI. **Impact: low.**

- **XIX embeds operational runtime content as constitutional non-extractable core**: L569–571 designates "Important Notes / operational gotchas: re-run overwrite behavior, agent count formulas, template-vs-skill responsibility boundary, baseline features list" as non-extractable constitutional content. Under Principle XVII (L496–517), "execution logic" belongs in SKILL.md and "contribution guidelines" belong in AGENTS.md. "Agent count formulas" and "baseline features list" are operational content — they describe how to use the system, not an architectural invariant that must be impossible to accidentally violate. Principle XIX's stated reason for non-extractability is that these items are "needed by every invocation path or define invariants that must be impossible to accidentally violate" (L555–556). Agent count formulas are parameterized operational rules, not invariants. By listing them alongside the truly invariant multi-agent isolation rules (L561–565), XIX grants operational content the protection of constitutional permanence, which directly conflicts with XVII's separation of concerns. **Impact: medium.**

- **Multi-agent isolation rules in XIX have no standalone principle home**: L561–565 describes "Non-negotiable multi-agent rules: one agent per output file, all agents within a phase launch in a single message, each agent is context-isolated, no meta-agents, phase boundaries are hard barriers." XIX's Origin note says these "received the strongest consensus: any decomposition must make invariant violations impossible to introduce" (L579–580). These rules are architectural invariants governing the deliberation system's adversarial correctness. They are treated as non-extractable SKILL.md content, not as constitutional principles in their own right. The gap at Principle VI (between V: Observable Deliberation and VII: Reproducibility) is the natural location where a principle governing multi-agent isolation constraints would fit. As written, these invariants are protected only by XIX's enumeration — a structural consequence of a decomposition rule, not a first-class principle. If XIX were amended or relaxed, the invariants could be extracted. **Impact: medium.**

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **"I-XXVII are grandfathered" treats the range as contiguous**: L968–969 reads "Existing principles I-XXVII are grandfathered." The principle numbering contains known gaps at VI and X, meaning there are 25 principles in the I–XXVII range, not 27. The grandfathering clause states the range as if it were contiguous. This creates a latent ambiguity: if someone were to introduce a new "Principle VI" or "Principle X" as a later addition, the claim that VI and X are "grandfathered" would be technically supportable from the text but historically wrong (they never existed to be grandfathered). The intent is clearly to grandfather the 25 existing principles, but the stated range includes 2 nonexistent slots. The correct phrasing would name the existing set explicitly or acknowledge the gaps.

- **Principle XXVII's characterization of XV assumes the clarification is the dominant register**: XXVII (L821) says "Principle XV governs how plugins **add** capabilities." This assumes that XV's v2.3.1 Clarification, which introduces the registry-as-extension-interface model, is the authoritative reading of XV. But a reader encountering XV fresh reads a principle titled "Plugin Isolation" whose main body (L324–339) establishes a unidirectional, consumers-only data flow model. The clarification reframes plugins as active contributors to the registry — a model that is consistent with the main body but not derivable from it. XXVII's summary effectively requires the reader to have already internalized XV's clarification before XXVII's description of XV makes sense. A constitution should not require readers to hold extensions in mind when reading its cross-references.

- **The amendment process assumes arbiter rulings do not constitute amendments**: The Governance section (L926–927) describes a single amendment mechanism: "Use /speckit.constitution to update." But XXIV (L724), XXV (L775), and XXVII (L836) each attribute constitutional content to "The 2026-04-25 deliberation arbiter." The constitution implicitly assumes that arbiter rulings are interpretations or triggers, not amendments. If that is true, then embedding arbiter rulings in the normative body of principles (as XXIV does at L724, not just in an Origin note) blurs the line between constitutional text and deliberation outputs. If arbiter rulings can add normative scope to a principle (as XXIV's L724–727 does — extending scope from synthesis to provider protocols), they are functionally amendments. The Governance section does not address this distinction.

### Actionable Recommendations

1. **Repair IX Extension's cross-domain claim** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: L183–186 claims domain-specific principles "reference this general framework rather than introducing parallel 'behavioral validation' requirements per domain."
   - **Proposed change**: Either (a) add "See Principle IX (behavior-over-shape extension)" to the opening paragraph of XXIII and XXIV, or (b) amend the IX Extension text to read: "Domain-specific testing principles (XXIII, XXIV, XXVIII) operate within this framework; XXVIII explicitly references it. XXIII and XXIV express the same behavioral-over-shape discipline in domain-specific forms without requiring a forward citation."
   - **Rationale**: A constitution that states an architectural design ("domain-specific requirements reference this framework") and then produces two principles that don't is internally inconsistent. The inconsistency is verifiable by inspection and will be flagged by any future coherence review.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future readers of IX will expect XXIII and XXIV to cite it. When they do not, readers will either conclude the extension is aspirational (not normative) or that XXIII and XXIV are noncompliant with the IX framework — neither of which is the intended reading.

2. **Formalize arbiter rulings as amendment records** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: L724–727, L774–775, and L836–839 embed "The 2026-04-25 deliberation arbiter ruled/extended" as normative constitutional content without an associated amendment record. The Governance section (L926–927) documents no role for arbiter rulings in the amendment process.
   - **Proposed change**: Add a Governance sub-clause: "**Arbiter rulings**: a conversus deliberation arbiter ruling that modifies the normative scope of a principle constitutes a constitutional amendment and MUST be followed by a /speckit.constitution update within the same PR. The arbiter ruling serves as the rationale documentation; the /speckit.constitution update serves as the formal record. An arbiter ruling embedded in principle body text without a corresponding amendment record is a process violation detectable by diff-shape check (Principle XXVIII applies: the diff modifies a principle body, which categorizes as a production bug requiring ≥1 amendment-record file modified)."
   - **Rationale**: The constitution defines one amendment mechanism; three constitutional modifications were made through a second mechanism. Leaving these unreconciled creates a precedent that arbiter rulings are an alternate amendment pathway with no process requirements.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future deliberation arbiters may embed rulings in constitutional text without amendment records, gradually eroding the Governance section's authority as the sole amendment mechanism.

3. **Correct XV's characterization in XXVII** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: L821 says "Principle XV governs how plugins **add** capabilities." XV's headline is "Plugin Isolation" and its main body covers prohibition and isolation, not capability addition.
   - **Proposed change**: Change L821 to: "Principle XV's Clarification (v2.3.1) governs how plugins **add** capabilities via the registry; Principle XV's main body governs what plugins MUST NOT do (modify core artifacts, write to core directories)."
   - **Rationale**: The characterization is sourced from XV's clarification, not its headline or main body. Making this explicit preserves accuracy while correctly attributing the framing to the clarification rather than XV as a whole. A reader who follows XXVII's summary to XV should find the characterization confirmed.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Readers who trust XXVII's summary encounter XV and find a principle about isolation, not capability-addition. The mismatch creates confusion about what XV actually constrains.

4. **Replace XII and XIII with stronger exemplars in Governance calibration note** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: L974–978 cites "Principles XI, XII, XIII, XXII, XXIV, XXVI" as exemplars of principles with explicitly named verification artifacts.
   - **Proposed change**: Replace XII and XIII with XXVIII: "prefer the structural pattern of principles whose verification artifact is named explicitly (e.g., Principles XXIV, XXVI, XXVIII)." Optionally add: "XII and XIII have SHOULD-based verification targets not yet promoted to MUST; they do not meet this calibration bar and should not be cited as exemplars."
   - **Rationale**: XII (L268–269) says "The linter SHOULD eventually check for dead variables" and XIII (L288) says "The test suite SHOULD verify enum completeness." These are deferred aspirations, not named artifacts. XXVIII has two concrete "Mechanical check:" descriptors (L854–856, L870–875) that name the check's structure precisely. Citing XII and XIII misleads readers about what "explicitly named verification artifact" means.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Draft authors following this calibration note will produce principles with SHOULD-based deferred checks and believe they have satisfied Criterion 1.

5. **Remove line numbers from constitutional file:line reference in XVI** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: L445–447 reads: "pinning is auditable via `SourceProvenance.filled_by` (`conversus/schemas/construction.py:262-279`)."
   - **Proposed change**: Remove the line numbers: "pinning is auditable via `SourceProvenance.filled_by` in `conversus/schemas/construction.py`." If the specific location is important for audit purposes, move the line-number reference to an Origin note or footnote with a note that line numbers are point-in-time.
   - **Rationale**: Line numbers in a constitution are fragile in a way that stable interface names (governed by Principle II) are not. Principle II's stability contract applies to named interfaces, not to the line position of those interfaces in source files.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future modifications to `construction.py` will silently invalidate the constitutional reference. The stale reference will be discovered during audit, not at change time.

6. **Acknowledge numbering gaps in Governance grandfathering clause** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: L968–969 reads "Existing principles I-XXVII are grandfathered."
   - **Proposed change**: "Existing principles (I–V, VII–IX, XI–XXVII, totaling 25 principles) are grandfathered. Principles VI and X are absent from the numbering; any future principle assigned those numbers is subject to the full three-criterion gate."
   - **Rationale**: "I-XXVII" implies a 27-principle contiguous range when 25 principles exist. If a future principle VI or X is added, the grandfathering clause as written would treat it as grandfathered (already in the range) when it is new (post-gate).
   - **Risk if ignored**: A future Principle VI added to fill the gap could be misclassified as grandfathered rather than subject to the three-criterion gate.

7. **Add cross-reference from VII and IX to XVI's "deliberation run" definition** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: "deliberation run" is defined at L439–443 within XVI's Clarification block. VII (L96–97) and IX (L135–137) use the concept implicitly without a pointer to the definition.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to Principle VII's bullet list: "The definition of a 'deliberation run' (the lifetime of one `objective.yml` artifact) is specified in Principle XVI." Add a parenthetical to IX's "No mutable global state" bullet: "(within a deliberation run as defined by Principle XVI)."
   - **Rationale**: Readers of VII and IX encounter the concept without a definition. The embedded definition in XVI's Clarification (a versioned sub-block within a later principle) is structurally unlikely to be discovered before VII and IX are read.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Readers will infer different run boundaries from VII and IX, leading to inconsistent implementations of what "same inputs" and "loaded once" mean.

8. **Separate multi-agent isolation invariants from operational gotchas in XIX** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: L561–571 conflates multi-agent isolation rules (one agent per output file, context-isolated, phase boundaries are hard barriers) with operational gotchas (re-run overwrite behavior, agent count formulas, baseline features list) under a single "non-extractable core" category.
   - **Proposed change**: Split XIX's bullet list into two categories: **Architectural invariants** (multi-agent rules, phase-level flow summary, dispatch table) and **Operational constants** (re-run overwrite behavior, agent count formulas, baseline features list). Add a note: "Architectural invariants are protected because violating them makes deliberation non-adversarial. Operational constants are protected because they are needed on every invocation path; they may be migrated to a dedicated reference section via the XIX amendment process."
   - **Rationale**: Principle XVII (L496–517) requires execution logic and operational content to be classified separately. Listing agent count formulas (operational) alongside "no meta-agents" (architectural invariant) treats them as the same class of content. They are not.
   - **Risk if ignored**: A future decomposition proposal that attempts to extract "operational gotchas" into a reference file will be blocked by XIX's blanket protection. The protection is appropriate for invariants but excessive for operational constants.

9. **Add a Governance note that the constitution's amendment process covers arbiter-originated principles, not just developer-originated ones** (Priority: P3)
   - **Current state**: The Governance amendment process (L926–929) addresses developer-initiated amendments. It does not acknowledge that conversus's own deliberation artifacts (arbiter rulings) can have constitutional implications.
   - **Proposed change**: Add a sub-clause under "Amendments": "**Arbiter-originated amendments**: when a /conversus deliberation or arbiter ruling reaches a conclusion with constitutional implications (scope extensions, new principle rationale, ordering requirements), the ruling author MUST submit a /speckit.constitution invocation before the principle is published. Arbiter rulings that modify constitutional scope are amendments in substance; the process requirement applies regardless of the ruling's source."
   - **Rationale**: Three existing principles were modified or created via arbiter rulings (XXIV, XXV, XXVII). Making the process explicit retroactively legitimizes these and prospectively prevents silent constitutional drift via deliberation outputs.
   - **Risk if ignored**: The constitution will continue to accumulate arbiter-originated normative content without amendment records, gradually making the Governance section's amendment documentation inaccurate.
