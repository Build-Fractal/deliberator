  **Modified recommendation**: The Governance sub-clause should address both dimensions — prospective process (future arbiter rulings modifying normative principle scope require /speckit.constitution updates before publication) AND retroactive record (the same PR that adds the sub-clause must include Origin note amendments to XXIV L724–727, XXV L774–775, and XXVII L836–839, each explicitly crediting the 2026-04-25 arbiter ruling as the amendment mechanism and noting the absence of a /speckit.constitution invocation at the time as a historical process gap now being retroactively closed). This modification was prompted by numbering-skeptic's cross-review and is correct.

  - **Triggered by**: My own cross-review of numbering-skeptic (Tensions: "Principle II's stable-interfaces expansion: atomicity implication"). The tension I identified: Principle II's stable-interfaces contract states "Changing a stable interface requires updating every consumer... in a single atomic change." If principle numbers are declared stable interfaces under Principle II (numbering-skeptic's Recommendation 3), Principle II's atomicity requirement fires immediately — all consumers of "principle number as stable interface" must be updated in the same PR.
  - **Proposed change**: The synthesis should note that implementing numbering-skeptic's Recommendation 3 (adding principle numbers to Principle II's stable-interfaces list) carries an implicit atomicity dependency: the same PR must contain numbering-skeptic's Recommendations 1 (no-reuse rule), 2 (tombstones for VI and X), 4 (removal checklist), and 6 (grandfathering clause correction), because these are all consumers of the newly declared stable interface. This is implementation sequencing guidance for the synthesis rather than a new constitutional amendment, but the constraint must be surfaced explicitly or the amendment PR structure will instantiate a Principle II violation in the same change that grants principle numbers stable-interface status.
  - **Rationale**: Self-defeating amendments — changes that violate the principle they are trying to protect — are the most damaging class of constitutional error because they pass review (the amendment is facially valid) while producing an incoherent document (the declared contract is immediately violated). The atomicity implication is non-obvious because Principle II's text lists its stable interfaces as content-level artifacts (markers, variable names, dispatch subcommand names), not governance identifiers. A drafter who reads Principle II's list before implementing numbering-skeptic's Recommendation 3 may not notice that extending the list activates the atomicity requirement over the other numbering recommendations.

### Position Summary

Of nine original recommendations, I withdraw none, modify three (Recommendations 2, 4, and 8), and maintain six without change (Recommendations 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, and 9). Two new recommendations emerge from the cross-review process: one substantive (retroactive Origin note amendments for the three existing arbiter-originated modifications, P2), and one structural (the atomicity bundle implication for numbering-skeptic's stable-interface declaration, P3).

The most significant change in my thinking concerns Recommendation 2's temporal scope. In Phase 1, I diagnosed the arbiter-ruling gap as a prospective process problem — the Governance section lacks a pathway for this amendment type — and proposed a forward-looking sub-clause to prevent future occurrences. The numbering-skeptic's cross-review correctly identified this as leaving the three triggering cases (XXIV, XXV, XXVII) without retroactive documentation, creating an asymmetry with the retroactive tombstone approach both reviews endorse for number gaps VI and X. I had failed to apply to my own recommendation the same Principle XI standard I was using to critique the document: if retroactive documentation is warranted for numbering gaps, it is equally warranted for the arbiter-originated modifications that motivated the new sub-clause. Extending Recommendation 2 to include retroactive record-keeping in the same PR is the correct scope, and I credit numbering-skeptic's cross-review for surfacing it. I also acknowledge that my Phase 1 description of Principle VI as the "natural location" for a multi-agent isolation principle was architectural commentary that should have been more carefully framed as non-normative, since it could be read as conflicting with a tombstone-based never-reuse policy.

My remaining highest-priority recommendation is Recommendation 1: repair the IX Extension's cross-domain claim. It received no challenge from any cross-review, survives direct re-inspection, and presents the clearest case of a stated architectural design (domain-specific principles reference IX's framework) being falsified by the document's own structure (XXIII and XXIV do not reference IX, while XXVIII does). The Governance section amendments are collectively more expansive and important as constitutional infrastructure, but Recommendation 1 is the most precisely verifiable internal contradiction: a single paragraph makes a falsifiable claim, the claim is testable by inspection, and the inspection fails. If the synthesis must sequence amendments, Recommendation 1 should land first as a PATCH-class change with no dependencies on Governance machinery, followed by the coordinated Governance MINOR-class PR that incorporates Recommendations 2, 9, and their cross-review-coordinated companions.
