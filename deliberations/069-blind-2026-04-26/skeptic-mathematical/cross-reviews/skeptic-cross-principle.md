# Cross-Review: skeptic-cross-principle by skeptic-mathematical

I read skeptic-cross-principle's review against my own (skeptic-mathematical) and the v2.4.0 target. We are both skeptics of the gate, but our diagnoses converge on different attack surfaces — mine is *logical structure* (orthogonality, proxy validity, calibration), theirs is *corpus consistency* (which grandfathered principles fail the gate, what XI already says). The overlap is large enough to validate both findings; the divergences are sharp enough to require explicit reconciliation before either can be accepted as-is.

### Dangerous Contradictions

1. **Their Recommendation 1 (merge criterion 3 into XI) versus my Recommendation 7 (vocabulary novelty test).** Cross-principle wants criterion 3 *deleted* and absorbed into XI's body as "Principle XI applies recursively to the principles list." My review wants criterion 3 *strengthened* with an operational test (a noun phrase or domain term not present in any existing principle). These are incompatible: if criterion 3 is deleted from the gate, no new vocabulary test can be attached to it, and the constitutional-amendment process loses its only discriminator between "new principle" and "extension of existing principle." Their fix removes the only place to install my fix. The contradiction must be resolved one way: keep criterion 3 with a tightened test, OR fold it into XI with a clear delegation that XI now governs the principle-of-principles distinctness check and inherits the vocabulary test there.

2. **Their Recommendation 10 (eliminate the gate entirely, fold criterion 1 into Principle XII) versus my Recommendation 10 (replace the gate with a structured amendment template).** Both are "kill the gate as a separate construct," but they point in opposite architectural directions. Theirs collapses the gate into existing principles (criterion 1 → XII, criterion 3 → XI, criterion 2 → amendment-process prose). Mine externalizes the gate into a PR template artifact. If both are accepted, the result is incoherent: existing principles silently absorb the gate's content while a separate template also exists. One eliminates structure, the other adds structure. The constitution cannot do both without becoming exactly the kind of duplication XI prohibits.

3. **Their grandfather-as-permanent-inconsistency framing (Missed Opportunities §3, §7) versus my "exemplar/tolerated" classification (Recommendation 4).** Cross-principle treats the grandfather as either a migration backlog with a v3.0.0 audit deadline, or a permanent two-tier system that should be honestly labeled. My review proposes a *third* path: keep the grandfather, but classify each pre-gate principle as "exemplar" (model new amendments on these) or "tolerated" (do not). These are partially compatible but operationally divergent. Theirs migrates the failing principles out; mine keeps them in but marks them as non-exemplary. If both are adopted, there is no clear answer to "what does an author do today when looking at Principle X?" — migrate it, mark it tolerated, or both? Pick one regime.

4. **Their criterion 3 redundancy claim versus my criterion 1-2 collapse claim.** Cross-principle says criterion 3 is the redundant one (overlaps XI). My review says criterion 1 collapses into criterion 2. If both are correct, the gate has *only one* genuinely independent criterion — but we disagree on which one. Cross-principle keeps criterion 1 (mechanical verification) as the load-bearing survivor; I keep criterion 2 (falsifiability) as the parent and demote criterion 1 to a content-artifact requirement. A reader applying both reviews would conclude the gate has zero independent criteria, which is stronger than either of us argued. The constitution cannot resolve this without picking which redundancy is real.

### Tensions

1. **Asymmetric depth on the verification-mechanism prescription.** Cross-principle's Recommendation 2 (replace "concrete enough" with a structured `Verification:` block naming check type, artifact, failure signal) is significantly more concrete than my Recommendation 1 (include a failing test or named CI job with 5-10 line pseudo-code). Theirs is better-engineered as a constitutional artifact. Mine is better-engineered as a content gate. They are not contradictory but they need merging — the `Verification:` block should require a content artifact, not just naming.

2. **Treatment of "interaction with existing principles."** Cross-principle's Missed Opportunities §6 raises principle interaction drift but does not develop it into a recommendation. My Recommendation 5 promotes it to a fourth gate. We agree on the diagnosis (criterion 3 catches composition, not contradiction) but disagree on prominence. If interaction is a fourth gate, the constitution must enumerate compatibility checks; if it stays diagnostic, it remains an unenforced concern.

3. **Severity of the prospective-only design's instability.** Cross-principle's Off-Base Assumption §2 argues the prospective-only design is unstable because amendments-to-grandfathered-principles drag the gate into partial retroactivity. My review notes the same dynamic but treats it as an asymmetric error problem (Recommendation 8). Cross-principle treats it as a structural defect requiring an audit deadline; I treat it as a calibration philosophy. The disagreement is whether the cure is procedural (deadline) or philosophical (stated bias toward operational guidance).

4. **Antipatterns catalog as migration destination.** Cross-principle's Missed Opportunities §6 explicitly proposes the antipatterns catalog as the natural home for migrated VI/X/XVI/XX/XXI. My review does not consider this destination at all. This is a genuine gap in mine — the catalog *is* a better fit for "rules of thumb learned from recurring failures" than CONTRIBUTING.md. Cross-principle is correct here and my Recommendation 4 (exemplar/tolerated tiers) should incorporate the catalog as a third disposition.

5. **Versioning interaction with migrations.** Cross-principle's Missed Opportunities §8 and Recommendation 7 raise the versioning ambiguity (is migration MAJOR or PATCH?). My review does not address this. Cross-principle's resolution (migration = MAJOR, mechanism-only addition = PATCH, extension block = MINOR) is sound and should be folded in. The tension is that this taxonomy assumes the constitution's existing versioning rules are stable, while my review questions whether the gate's enforcement path even exists (so versioning is downstream of a more fundamental enforcement gap).

### Safe Agreements

1. **The "concrete enough to sketch in one paragraph" sub-clause is the gate's weakest point.** Cross-principle's Missed Opportunities §5 and my Executive Summary independently identify the same flaw: the bar is recursive (a human judgment about whether a sketch is concrete) and gameable (fluent prose passes). Both reviews recommend replacing it with a structural artifact requirement (their `Verification:` block, my failing test or named CI job). This is a high-confidence convergence — implement it.

2. **The grandfather clause needs a stated disposition (audit, expiry, or honest acceptance of permanent inconsistency).** Both reviews agree the open-ended grandfather is unstable. Cross-principle frames it as an XI-violation by the gate against itself; I frame it as a calibration problem. Either framing lands on the same prescription: state the policy explicitly (v3.0.0 audit, or labeled permanent exception, or tiered classification). High-confidence convergence on the diagnosis, methods differ.

3. **Specific pre-gate principles are gate-failure candidates.** Both reviews flag VI (Scripts Over Markdown), X (Zen of Python Output), XVI (Mathematical Transparency), XX (Decomposition Mechanism Precedence), XXI (Extraction Ordering) as principles that would be rejected if proposed today. Cross-principle adds XX explicitly; my review groups them as workflow heuristics. Strong agreement on the candidate list — these are the migration/classification targets.

4. **The gate has no specified enforcement path.** Cross-principle's Off-Base Assumption §4 ("no mechanism is specified for *applying* the gate") and my Recommendation 10 (structured amendment template as the operational gate) converge on the same finding: the gate is rhetorical without a `/speckit.constitution` checklist or PR-template enforcement hook. Both reviews want the enforcement path made concrete in the Governance text.
