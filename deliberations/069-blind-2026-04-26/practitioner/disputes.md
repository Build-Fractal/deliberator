# Practitioner Disputes — Phase 4

Iteration 1, Phase 4. Read against the v2.4.0 target plus all three Phase 3 revisions (skeptic-mathematical, skeptic-cross-principle, my own). After Phase 3 a great deal of convergence has emerged; what remains splits cleanly into two-to-four genuine disputes, three-to-five points of real convergence, and a final position statement. As the future-amendment-author lens, I prioritize what an engineer trying to land a real amendment would actually trip over.

---

### Remaining Disputes

**Dispute 1 — Should grandfathered principles be tiered (exemplar / tolerated / migration-candidate) with a v3.0.0 deadline for migration-candidates?**

Skeptic-cross-principle's revised Disposition 4 lands on a tiered classification *now*, with v3.0.0 disposition required for "migration-candidate" only. Skeptic-mathematical withdrew their tiering proposal entirely and now endorses a non-precedential footnote (R-N2) instead. I withdrew my own footnote proposal because both skeptics correctly flagged it as worse than the disease, and I explicitly declined to adopt either the tiered audit or the time-boxed audit ("the grandfathered-precedent problem is real but the remedy needs more thought than a footnote — and probably belongs in a separate amendment, not in this gate").

This is the largest genuinely-unresolved disagreement. Skeptic-cross-principle wants tiering shipped with this amendment; skeptic-mathematical wants a footnote shipped with this amendment; I want neither shipped with this amendment because both pre-litigate the migration spec the constitution itself mandates. As the amendment-author lens, I claim the practical cost of tiering is high: it forces a per-principle judgement on every grandfathered item *as part of this amendment*, expanding the amendment's blast radius from "add a gate" to "audit the corpus." The constitutional discipline cost is also high: tiering a principle as "migration-candidate" without going through a migration spec hands future authors a procedural lever to demote it without the migration spec actually existing. My position: defer this decision to a follow-up amendment whose only job is grandfathering disposition. The current amendment ships the gate prospectively-only and stops there.

**Dispute 2 — Does the amendment introducing the gate require a separate prerequisite amendment establishing the maintainer role (CODEOWNERS or named team), or is citing an existing artifact sufficient?**

Skeptic-cross-principle's N3 lands on "Amendment review requires sign-off from at least one CODEOWNER of `constitution.md`" and treats CODEOWNERS as the existing artifact. Skeptic-mathematical's R-N3 lands on "at least one project maintainer who is not the amendment author" without specifying the artifact. My Recommendation 11 lands on "(a) cite an existing CODEOWNERS file or GitHub team that resolves the ambiguity, or (b) note the enforcer clause is conditional on a follow-up amendment establishing the role."

If a CODEOWNERS file or named team already exists in the repo today, skeptic-cross-principle's framing is sufficient and the dispute disappears. If it does not, my framing is the safer one — declaring the dependency rather than hand-waving past it. As the amendment-author lens, I claim this distinction is load-bearing because the gate's enforcer clause is the single change that converts the gate from rhetoric to operational machinery (Recommendations 1, 2, 4, 5, 12 all hinge on it); shipping that clause against an undefined role is exactly the failure mode the gate exists to prevent (an unverifiable rule whose check does not exist). My position: the amendment text MUST cite the artifact by name. If CODEOWNERS exists, name it. If it does not, the gate's enforcer clause MUST be paired with a follow-up amendment (or a paragraph in this amendment) that establishes it. No phantom roles.

**Dispute 3 — Does the gate retain three criteria (mechanize-and-retain) or does it reduce to a structured PR template that subsumes the criteria into required sections (collapse-into-template)?**

Skeptic-mathematical's promoted Recommendation 10 frames the PR template as the *vehicle* through which Recommendations 1, 3, 5, 8 land — i.e., the criteria persist as PR-template sections, not as standalone gate text. Skeptic-cross-principle's Disposition 6 keeps the gate "as a discrete construct in Governance, mechanized via Recommendation 6's enforcement path" — i.e., the criteria remain in Governance prose and the template is the enforcer. My Recommendations 1 and 5 align with skeptic-cross-principle: criteria remain in Governance, template is the enforcement vehicle.

This dispute is closer to a wording question than a substantive one, but the wording matters for future authors. If the criteria live only in the PR template, future authors who don't open the template don't see the criteria; the criteria become administrative rather than constitutional. If the criteria live in Governance and the template enforces them, future authors reading the constitution see the criteria as constitutional invariants. As the amendment-author lens, I claim Governance prose + template is the right split: the constitution names the invariants, the template enforces them. Skeptic-mathematical's "promote template to P1" framing is correct on enforcement but understates how much load the criteria-in-Governance framing carries for discoverability. My position: criteria stay in Governance with mandated structural blocks (`Verification:`, `Falsifying Example:`, `Existing-Principle-Distinctness:`); the PR template MUST require those blocks be filled. Both surfaces ship together.

### Convergence

**Convergence 1 — The "sketch in one paragraph" sub-clause must be replaced with a structured `Verification:` block.** All three reviewers independently arrived at this fix. Skeptic-mathematical (modified Recommendation 1) wants named artifact location or 5-10 line pseudo-code. Skeptic-cross-principle (kept-strengthened Recommendation 2) wants a structured `Verification:` block with check type + artifact + failure signal, deferrable only with a tracking-issue/spec citation. My Recommendation 2 (kept, refined) adopted skeptic-cross-principle's framing as a strict superset of mine. The `Verification:` block is the convergent fix.

**Convergence 2 — The gate's missing enforcement path is the single largest defect, and the fix is a PR template paired with named human review.** Three reviewers independently arrived at this finding. Skeptic-mathematical promoted their template recommendation from P3 to P1 and added R-N3 (named human reviewer). Skeptic-cross-principle's Disposition 6 keeps the enforcement-path recommendation and adds N3 (CODEOWNERS-grounded reviewer). My Recommendations 1, 5, and 12 land on the same two-keys requirement (template AND human reviewer, as a non-separable bundle). Without enforcement, the gate is rhetorical. With enforcement-by-template-only, the gate is bot rubber-stamping. With enforcement-by-reviewer-only, the gate is human rubber-stamping. The bundle is load-bearing.

**Convergence 3 — Worked rejection examples are required calibration; one is insufficient.** Skeptic-mathematical's modified Recommendation 3 expanded from one to two examples (mechanical-falsifiability rejection + distinctness/composition rejection) and added R-N1 (interaction-conflict example). My expanded Recommendation 4 reached the same conclusion (clean rejection + accepted scope-extension precedent). Skeptic-cross-principle's N1 (`Existing-Principle-Distinctness:` block) provides the structural counterpart that the worked examples calibrate. The convergent answer: the constitution ships at least two worked examples — one rejection demonstrating what fails the gate, one acceptance demonstrating what passes despite scope-extension — and the PR template requires the equivalent block from amendment authors.

**Convergence 4 — Aggressive simplification of the gate's three criteria has been correctly defeated.** Skeptic-mathematical withdrew their criteria-1+2 collapse. Skeptic-cross-principle withdrew their "remove the gate entirely, fold criterion 1 into XII" alternative. I withdrew my "remove Criterion 1" recommendation. All three of us now agree that Criterion 1 is the gate's load-bearing artifact-producing requirement, and removing it (whether by merging into Criterion 2, folding into XII, or collapsing into template-only) inverts the gate's value proposition. The gate ships with three criteria intact, with Criterion 1 strengthened by the `Verification:` block.

**Convergence 5 — The "linter SHOULD eventually" hedge in XII / XIII / XXII §1 is debt that should not propagate, but retroactive cleanup is out of scope for this amendment.** Skeptic-mathematical withdrew their auto-reclassification deadline. Skeptic-cross-principle's Disposition 4 narrowed retroactive treatment to "migration-candidate" only. My Recommendation 8 (kept) bounds future use of the hedge to require a tracking-spec citation, prospective only. All three reviewers agree the hedge is a debt sink and that prospective discipline is the right scope; retroactive application is either deferred (my and skeptic-mathematical's view) or narrowed to a small explicit list (skeptic-cross-principle's view). The convergent floor: future amendments using "linter SHOULD eventually" MUST cite a tracking spec; existing principles are not retroactively required to ship the linter via this amendment.

---

### Final Position Statement

#### Non-Negotiables

**Non-Negotiable 1 — Enforcement and template ship together as a non-separable bundle (Recommendations 1 + 5 + 12).** A PR template without a named human reviewer is bot rubber-stamping; a named human reviewer without a structured PR template is human rubber-stamping. Each one alone produces the appearance of governance without the substance and consumes political capital for no net gain. The amendment introducing this gate ships both at once or ships neither. The constitution text MUST explicitly note the pairing as a requirement (not a recommendation) for the gate to be considered enforced. This is convergent across all three reviewers and is the single highest-leverage change.

**Non-Negotiable 2 — Criterion 1 stays, and stays load-bearing, via the structured `Verification:` block.** Three reviewers converged on this after independently considering and rejecting variants that would have removed it (collapse into Criterion 2, fold into XII, delete entirely). Criterion 1 is the only criterion that demands a content artifact rather than self-certified wording. The `Verification:` block (check type + artifact + failure signal, deferrable only with tracking-spec citation) is the operational expression. Without this, the gate's value proposition inverts.

**Non-Negotiable 3 — The maintainer role MUST be cited by name, not hand-waved.** The enforcer clause depends on a role the constitution does not currently establish. Either the amendment cites an existing CODEOWNERS file or named GitHub team, or it pairs the enforcer clause with a follow-up amendment that establishes the role. No phantom roles. This is the procedural counterpart to Non-Negotiable 1: shipping enforcement against an undefined enforcer is the same failure mode as shipping a principle whose check does not exist.

#### Flexibility

**Flexibility 1 — On grandfathering disposition, I will accept skeptic-mathematical's footnote (R-N2) if the amendment owner judges the corpus-coherence cost too high to defer, but I prefer deferring the entire disposition to a follow-up amendment.** My strict preference is: this amendment ships the gate prospectively, full stop, and a separate amendment handles grandfathering disposition. If the maintainer judges the corpus-coherence cost too high to defer, skeptic-mathematical's footnote (warning future authors which existing principles to model on, without demoting any principle's precedential force) is acceptable. I will NOT accept skeptic-cross-principle's tiered classification (exemplar / tolerated / migration-candidate) shipped as part of this amendment because it pre-litigates migration without the migration spec.

**Flexibility 2 — On where the criteria live structurally (Governance prose vs. PR template sections), I prefer Governance prose with mandated structural blocks but will accept skeptic-mathematical's "criteria-as-template-sections" framing if paired with a Governance summary that names the invariants.** The discoverability cost of moving the criteria entirely into the template is real but bounded if Governance retains a one-paragraph summary pointing to the template. My preference is criteria-in-Governance with template-enforcement; my acceptable fallback is criteria-as-template-sections with Governance summary.

**Flexibility 3 — On worked-example count, the floor is two (one clean rejection demonstrating gate failure, one accepted scope-extension demonstrating scope-extension is permissible). I will accept three (adding skeptic-mathematical's R-N1 interaction-conflict example) if the amendment owner judges the calibration value worth the prose budget.** Two examples are non-negotiable; the third is a flexibility lever. The interaction-conflict example does work the other two cannot (cross-principle compatibility rather than intra-principle quality), but it expands the amendment's prose footprint meaningfully. I defer to the amendment owner on whether to ship two or three.

---

Net position: **mechanize the gate as a three-criterion structure with `Verification:`, falsifying-example, and `Existing-Principle-Distinctness:` blocks; ship enforcement (PR template + named CODEOWNER review) as a non-separable bundle; cite the maintainer role by existing artifact or pair with a prerequisite amendment; defer grandfathering disposition to a follow-up amendment unless the maintainer accepts skeptic-mathematical's non-precedential footnote.** The three convergent findings (enforcement, `Verification:` block, two worked examples) are the gate's operational floor. The remaining disputes are about scope (what ships with this amendment vs. a follow-up), not about whether the floor is correct.
