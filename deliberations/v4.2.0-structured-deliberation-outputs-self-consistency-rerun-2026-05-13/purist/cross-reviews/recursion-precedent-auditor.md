### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Bootstrap accommodation constitutional pathway**
  - **recursion-precedent-auditor claims**: Focus on technical containment mechanisms and anti-precedent language (Recommendation 1 Modified: "combine technical conditions with categorical prohibitions") and precedent-enforcement via citation requirements (Recommendation 3 Surviving).
  - **purist claims**: "Replace the temporal accommodation with a **one-time bootstrap exception** that explicitly requires constitutional amendment process for any future accommodation" (Recommendation 1 Modified) and new recommendation for "Bootstrap exception constitutional amendment requirement."
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Creates two incompatible enforcement regimes. Technical containment assumes the accommodation can be managed through specification language, while constitutional amendment requirement treats it as a precedent requiring formal constitutional process. If both were implemented, future similar situations would face unclear pathways — technical conditions or constitutional amendment?
  - **Suggested resolution**: Recursion-precedent-auditor should acknowledge that technical conditions alone cannot prevent constitutional precedent creation. Constitutional discipline requires formal pathways for accommodations from universal principles, even when technically contained.

- **Accommodation doctrinal classification**
  - **recursion-precedent-auditor claims**: "scope clarification, not relief" (Recommendation 2 assessment, Position Summary) — the temporal-constraint accommodation is scope clarification because strict reading is logically impossible.
  - **purist claims**: Withdrawn my demand for "relief categorization" but maintain the accommodation "must be constitutionally contained" (Recommendation 6 Withdrawn explanation) and requires formal constitutional amendment process (new Bootstrap recommendation).
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Scope clarification and constitutional exception have different precedential weight. Scope clarification can be invoked more casually in future amendments; constitutional exceptions require formal process. This classification determines how future bootstrap scenarios are handled.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Recursion-precedent-auditor should recognize that even scope clarifications involving universal principles require constitutional process when they create accommodations. The bootstrap paradox may be logical necessity, but accommodating it still requires formal constitutional pathway.

- **Schema versioning stability discipline**
  - **recursion-precedent-auditor claims**: Accepted RC phases as "implementation flexibility" (Recommendation 2 disposition in their cross-review).
  - **purist claims**: "RC phases remain implementation convenience that undermines constitutional clarity. The purist position demands that stable interfaces launch stable, without escape valves for 'ergonomic refinement'" (Recommendation 2 Surviving).
  - **Why this is dangerous**: RC phases either do or do not provide stability guarantees. If they provide stability, calling them "RC" is misleading. If they don't provide stability, launching with RC phases violates the constitutional clarity principle. These positions cannot coexist in the same implementation.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Recursion-precedent-auditor should acknowledge that constitutional interfaces require constitutional clarity from day one. Implementation convenience cannot override doctrinal clarity when schema versioning is itself a constitutional mandate.

### Tensions

- **Constitutional amendment formalism vs technical precision**
  - **recursion-precedent-auditor's position**: Emphasizes specific technical conditions and anti-precedent language as primary containment (Recommendation 1 Modified, Recommendation 5 Surviving on schema infrastructure boundaries).
  - **purist's position**: Emphasizes formal constitutional amendment process as primary discipline (new Bootstrap recommendation, modified Recommendation 5 combining technical conditions with general criteria).
  - **Nature of tension**: Technical precision focuses on preventing misuse through specification language; constitutional formalism focuses on preventing precedent creation through procedural discipline. Both are valid protective mechanisms but operate at different levels.
  - **Coordination needed**: Combine both approaches — technical precision for immediate containment, constitutional process for precedential protection. Technical conditions address "how to contain this specific case," constitutional process addresses "how to prevent casual invocation of similar accommodations."

- **Analysis definitiveness vs systematic verification**
  - **recursion-precedent-auditor's position**: Supports systematic constitutional citation and verification approaches (references to strict-reader's systematic verification in Recommendation 2 assessment).
  - **purist's position**: Demands "binary definitiveness" combined with systematic citation but insists on definitive rather than hedge-language conclusions (Recommendation 8 Modified).
  - **Nature of tension**: Systematic verification can lead to nuanced conclusions; binary definitiveness demands clear pass/fail determinations. Both serve constitutional clarity but through different analytical approaches.
  - **Coordination needed**: Systematic analysis that culminates in definitive binary determinations. The analysis can be comprehensive and citation-heavy while the conclusions remain unambiguous.

- **Rollout complexity vs constitutional simplicity**
  - **recursion-precedent-auditor's position**: Accepted current four-tier rollout structure as addressing implementation concerns raised in cross-reviews.
  - **purist's position**: "Compress the rollout to **two phases maximum**: (1) parallel-format grace period of 90 days, (2) JSON-only enforcement" (Recommendation 3 Modified).
  - **Nature of tension**: Implementation pragmatism suggests graceful migration; constitutional clarity suggests minimal transition periods. Extended rollouts risk becoming permanent dual-format support.
  - **Coordination needed**: Acknowledge that some migration window may be necessary but establish firm boundaries. The four-tier rollout may be engineering-sound but constitutionally excessive.

- **Precedent documentation vs precedent prevention**
  - **recursion-precedent-auditor's position**: Strong emphasis on citation requirements and antipattern catalog entries (Recommendations 3, 4, 7 all Surviving).
  - **purist's position**: Emphasis on preventing precedent creation through formal process (new Bootstrap recommendation) rather than documenting precedent patterns.
  - **Nature of tension**: Documentation assumes precedents will be created and focuses on making them traceable; prevention assumes precedents can be avoided through procedural discipline. Both serve constitutional stability through different mechanisms.
  - **Coordination needed**: Combine prevention-first approach with documentation backup. Primary discipline should prevent casual precedent creation; secondary discipline should document unavoidable precedents for future reference.
