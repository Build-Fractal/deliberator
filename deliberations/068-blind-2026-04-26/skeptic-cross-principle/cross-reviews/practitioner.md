# skeptic-cross-principle reviewing practitioner

The practitioner reviewed XVI from the perspective of "developer about to ship a PR." I audited the same principle for cross-principle conflicts. Where my review tested whether XVI conflicts with the rest of the constitution, theirs tested whether XVI is enforceable in code review. The two views agree on the diagnosis far more than they disagree — but the practitioner pushes harder toward removal where I leaned toward merger, and that is where the disagreement matters.

### Dangerous Contradictions

1. **The practitioner's "remove XVI entirely" recommendation (rec #7) clashes with my "merge as VII extension" recommendation in a way that matters for governance.** If the deliberation accepts removal, the parameter-pinning rule becomes a sub-bullet of VII. If it accepts merger, XVI survives as a named extension. These have different downstream consequences: a sub-bullet under VII inherits VII's "non-negotiable" framing (which my review flagged as already overstated), while a named extension preserves the carve-out signal. The practitioner does not engage with my concern that VII's "non-negotiable" claim is the load-bearing bug — they treat XVI as the problem to delete, when my read is that VII's absolutism is the root cause that XVI patches awkwardly. **If both reviews are accepted at face value, we delete XVI without fixing VII, leaving VII still claiming "deterministic orchestration is non-negotiable" with no surviving carve-out for stochastic LLM steps.** That is strictly worse than the status quo.

2. **The practitioner says the solver-swap invariant (lines 466–467) is "useful" and "real, enforceable" (their alignment #2). My review #2 (Off-Base Assumptions) called it conflated and undeliverable — two solvers cannot produce bit-identical numerical results.** This is a direct factual disagreement. The practitioner reads "MUST NOT change what is being optimized" as an architectural rule about the objective function as contract; I read it as a promise solvers cannot keep. Both readings are textually defensible because the principle does not distinguish "what is being optimized" (the function) from "the optimization result" (the numerical answer). A deliberation that ratifies the practitioner's framing without my qualification leaves the constitution promising solver-substitution equivalence that real solvers will violate, and the first AMPL-vs-nashopt comparison will surface a "constitutional violation" that is actually a numerical-methods inevitability.

3. **The practitioner's "move plain-language rule into Principle X" (rec #5) is in direct tension with my "fold into IV (Documentation Is the Product)" framing (my Alignment #4).** Both are plausible homes; both are NOT the same home. X is about output readability (Zen-of-Python framing), IV is about specification text being the implementation. The plain-language gloss requirement could land in either, but it should not be silently assigned to one without acknowledging the other. If the deliberation merges practitioner rec #5 without considering my IV framing, we have committed to "plain language is an output-formatting concern" rather than "plain language is part of the spec product surface." Those are different contracts with different enforcement points (a linter on output strings vs. a review checklist on template authorship).

4. **The practitioner explicitly does not engage with the V (Observable Deliberation) interaction I flagged (my Missed Opportunity #5).** Their review is silent on whether pinned parameter values must appear in deliberation output. They treat XVI as a developer-PR-gate concern; I treat it as a user-audit concern. **If the deliberation adopts the practitioner's "collapse XVI to 4–6 lines on parameter-pinning" without my V-interaction clause, the resulting principle is enforceable in CI but invisible in run output — users cannot see what got pinned.** That fails the "Mathematical Transparency" name on its own terms even after the rewrite.

### Tensions

1. **Disagreement on what the principle is FOR.** The practitioner frames XVI as a developer-facing PR gate ("is what I'm about to do compliant?"). I framed it as a constitutional invariant against cross-principle drift. Both framings are legitimate, but they pull the rewrite in different directions: the practitioner wants operational language a reviewer can mechanically check; I want the principle to acknowledge VII, V, and XXIV explicitly. A rewrite optimized only for the practitioner's frame produces a tight CI-checkable rule but leaves the principle structurally orphaned from the rest of the constitution.

2. **Naming convergence with different motivations.** The practitioner suggests renaming to "Pinned Parameter Discipline" or "Optimization Reproducibility Contract" (their rec #8). I did not propose a rename but argued the merger candidate is VII-extension. The practitioner's renames preserve XVI as a top-level principle; my merger eliminates it. We agree the current name is broad/wrong but disagree on the structural fix. A rename without the merger risks the practitioner's own concern (scope creep) recurring under a narrower name within two amendment cycles.

3. **The "clarification block is a tell" argument (their Missed Opportunity #3) is one I share but draw a different conclusion from.** They read the clarification as evidence of bad first-pass wording requiring rewrite. I read it as evidence of post-hoc patching against a VII conflict that was never structurally resolved. Same observation, different remediation: theirs is "rewrite XVI"; mine is "rewrite VII to acknowledge XVI exists." If the deliberation only rewrites XVI, the clarification block disappears but the underlying VII-vs-XVI determinism contradiction remains, just better hidden.

4. **The practitioner's rec #4 (verification mechanism / contract test) only partially overlaps my rec #6 (V-emission requirement).** They want a byte-identical assembly test (mirroring XXIV). I want pinned values emitted in deliberation output. Both are enforcement gates but they prove different properties: their test proves mechanical assembly is deterministic; my emission proves the user can audit the pinned set. A constitution adopting only their test gate could pass CI on a pipeline whose pinned values are never observable to the user.

5. **The practitioner's claim that "Within-run determinism" terminology is "academic" and "obscures" the rule (their Missed Opportunity #2) directly contradicts my Missed Opportunity #4, where I argued the term is undefined relative to VII's "run" but is the right concept once defined.** They want the term replaced with operational language. I want the term harmonized with VII's vocabulary. These are not the same fix: replacement removes a useful concept; harmonization keeps the concept but makes it cross-referenceable. A rewrite collapsing both reviews would risk losing the within-run/cross-run distinction entirely, which is XVI's actual technical contribution.

### Safe Agreements

1. **Both reviews independently identify the 3-stage pipeline description as belonging in spec 013, not the constitution.** The practitioner's rec #1 ("drop the 3-stage pipeline breakdown") and my Off-Base Assumption #4 ("XVI assumes the 3-stage pipeline is the only architecture worth constitutionalizing") converge on the same fix from different routes. This is high-confidence guidance for the deliberation: the pipeline description leaves XVI regardless of whether XVI is collapsed, merged, or removed.

2. **Both reviews identify VII↔XVI as the load-bearing tension.** The practitioner's Off-Base Assumption #3 (cross-run reproducibility ambiguity vs. VII's same-config-same-output promise) and my Missed Opportunities #1–#2 (no cross-reference in either direction) name the same gap. Whichever rewrite path the deliberation takes, this cross-reference must be added. This is the safest single edit to the constitution: VII gets a back-reference acknowledging XVI's bounded LLM exception, regardless of XVI's eventual structural fate.

3. **Both reviews identify the "Mathematical Transparency" name as too broad and inviting scope creep.** The practitioner's Missed Opportunity #4 and my structural critique both note the name does not match the content. This is a low-risk ratification target: rename or eliminate the heading, regardless of disposition.

4. **Both reviews independently conclude that the principle as written is doing too many jobs at once.** Practitioner: "It mixes three different things — a user-facing UX promise, an architectural invariant, and an internal pipeline description." Me: "XVI grants itself two privileges that no other principle in the constitution enjoys." Different decompositions, same diagnosis: XVI is not one principle. Whatever the deliberation does next, the multi-job nature of XVI is the structural defect both reviewers can attest to without re-litigating their respective recommendations.

---

**Net read on practitioner's review**: stronger than mine on operational enforceability; weaker on cross-principle structural debt. The practitioner is right that XVI's only mechanically checkable rule is parameter-pinning, and right that the rest is documentation that drifted into a constitution. They are insufficiently alarmed that even the rewrite they propose leaves VII's absolutism standing — which means the next stochastic-LLM principle will need its own clarification block bolted on, recreating the same defect. My recommendation to the deliberation: take the practitioner's collapse on XVI's bulk content, take my back-reference fix on VII, do not adopt removal without the VII patch, and treat the solver-swap claim as factually under-specified rather than ready to ship.
