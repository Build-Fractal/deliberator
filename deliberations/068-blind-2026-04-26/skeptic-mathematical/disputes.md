### Remaining Disputes

#### Dispute: Standalone-XVI vs decomposition endpoint

- **My claim**: After Phase 3, the analytic decomposition of XVI's content along the transparency / pinning / pipeline-narration axes should result in (a) the pipeline narration physically relocating to spec 013, (b) the pinning fragment expressed as a single inline operational sentence cross-referenced to VII, and (c) the transparency fragment surviving as the residual XVI prose. The structural endpoint is "XVI shrinks but remains a numbered principle"; decomposition is the analytic move, not the output.
- **Opposing position(s)**:
  - Skeptic-cross-principle's revision (New Recommendation 1, "Sequence the disposition: split → relocate → cross-reference → absorb") still terminates in fragment-level absorption: "fold the transparency fragment into IV; fold the pinning fragment into VII as a named extension OR keep it freestanding." The revision's Recommendation 10 disposition ("WITHDRAW as standalone, FOLD into Recommendation 1") explicitly contemplates an endpoint where XVI has no surviving constitutional surface — "specs get redirected to the new homes."
  - Practitioner's revision Original Rec 1 ("KEEP, slightly revised") preserves their preference for "tight standalone XVI" but explicitly defers to the deliberation: "I'll defer to the deliberation if the room prefers a split, but I'm not retracting the collapse direction." This leaves the structural endpoint genuinely contested rather than resolved.
- **Why I will not concede**: Two independent reasons. First, the spec-redirect cleanup that absorption requires (specs 012-019 currently reference "Principle XVI") is constitutionally non-trivial — every consuming spec must be re-read, the references rewritten, and the rewrites verified. Skeptic-cross-principle's framing treats this as a mechanical cleanup ("redirected to the new homes") but it is an XII-anti-pattern hazard at scale. Second, my own withdrawal of original Recommendation 10 was predicated on the practitioner's Dangerous Contradiction #1 — that XVI's clarification block contains the *one* operationally crisp gate against re-resolving parameters mid-deliberation. Absorbing the pinning fragment into VII as a "named extension" puts that gate inside a principle whose lead sentence is "Deterministic orchestration is non-negotiable," which dilutes the gate's visibility for the most likely reader (a PR author skim-reading VII for the deterministic-orchestration absolutism, not for an embedded LLM-mediated exception clause).
- **Counter-argument**: Skeptic-cross-principle's strongest move is that XVI's name itself is load-bearing — "Mathematical Transparency" is broad enough that its breadth *enabled* the bundling defect we are correcting. Their New Recommendation 3 makes this explicit. If the surviving residual is only the transparency fragment, the name is fine; if the pinning fragment also survives, the name oversells. The honest fix is rename (their position) rather than narrow-but-keep-name (mine). I concede the name is a remediation target. What I do not concede is that the rename forces absorption — XVI can be renamed and shrunk without being dissolved.
- **Proposed resolution path**: Synthesizer adopts a three-tier endpoint. Tier 1 (mandatory regardless of structural choice): bilateral VII↔XVI cross-reference, V-emission requirement for pinned values, narrowed XXIV interaction scoped to synthesis-verdict consumers, solver-substitution wording softening. Tier 2 (structural choice, room picks): either (a) shrink XVI to transparency-residual and relocate pipeline + pinning to VII / spec 013 with explicit spec-reference rewrite tasks, or (b) keep XVI as a tightened standalone with the pinning sentence inline. Tier 3 (deferred): rename of XVI is a follow-up PR after structural choice lands and the residual content is known. Synthesizer should not split-the-baby on Tier 2; pick one path and own it. My preference remains tightened-standalone (option b), but the dispute is genuinely live and the room should not pretend it is resolved.

#### Dispute: Test-contract sequencing — same-PR mandate vs aspirational-until-test

- **My claim** (revised Recommendation 3): "Parameter resolution discipline MUST be enforced by a test that demonstrably fails if a future PR re-resolves parameters mid-deliberation. Until that test exists, this principle is aspirational; first PR adding the test removes the aspirational caveat." This framing acknowledges current unenforceability honestly without falsely citing patterns from elsewhere or pointing at fictional test files.
- **Opposing position(s)**:
  - Practitioner's revised Original Rec 4 ("KEEP, sequenced") rejects the aspirational framing: "the constitutional principle MUST point at a test contract, but the amendment that adds the principle MUST land in the same PR as the test, not in a future PR. Otherwise we ship a constitutional pointer to a missing artifact." Their explicit restatement: "The PR amending XVI MUST also add `tests/test_parameter_pinning.py::test_no_within_run_drift` (or equivalent name) and the principle text MUST reference the test by path."
  - Skeptic-cross-principle's revised Recommendation 5 takes a third position: "XVI MUST reference a contract test in spec 013 that asserts no within-run drift. The cache key schema itself stays in spec 013, not constitutional prose." This routes the test contract through spec 013 rather than landing it in the constitutional amendment PR directly.
- **Why I will not concede**: Practitioner's same-PR mandate is operationally clean but risks a different failure mode — it conflates the *constitutional amendment* PR (governance-weighted, requires deliberation) with the *test-implementation* PR (engineering-weighted, requires test design). Bundling these creates a PR that needs both a quorum sign-off on the constitutional change AND a code review on the test, with the failure mode that disagreement on the test design blocks the constitutional clarification. The aspirational framing decouples them: the constitutional clarification ships now (closing the wording defect) and the test ships in a follow-up PR (closing the enforcement defect). Skeptic-cross-principle's spec-013 routing is reasonable but it doesn't solve the same-PR vs aspirational tension — it just relocates where the test lives.
- **Counter-argument**: Practitioner's strongest point is that "constitutional pointer to a missing artifact" is a pattern the constitution explicitly prohibits elsewhere (XII, "no dead infrastructure"). If XVI text says "enforced by `tests/test_parameter_pinning.py`" and the test doesn't exist, that's a textbook XII violation regardless of the aspirational caveat. The aspirational caveat is itself a tell that the pointer is a false promise. I concede that part of the concern is real. What I don't concede is that bundling fixes it: the test design has its own surface area (what counts as "drift," what scope of parameters the test exercises, what the assertion mechanism is) and the deliberation we're closing did not engage with any of that.
- **Proposed resolution path**: Synthesizer chooses between three operational shapes. Shape A (practitioner's): same-PR mandate, constitutional amendment blocks until test design is settled. Shape B (mine): aspirational caveat, two-PR sequence, accept the temporary XII-tension with an explicit follow-up obligation. Shape C (skeptic-cross-principle's): test contract referenced *via spec 013*, so the constitutional text says "see spec 013's parameter-pinning contract test" and spec 013 is the artifact whose existence is required, not a specific test file path. Shape C is the most internally consistent with the constitution's existing spec-vs-implementation abstraction layer — the constitution already routes through spec 013 for cache-contract content, so routing the test contract through the same indirection is consistent. My recommended resolution: Shape C, with the synthesizer noting that the spec-013 contract test must exist before the constitutional amendment merges. This converges practitioner's same-PR mandate with my decoupling concern by making the dependency *spec 013 ships first*, not *test ships in same PR as constitution*.

### Convergence

#### Converged: VII↔XVI bilateral cross-reference is the highest-leverage edit

- **Shared position**: VII's "Deterministic orchestration is non-negotiable" framing and XVI's stochastic LLM-call admission cannot both stand without explicit mutual acknowledgement. The fix is a bilateral edit: VII gains a single sentence acknowledging XVI as the sanctioned exception scoped to LLM-mediated parameter resolution under within-run pinning discipline; XVI opens with "Subject to Principle VII, …" or equivalent.
- **Agreeing agents**: skeptic-mathematical (revised Rec 2), skeptic-cross-principle (revised Rec 3, strengthened), practitioner (revised Rec 10, expanded). All three reviewers identified this as a Safe Agreement in cross-review.
- **Strength**: Unanimous.
- **Path to convergence**: All three Phase 1 reviews independently flagged the VII/XVI tension; cross-reviews surfaced that any merge / split / shrink path *requires* the bilateral cross-reference regardless of structural disposition; Phase 3 revisions strengthened rather than weakened this position. No reviewer retreated. The mandatory wording is functionally agreed: the cross-reference is non-optional.

#### Converged: Solver-substitution wording softening from numerical to semantic

- **Shared position**: Replace XVI's "Changing solvers MUST NOT change what is being optimized" with "Changing solvers MUST NOT change the objective function or its parameter semantics. Numerical results may differ within solver-tolerance bounds; the contract is on the function, not the answer."
- **Agreeing agents**: skeptic-cross-principle (Original Rec 8, "KEEP, no change"), skeptic-mathematical (New Recommendation B, surfaced explicitly in Phase 3 revision), practitioner (New Rec 12, adopted from skeptic-cross-principle).
- **Strength**: Unanimous.
- **Path to convergence**: Skeptic-cross-principle proposed the softening in Phase 1; skeptic-mathematical's Phase 2 cross-review conceded "the conservative reading should win"; practitioner's Phase 3 revision explicitly adopted skeptic-cross-principle's wording. Three independent perspectives converged on the same operational gate (objective function as stable interface) without overpromising bit-identical numerical output two solvers cannot deliver.

#### Converged: Pinning is a term-of-art that needs a constitutional home

- **Shared position**: "Pinning" cannot remain a bare term in XVI without a definition somewhere in the stable-interface vocabulary. The definition ("a parameter value committed to a run-scoped store, immutable for the duration of the run, with invalidation policy specified in spec 013") belongs in II's stable-interface section, not in XVI's prose, so XVI can reference it without re-defining it.
- **Agreeing agents**: skeptic-cross-principle (revised Rec 5), skeptic-mathematical (New Recommendation A, surfaced in Phase 3 revision after Phase 2 cross-review concession). Practitioner did not contest this position in revision.
- **Strength**: Bilateral with practitioner non-objection (effectively unanimous).
- **Path to convergence**: Skeptic-cross-principle's Phase 1 review surfaced "pinning" as undefined; skeptic-mathematical's Phase 2 cross-review pushed back on the cache-contract overreach but conceded the underlying terminology gap; the Phase 3 compromise puts the *definition* in II (term-of-art) and the *cache contract* in spec 013 (implementation), preserving the abstraction boundary both reviewers care about.

#### Converged: Plain-language clause for plugin output is XVI's strongest content

"Equilibrium quality: 0.87" is insufficient; "87% of agents are at their best possible position given others' positions" is required. This clause has no clean home elsewhere in the constitution and survives any structural disposition of XVI.
- **Agreeing agents**: skeptic-mathematical (Rec 9 surviving), skeptic-cross-principle (Phase 1 Safe Agreement), practitioner (Original Rec 5 withdrawn explicitly to preserve this clause inside XVI).
- **Strength**: Unanimous.
- **Path to convergence**: All three Phase 1 reviews independently flagged the plain-language clause as XVI's most operationally crisp content. ### Final Position Statement

#### Non-Negotiables

1. **Bilateral VII↔XVI cross-reference with template-version precondition in VII**. This is non-negotiable because three independent reviewers converged on it as the highest-leverage edit, and because failing to make this edit propagates the contradiction regardless of any other structural disposition. VII as written silently breaks the moment any spec author edits a template, and XVI as written has a stochastic-LLM admission inside a constitution that elsewhere declares determinism non-negotiable. One bilateral edit closes both defects. Recommendation: synthesizer treats this as the floor — no Phase 4 outcome is acceptable that does not include this edit, regardless of whether XVI is shrunk, renamed, or absorbed.

2. **V-emission of pinned parameter values as deliberation output**. This is non-negotiable because XVI's transparency claim is hollow if pinned values are enforceable in CI but invisible in run output — the user cannot audit what their objective function was assembled from. Skeptic-cross-principle's Phase 1 review surfaced this; my own Phase 3 revision conceded I had under-weighted V; practitioner's New Rec 11 explicitly adopted it. The clause ("Pinned parameter values MUST be emitted as deliberation output under V's contract") closes the audit gap and operationalizes the transparency promise. Without it, "users understand what is being optimized" reduces to a slogan.

3. **Plain-language clause for plugin numerical output**. Non-negotiable because all three reviewers identified it as XVI's strongest content with no clean home elsewhere in the constitution. The clause catches "developer ships `Equilibrium quality: 0.87` with no gloss" and is the single concrete user-facing transparency gate XVI provides. Removing or relocating it loses the only operationally falsifiable transparency rule we have. Recommendation: regardless of XVI's structural disposition, this clause survives in some constitutionally numbered location (preferably XVI; secondarily IV if absorbed).

#### Flexibility

1. **Structural disposition of XVI (shrink-standalone vs absorb-and-redirect)**. Flexible. My Phase 3 preference is tightened-standalone with pipeline narration relocated to spec 013 and pinning expressed as one inline sentence cross-referenced to VII. Skeptic-cross-principle's preference is sequenced absorb (split → relocate → cross-reference → absorb fragments). Practitioner's preference is collapsed-standalone. What must be preserved across all three options: the plain-language clause survives, the bilateral VII↔XVI cross-reference lands, the V-emission clause lands, the solver-substitution wording is softened, and downstream specs 012-019 retain valid references (either to surviving XVI or to the new homes their content was absorbed into). What is flexible: whether XVI keeps its current name, whether the pinning fragment lives in XVI or in VII as a named extension, whether the pipeline narration lives in spec 013 or is dropped entirely.

2. **Test-contract sequencing (same-PR vs aspirational vs spec-013-routed)**. Flexible. My Phase 3 preference is the aspirational framing (constitutional clarification ships now, test ships in follow-up PR). Practitioner's preference is same-PR mandate. Skeptic-cross-principle's preference is spec-013 routing. What must be preserved: the constitutional text references a verification anchor (test contract or spec-013 contract), and the verification anchor is real (not pointing at fictional test files). What is flexible: which PR ships the test, where the test physically lives, and whether the aspirational caveat is needed during a transition window.

3. **Audience disambiguation for "transparency"**. Flexible. The dispute over whether the audience is end-user, spec-author, or both was resolved in Phase 3 — both audiences are retained, with template legibility (spec-author concern) addressed by the existing template-documentation clause and runtime output legibility (end-user concern) addressed by the plain-language clause. What must be preserved: both audiences explicitly named, neither silently dropped. What is flexible: whether the audience clause sits in XVI prose (my Phase 3 position), in IV's specification-as-implementation framing (skeptic-cross-principle's New Rec 2), or in a cross-reference between the two. The constitutional weight is in *naming both audiences*, not in choosing the prose home.
