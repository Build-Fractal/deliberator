### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Fundamental response to audit methodology**
  - **practitioner claims**: "defer this entire effort until there's evidence that the grandfathered principles are actually causing problems in practice" (Executive Summary)
  - **gate-strictness-skeptic claims**: "all three principles can pass the gate with reasonable interpretation of the criteria" and "proceed with the audit but apply it less strictly" (Executive Summary and recommendation 1)
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If practitioner's position is adopted, the audit stops and principles remain grandfathered indefinitely. If gate-strictness-skeptic's position is adopted, the audit continues but with relaxed interpretation. These are mutually exclusive approaches that cannot be implemented simultaneously—one requires halting the process, the other requires continuing with different criteria.
  - **Suggested resolution**: practitioner should yield on the "defer entirely" position but maintain their evidence requirements as additional criteria for the audit. The audit can proceed with both stricter evidence requirements AND more careful gate interpretation, combining both perspectives' strengths.

- **Principle XVI disposition strategy**
  - **practitioner claims**: supports "Option A preference for XVI" from the spec—refactoring in place to fix gate compliance while preserving constitutional status (Alignment section, L9)
  - **gate-strictness-skeptic claims**: "Reclassify Principle XVI as PASS" with "Change verdict to PASS based on structural substrate passing 2 of 3 criteria" (recommendation 1)
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Option A requires constitutional amendment to rewrite XVI's headline, while reclassification requires no changes. If both positions drive implementation, we get conflicting instructions about whether XVI needs textual changes or not. Implementation teams cannot simultaneously rewrite and leave unchanged.
  - **Suggested resolution**: gate-strictness-skeptic should examine whether their "structural substrate already passes" analysis actually supports practitioner's "refactor the headline" approach, since both recognize XVI's enforcement mechanisms work. The difference may be semantic rather than substantive.

### Tensions

- **Evidence requirements for proceeding**
  - **practitioner's position**: demands "evidence that VI, X, XVI actually cause confusion, conflict, or enforcement problems before migration" and "Audit last 6 months of PR reviews for principle VI/X/XVI citations" (recommendations 1-2)
  - **gate-strictness-skeptic's position**: demands "brief survey of 2-3 accepted principles that contain aspirational language alongside enforceable rules, as precedent for gate interpretation strictness" (recommendation 6)
  - **Nature of tension**: Both want more evidence before proceeding, but practitioner seeks evidence of operational problems while gate-strictness-skeptic seeks evidence of interpretation consistency. One looks downstream (user impact), the other looks sideways (constitutional precedent).
  - **Coordination needed**: Both evidence requirements could be satisfied in parallel—operational impact measurement and constitutional precedent analysis don't conflict and would strengthen the audit's foundation from both perspectives.

- **Risk mitigation priorities**
  - **practitioner's position**: emphasizes "consolidate all migrated guidance into single 'Constitutional Guidance' section of CONTRIBUTING.md" to prevent fragmentation (recommendation 6)
  - **gate-strictness-skeptic's position**: emphasizes keeping principles in constitution to preserve "enforcement weight" and avoid migration risks entirely (recommendations 1-3)
  - **Nature of tension**: practitioner accepts migration while minimizing fragmentation damage; gate-strictness-skeptic prefers avoiding migration to avoid damage entirely. Both recognize the same risks but differ on whether mitigation or avoidance is better.
  - **Coordination needed**: If any principles do migrate (despite gate-strictness-skeptic's objections), practitioner's consolidation approach should be the mitigation strategy. The positions are sequential rather than contradictory.

- **Standards for gate criterion interpretation**
  - **practitioner's position**: doesn't directly challenge gate interpretation but questions whether "gate compliance is means, not end" and whether "operational effectiveness matters more than theoretical consistency" (recommendation 5)
  - **gate-strictness-skeptic's position**: argues for "principle-level vs. clause-level gate interpretation" and objects to "word-by-word strictness test rather than evaluating principles as coherent units" (recommendation 4, missed opportunities)
  - **Nature of tension**: practitioner questions the importance of gate compliance when operational impact is unclear; gate-strictness-skeptic questions the interpretation of gate compliance requirements. Both challenge the audit's approach but from different angles.
  - **Coordination needed**: Both critiques could be integrated—requiring both operational justification AND consistent interpretation standards would strengthen the audit methodology from both practical and constitutional perspectives.
