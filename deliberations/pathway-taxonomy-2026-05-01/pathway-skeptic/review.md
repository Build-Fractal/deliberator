### Executive Summary

The v3.1.2 governance log entry (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, L557–587) accomplishes two tasks simultaneously: it applies a one-sentence elaboration to Principle II that was deferred from v3.0.0, and it introduces a fourth row into a governance pathway taxonomy table under the label "PATCH with pre-ratified deferred wording." The first task is uncontroversial and correct. The second task is the subject of this review, and it has a structural problem that cannot be papered over with charitable reading: governance scaffolding was added to the taxonomy by the same orchestrator that needed the scaffolding to justify the PR it was ratifying, in the same PR, without any external deliberation.

The four-pathway taxonomy now exists in CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md — the governance log, not the constitution itself — and its normative status is undefined. The "Cost" column for rows 2, 3, and 4 is identical ("single PR"). The "Trigger" column distinguishes them by second-order properties of wording origin, not by the nature of the amendment. The fourth row's only example is the PR that created it. There is no bounding rule preventing the addition of a fifth, sixth, or Nth row via the same mechanism. The taxonomy was built in a single session across four amendments, with each amendment's methodology note contributing a new row, and no external oversight at any step.

The case for scrubbing row 4 entirely is strong on authority and self-citation grounds, but the modification path (collapse rows 3 and 4 into a single "PATCH + single-PR" row with sub-notes) is stronger on practical grounds — it preserves the useful verbatim-preservation contract insight without enshrining a pathway whose only canonical example is its own ratification. My most important recommendation: collapse rows 3 and 4, move the resulting two-row table to CONSTITUTION.md's Governance section where it has normative force, and add an explicit amendment requirement for any future row additions.

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **Assumption: a methodology note in the governance log can establish binding governance classifications.** The v3.1.2 entry presents the pathway taxonomy as governing future amendments: "Future use should cite the prior SIR's TODO specification by version and section" (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, L586). But CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md is the governance log — a record of what happened, not a specification of what should happen. CONSTITUTION.md's Governance section (L1809–L1814) is where binding governance rules live. The same section says "Amendments: Require documentation of the change, rationale, and impact on existing specs." The pathway taxonomy is a classification mechanism for amendments — it is itself a governance amendment. Placing it in the governance log, via a methodology note, without deliberation, assumes the log and the constitution carry equivalent normative weight for governance decisions. They do not.

- **Assumption: the "pre-ratified" distinction is constitutionally meaningful.** The v3.1.2 entry treats rows 3 and 4 as distinct pathways requiring separate entries. But the constitutionally operative distinction is between amendments that require deliberation and amendments that do not — a binary determined by the verbatim-preservation contract (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, L553). Row 3 (sub-headings within existing structure) and row 4 (pre-specified deferred wording) are both single-PR, both verbatim-preservation contract, both PATCH-class. The only distinction is whether the verbatim text originated in a structural rearrangement or in a SIR TODO. This distinction determines provenance, not verification cost. From the constitution's perspective — where the operative question is "does this amendment require deliberation?" — both rows have the same answer ("no, when the verbatim contract holds"). A pathway taxonomy that creates two rows for the same deliberation-requirement answer over-decomposes the classification space, and does so based on a provenance distinction that the constitution does not recognize as load-bearing.

- **Assumption: four pathways adequately cover the space without a bounding principle.** The entry's methodology note states: "the taxonomy now reads:" followed by the four-row table — with no statement about whether the table is complete, bounded, or subject to amendment. The RFC/CVE analogy the v3.0.0 SIR uses (CONSTITUTION.md, L291–295 region: "Cf. RFC and CVE numbering: retired identifiers stay attached to their original assignments forever") is an analogy about identifier stability, not about process taxonomy design. RFC processes have one amendment pathway. CVE has one identification pathway. The conversus analogy was invoked to justify number stability; the v3.1.2 entry implicitly extends it to justify a four-pathway taxonomy, which the RFC/CVE analogy does not support and would actively contradict — both RFC and CVE gain their stability precisely from having a single, uniform process rather than a differentiated multi-row taxonomy.
