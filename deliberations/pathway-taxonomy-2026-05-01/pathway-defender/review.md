### Executive Summary

The v3.1.2 governance log entry documents a fourth pathway in the constitutional amendment taxonomy: "PATCH + pre-ratified deferred wording." This pathway applies when a prior SIR's TODO section explicitly specifies the exact wording to be applied in a future follow-up PR, and that prior verification cycle implicitly validated the wording by routing the deferral through an arbiter-reviewed process. The entry is asking whether this pathway is a meaningful governance distinction or a retrospective gloss on an unremarkable single-PR change.

The strongest case for retaining this pathway as a distinct row in the taxonomy rests on a single architectural observation: the authorization mechanism is different in kind from the v3.1.1 pathway, not merely different in degree. The v3.1.1 "PATCH + verbatim contract" pathway is authorized by the absence of new content (nothing changed, so no verification is needed). The v3.1.2 "PATCH + pre-ratified deferred wording" pathway is authorized by the presence of prior verification (something did change, but it was already verified). These are not the same authorization logic. Conflating them creates a governance gap: a future amendment could claim the v3.1.1 pathway for a change that actually has new content, if the reviewer does not distinguish between "verbatim preservation of structure" and "verbatim preservation of wording that was pre-agreed in a prior cycle."

The taxonomy functions as an authorization protocol, not a classification scheme. Its value is proportional to its precision in distinguishing what authorizes a single-PR pathway. Adding the fourth row is warranted, and it does not open the door to proliferation, because the authorization mechanism for each pathway is bounded by the finite set of verification contracts conversus currently has.

**Most important recommendation**: retain the fourth pathway row, but add one sentence to the v3.1.2 entry making the authorization mechanism explicit: "the single-PR pathway is authorized by the prior verification cycle's implicit validation, not by the absence of new content."

### Alignment

- **Verbatim-preservation contract discipline** (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 entry): The governance log entry specifies the verbatim-preservation contract in precise, character-for-character terms for each affected bullet. This aligns with the constitution's Principle II (Stable Interfaces) requirement that "changing a stable interface requires updating every consumer in a single atomic change." The contract is defined precisely enough that a future auditor can confirm compliance mechanically — the sentence either matches the v3.0.0 SIR's TODO specification character-for-character or it does not. This is exactly the "mechanical verification capability" that the Constitutional Inclusion Criteria (CONSTITUTION.md, Governance section) requires of constitutional content.

- **Four-pathway taxonomy placement** (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 methodology note): The taxonomy table in the v3.1.2 entry correctly anchors each pathway to a verifiable trigger: dual-deliberation triggered by principle removal/redefinition; verbatim contract triggered by first-instance structural feature; verbatim contract triggered by sub-headings in existing structure; pre-ratified wording triggered by same-version follow-up named in prior SIR. The trigger-column entries correspond to observable conditions, not to interpretation. This respects Principle XI's (Single Source of Truth) requirement that authoritative sources be defined without duplication.

- **Descriptive framing** (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 entry, "Why no deliberation cycle" paragraph): The entry correctly frames the pathway as descriptive — "the same blind verdict that ruled the deferral acceptable also pre-validated the wording. Re-running dual-deliberation on already-ratified language would be ceremonial rather than informative." This framing preserves Principle I's (Spec-Driven Development) requirement that process steps have purpose; when the purpose has already been served, repeating the step violates the principle more than skipping it.

- **TODO removal as resolution mechanism** (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 entry): The entry explicitly documents that the `# TODO(spec-070-cycle2-followup)` HTML comment block is removed as the resolution mechanism, and explains that leaving it in place "would mis-document the principle as still deferring." This aligns with Principle XII (No Dead Infrastructure) — a TODO comment for a completed deferral is dead infrastructure, and removing it is the correct action.

- **Citation of prior SIR TODO by version and section** (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 methodology note): The entry states: "Future use should cite the prior SIR's TODO specification by version and section." This aligns with Principle XI's single-source-of-truth requirement and Principle XIV's (Spec-Implementation Parity) requirement that "specs and implementations MUST agree on what was built." Citing the prior SIR's TODO by version and section ensures that future auditors can trace the authorization chain without ambiguity.

### Missed Opportunities

- **Explicit authorization mechanism statement**: The v3.1.2 entry describes the pathway's authorization through example ("the same blind verdict that ruled the deferral acceptable also pre-validated the wording") but never states the authorization mechanism abstractly. The entry would benefit from a one-sentence declaration: "This pathway is authorized by the prior verification cycle's implicit validation of the deferred wording, not by the absence of new content." Without this, a future reviewer conflating v3.1.1 and v3.1.2 could claim the v3.1.1 verbatim-contract pathway for a change that was actually deferred from a prior cycle — the two authorization mechanisms would blur. Impact: high.

- **Distinction from v3.1.1 made explicit in the taxonomy table**: The "Cost" and "Trigger" columns in the taxonomy table do not distinguish v3.1.1 from v3.1.2 mechanically. Both cost "single PR" and both have verbatim preservation. The distinguishing property — prior authorization vs. absent-content authorization — is buried in the "Trigger" column's prose ("Same-version follow-up named in prior SIR") without explaining why that trigger is categorically different from v3.1.1's trigger ("Sub-headings within existing structure"). Adding a "Authorization basis" column to the table would make the distinction crisp: "Absence of new content" for v3.1.1, "Prior verification cycle validation" for v3.1.2. Impact: high.

- **Proliferation guard not formalized**: The v3.1.2 methodology note asserts the pathway is rare — "it requires that the wording was specified in a prior SIR's TODO and that the prior SIR's verification cycle implicitly validated it" — but does not formalize the conditions as a gate. A future amendment could claim this pathway for wording that appeared in a prior document without being routed through a verification cycle. A formal gate sentence ("The prior SIR's TODO specification must have been produced during or in response to a spec 067 verification cycle, not merely noted as a future change in a non-verification context") would prevent drift. Impact: medium.

- **Relationship to the "deferred follow-up" pattern across the session**: The v3.1.2 entry is the third amendment in a sequence (2A → 2B → 2C → v3.1.2) that used deferred-follow-up patterns. Each earlier deferral was documented differently: cycle 2B used a separate GitHub issue; cycle 2C named the deferral in the issue's sequencing constraint; v3.1.2 used a SIR TODO block. These are three different deferral mechanisms, but the v3.1.2 taxonomy only formalizes the SIR-TODO form. If a future amendment defers wording via a GitHub issue rather than a SIR TODO, the pathway taxonomy provides no guidance. Extending the pathway definition to cover both forms ("named in a prior SIR's TODO or in a ratified verification cycle's acceptance criteria") would close this gap. Impact: medium.

- **Retroactive application risk**: The entry establishes that the pathway is rare and requires specific preconditions. But it does not address whether the pathway can be applied retroactively — that is, whether a prior amendment that was implemented via a regular single PR (without the pathway formalization) could be re-classified as a v3.1.2-pathway amendment. This matters because the v3.0.0 SIR itself has multiple deferred items (issue #94, the P3 XIX migration eligibility note) that could be candidates. Clarifying that the pathway applies only prospectively (to follow-ups whose deferral was documented in a prior SIR's TODO after v3.1.2's ratification) would prevent retroactive classification drift. Impact: low.

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **Implicit assumption that the pathway is self-evidently distinct**: The v3.1.2 entry's methodology note asserts the four-pathway taxonomy has been "validated" by the session's cycle sequence, but this assertion is not argued — it is claimed. The entry does not address whether the v3.1.2 pathway's distinguishing property (prior verification cycle validation) is meaningfully different from the v3.1.1 pathway's distinguishing property (absence of new content). This is the central governance question, and the entry's methodology note sidesteps it by treating the four-pathway listing as a natural outcome rather than an argued conclusion. The assumption that the distinction is self-evident is not warranted; it requires the explicit authorization-mechanism argument described in the "Missed Opportunities" section above.

- **Assumption that the pathway prevents ceremony without introducing risk**: The entry's "Why no deliberation cycle" paragraph argues that "re-running dual-deliberation on already-ratified language would be ceremonial rather than informative." This is correct for the specific case where the wording is identical to the prior SIR's TODO specification. But it implicitly assumes that "already-ratified" and "verbatim match to prior SIR" are sufficient conditions for the pathway — without noting that the pathway offers no protection against a wording that LOOKS like it matches the prior SIR's TODO but actually differs in a consequential word. The verbatim-preservation contract is the protection mechanism, but the entry does not specify who verifies the verbatim match or how. A future use of this pathway could produce a near-verbatim match that the orchestrator claims is "character-for-character" without independent verification. This is a genuine risk gap, not just a polish concern.

### Actionable Recommendations

1. **Add explicit authorization-mechanism statement to pathway definition** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: The v3.1.2 entry describes the pathway's authorization through example and implication but never states the mechanism abstractly (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 "Why no deliberation cycle" paragraph).
   - **Proposed change**: Insert after the "Why no deliberation cycle" paragraph: "This pathway's authorization mechanism is categorically distinct from v3.1.1: the v3.1.1 pathway is authorized by the *absence* of new content (nothing changed, so verification is unnecessary); the v3.1.2 pathway is authorized by *prior verification* (the wording was validated in a prior cycle's arbiter ruling, so re-verification would be ceremonial). A future amendment that cites this pathway MUST identify the prior verification cycle and the specific arbiter ruling or TODO specification that pre-validated the wording."
   - **Rationale**: Without this statement, the two pathways blur at their distinguishing boundary. Principle II requires that stable interfaces be defined with enough precision to identify breaking changes unambiguously; the pathway taxonomy is a stable governance interface.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future amendments conflate v3.1.1 and v3.1.2, applying the lower-ceremony pathway to changes that lack the prior-verification-cycle precondition. This silently degrades the verification coverage of the amendment process.

2. **Add Authorization-basis column to taxonomy table** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: The taxonomy table in the v3.1.2 entry has columns: Pathway, Cost, Trigger, Examples. The "Trigger" column does not distinguish authorization mechanisms (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 methodology note, table).
   - **Proposed change**: Add a fifth column "Authorization basis" with values: "full dual-deliberation (spec 067)" for v3.0.0; "absence of new normative content" for v3.1.0 and v3.1.1; "prior verification cycle's implicit validation of deferred wording" for v3.1.2. This makes the distinguishing property of each pathway visible at a glance.
   - **Rationale**: The table's purpose is to allow future amendments to self-classify. Without an authorization-basis column, classification requires reading the prose around each table entry — which defeats the table's purpose as a compact decision aid.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future amendments continue to require reading four governance log entries in full to determine pathway applicability, which is the problem the taxonomy was designed to solve.

3. **Formalize the proliferation guard as a gate condition** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: The v3.1.2 methodology note states the pathway "requires" specific preconditions but does not state these as gate conditions that a claiming amendment must satisfy (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 methodology note, "Future use should cite the prior SIR's TODO specification by version and section").
   - **Proposed change**: Add a gate-condition paragraph: "A claiming amendment satisfies the v3.1.2 pathway only when all three conditions hold: (a) the wording to be applied was specified verbatim in a prior SIR's TODO, identified by version and section; (b) the prior SIR's TODO was produced during or in response to a spec 067 verification cycle (not merely noted as a future change in a non-verification context); (c) the claiming amendment applies the wording character-for-character without modification. If condition (a), (b), or (c) fails, the claiming amendment must use the v3.1.1 pathway (if no new content is introduced) or the full dual-deliberation pathway (if new content is introduced)."
   - **Rationale**: Without explicit gate conditions, the pathway's proliferation guard depends on future orchestrators correctly inferring the preconditions from the prose — which is the same reliance-on-interpretation failure mode that Constitutional Inclusion Criterion 2 (Falsifiable scope) was designed to prevent.
   - **Risk if ignored**: The pathway becomes a "single-PR escape hatch" that future amendments invoke without satisfying the prior-verification precondition, silently degrading the verification coverage over time.

4. **Extend pathway definition to cover GitHub-issue-deferred wording** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: The v3.1.2 pathway is defined in terms of "prior SIR's TODO" but the session used at least three different deferral mechanisms (SIR TODO block, GitHub issue, issue sequencing constraint) and only formalizes one (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 methodology note).
   - **Proposed change**: Extend the pathway definition: "The prior SIR's TODO specification may take any of the following forms: (a) a `# TODO(...)` HTML comment block in the SIR, with the exact wording specified; (b) a GitHub issue filed during the ratification commit, with the exact wording specified in the issue body; (c) a verification cycle acceptance criteria entry that specifies the exact wording as a required future fix. The pathway does not apply to wording noted informally (without verbatim specification) in any of these locations."
   - **Rationale**: The cycle 2B deferral used a GitHub issue; if that issue had contained pre-ratified wording, it would have qualified for this pathway under the proposed extension. Limiting the pathway to SIR TODO blocks creates an arbitrary formalism distinction between structurally equivalent deferral mechanisms.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future amendments with GitHub-issue-deferred wording are forced into the full dual-deliberation pathway even when the prior verification cycle already validated the wording, increasing ceremony without adding coverage.

5. **Construct and document a future-use-case scenario in the methodology note** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: The v3.1.2 methodology note documents the pathway retrospectively (the four pathways that were validated this session) but does not provide a prospective example of when the v3.1.2 pathway would be the right choice for a future amendment (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 methodology note).
   - **Proposed change**: Add a one-paragraph prospective example: "Hypothetical future application: a spec 067 blind verification cycle produces an ACCEPT-level finding requiring a new sentence in Principle II's breaking-change coordination text, but the arbiter explicitly authorizes deferring the sentence to a same-version follow-up because it is a downstream elaboration of already-ratified language. The ratification PR's SIR includes a TODO block specifying the exact sentence verbatim. The follow-up PR applies that sentence character-for-character under this pathway without re-running dual-deliberation."
   - **Rationale**: A pathway without a prospective use case is a retrospective gloss, not a governance contribution. Documenting a future-use-case scenario demonstrates that the pathway has independent value beyond rationalizing what the orchestrator already did.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future governance auditors conclude the pathway was added for retrospective coherence rather than forward-looking governance value, and treat it as a descriptive footnote rather than an authoritative pathway.

6. **Clarify prospective-only applicability** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: The v3.1.2 entry does not address whether the pathway can be applied retroactively to prior amendments or only to future ones (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 methodology note).
   - **Proposed change**: Add a sentence to the methodology note: "This pathway applies prospectively to follow-up PRs whose deferral was documented in a prior SIR's TODO after v3.1.2's ratification (2026-05-01). Prior amendments implemented as regular single PRs are not retroactively reclassified as v3.1.2-pathway amendments; their authorization is their own verbatim-preservation contract or dual-deliberation record."
   - **Rationale**: Retroactive reclassification would allow future orchestrators to claim the pathway for prior changes without the prior-verification precondition being satisfied, which would undermine the gate conditions established in Recommendation 3.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Issue #94 and the P3 XIX migration eligibility note — both of which are deferred items from prior cycles — could be claimed as v3.1.2-pathway candidates, lowering ceremony without the pathway's preconditions being satisfied.

7. **Remove the implicit claim that four pathways are "validated"** (Priority: P3)
   - **Current state**: The v3.1.2 methodology note states "cycles 2A/2B/2C together demonstrate three governance pathway types in lockstep" and "Now ratified twice (v3.1.1 cycle 2C original, v3.1.3 cycle 2C follow-on)" — framing implies the four-pathway taxonomy has been empirically validated (CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, v3.1.2 and v3.1.3 entries).
   - **Proposed change**: Replace "The pathway is robust to the kind of interpretation slip..." (v3.1.3 entry) with: "The pathway's verbatim-preservation contract was exercised twice across v3.1.1 and v3.1.3. This is sufficient to treat the contract as a reliable mechanism, but not sufficient to claim the pathway taxonomy has been 'validated' in the sense of the Constitutional Inclusion Criteria gate — the gate applies to constitutional principles, not to governance process descriptions."
   - **Rationale**: The Constitutional Inclusion Criteria gate (CONSTITUTION.md, Governance section) applies prospectively to principles, not to governance process descriptions. Claiming the pathway taxonomy is "validated" conflates the empirical exercise of a process with the normative approval of a principle, which could be used by future amendments to argue that governance process descriptions require the same evidence base as constitutional principles.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Minor — this is a framing issue, not a structural one. But it creates a false equivalence between exercising a process and ratifying a principle that could cause confusion in future deliberations.
