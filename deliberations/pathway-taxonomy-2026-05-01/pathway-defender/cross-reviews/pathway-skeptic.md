### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Row 4 retention vs. structural collapse**
  - **pathway-skeptic claims**: "Collapse rows 3 and 4 into a single 'PATCH + single-PR' row" (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 1, P1). The skeptic argues both rows have "identical Cost ('single PR') and differ only in the origin of the verbatim text: structural rearrangement (row 3) vs. wording named in a prior SIR TODO (row 4). These are not distinct pathway types — they are both instances of 'PATCH-class amendment whose content is fully determined before the PR opens.'"
  - **pathway-defender claims**: "The central governance question" is whether prior-verification authorization "is meaningfully different from the v3.1.1 pathway's distinguishing property (absence of new content)." The defender's entire Executive Summary and the Alignment section on "Verbatim-preservation contract discipline" rest on the answer being yes: "the v3.1.1 pathway is authorized by the *absence* of new content (nothing changed, so verification is unnecessary); the v3.1.2 pathway is authorized by *prior verification* (the wording was validated in a prior cycle's arbiter ruling, so re-verification would be ceremonial)" (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 1).
  - **Why this is dangerous**: These positions cannot coexist in implementation. If the skeptic's P1 collapse recommendation is adopted, the authorization-mechanism column the defender recommends adding (Rec 2) has no surface to attach to — it cannot distinguish absence-of-content from prior-verification within a single merged row without reconstructing the very distinction it collapsed. Conversely, if the defender's two-row structure is retained and an authorization-basis column is added per Rec 2, the skeptic's collapse recommendation is rendered moot but its underlying concern (proliferation anchored to provenance rather than verification cost) remains unaddressed. Synthesis cannot split the difference by partially collapsing: either the rows are merged (and the distinction the defender argues is load-bearing disappears) or they are retained (and the skeptic's proliferation analysis applies).
  - **Suggested resolution**: The resolution requires committing to one of the following frameworks at the synthesis level: (a) the operative classification axis is "verification cost / deliberation requirement," in which case the skeptic's collapse argument applies and the two rows merge with a sub-note; or (b) the operative classification axis is "authorization mechanism / what licensed the single-PR pathway," in which case the defender's rows are distinct and the authorization-basis column is the right addition. The synthesis should explicitly decide which axis the taxonomy is designed to answer and let the structural consequence follow from that decision. The defender's framing has the stronger claim to constitutional load-bearing, because the question future amendments must answer is "what licensed me to skip deliberation?" — but the skeptic's proliferation concern can be addressed within the two-row structure by adding gate conditions per the defender's Rec 3 rather than collapsing.

- **Normative authority of a methodology note**
  - **pathway-skeptic claims**: "A methodology note in the governance log can establish binding governance classifications" — and calls this "Off-Base" (Off-Base Assumptions, item 1). The skeptic argues that CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md is "the governance log — a record of what happened, not a specification of what should happen" and that placing the taxonomy there "assumes the log and the constitution carry equivalent normative weight for governance decisions. They do not."
  - **pathway-defender claims**: The pathway is "descriptive (it documents what the orchestrator already did), not prescriptive" — and treats this as acceptable because the descriptive framing avoids the prescriptive-norm problem (Executive Summary; also "Assumption: a pathway prevents ceremony without introducing risk" in Off-Base Assumptions, which the defender labels as partly correct but incomplete). The defender's Alignment section treats the taxonomy table as having sufficient legitimacy to ground Recs 1–7 without requiring a prior deliberation.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If the skeptic is right that methodology notes cannot establish binding governance, then even the defender's correct authorization-mechanism argument is insufficient to save the pathway: the pathway could be entirely right in substance and still fail to govern future amendments, because future amendments can simply cite a different methodology note to claim a different pathway. But if the defender is right that the pathway is descriptive and therefore needs no deliberation, the skeptic's entire "Off-Base Assumptions" section and its Recs 3 and 8 ("add amendment requirement for new taxonomy rows"; "deliberation-bypass prevention clause") are over-engineered responses to a non-problem. These positions embed different theories of how governance classification mechanisms acquire authority, and whichever theory the synthesis adopts will determine the scope of every other recommendation.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The two positions can be reconciled if the synthesis distinguishes between the pathway's current status (descriptive, non-normative) and its target status (normative, in CONSTITUTION.md). Both reviews agree the taxonomy should move to CONSTITUTION.md (skeptic Rec 2, defender Rec 2); the migration is the moment at which the descriptive record becomes a normative rule. Before migration: non-normative, as the skeptic requires. After migration: normative, carrying the authorization-mechanism distinction the defender argues is essential. This framing allows both reviews to be correct simultaneously, but requires the migration to happen before any future amendment cites the taxonomy as binding governance.

- **Sufficiency of the verbatim-preservation contract as a protection mechanism**
  - **pathway-skeptic claims**: The v3.1.2 entry's "implicit validation" claim is "not falsifiable" because "any orchestrator can write a TODO sentence in a SIR, claim the underlying deliberation 'implicitly validated' it, and then apply the low-cost pathway in a subsequent PR" (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 5; also Missed Opportunities item on "implicit validation"). The skeptic argues the scope constraint must be: "the deliberation must have considered whether the claim is correct, not merely authorized the deferral."
  - **pathway-defender claims**: "The verbatim-preservation contract IS the protection mechanism" — the prior SIR's TODO specification must be cited by version and section, and the claiming amendment applies the wording "character-for-character without modification" (Rec 3, gate conditions (a) and (c)). The defender's gate conditions treat verbatim match plus SIR provenance as sufficient, without requiring that the prior deliberation have explicitly evaluated the substantive claim.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: These positions encode incompatible risk models. The defender's gate conditions (Rec 3) would accept a case where a SIR TODO was written by the orchestrator without deliberation scope coverage of the substantive claim — which is exactly the laundering scenario the skeptic's Rec 5 is designed to prevent. If the defender's gate conditions are adopted without the skeptic's scope constraint, the pathway can be invoked by any orchestrator who writes pre-ratified wording into a SIR TODO, regardless of whether the prior deliberation evaluated the wording's correctness. The two gate conditions (verbatim match, SIR provenance) cannot detect this failure mode; only a scope constraint on what the prior deliberation evaluated can detect it.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The defender's gate conditions (a), (b), and (c) in Rec 3 should be augmented with the skeptic's Rec 5 scope constraint as condition (d): "the prior deliberation must have evaluated the substantive claim being applied in its scope, not merely authorized a deferral of its wording." The skeptic yields on the mechanism (verbatim contract remains the verification tool); the defender yields on the authorization chain (SIR provenance alone is insufficient without deliberation scope coverage). The combined gate is strictly more protective than either alone and preserves the defender's core authorization-mechanism distinction.

### Tensions

- **Proliferation prevention: amendment requirement vs. gate conditions**
  - **pathway-skeptic's position**: New taxonomy rows must require a MINOR amendment per the standard amendment process; "no methodology note, SIR appendix, or governance log entry may establish a new pathway unilaterally" (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 3, P1).
  - **pathway-defender's position**: The proliferation guard should be formalized as gate conditions that a claiming amendment must satisfy — three conditions ((a) verbatim SIR TODO, (b) produced during a spec 067 verification cycle, (c) character-for-character application) — with cascade logic directing non-qualifying amendments to higher-cost pathways (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 3).
  - **Nature of tension**: The skeptic's amendment requirement is a process constraint on who can add rows; the defender's gate conditions are a substantive constraint on what qualifies for the existing rows. These operate at different levels. Gate conditions without an amendment requirement still allow a future orchestrator to ADD a row by methodology note if they argue the new content doesn't fit any existing row. An amendment requirement without gate conditions still allows the amendment process to be invoked for a trivially different distinction. Neither alone closes the other's gap.
  - **Coordination needed**: Both constraints should be adopted together. The gate conditions govern use of existing rows (defender's mechanism); the amendment requirement governs creation of new rows (skeptic's mechanism). These are complementary rather than competing. The synthesis should recommend both: apply defender's gate conditions to row eligibility and skeptic's amendment requirement to row creation, and make the boundary between "using an existing row" and "creating a new row" explicit in the taxonomy.

- **Self-citation severity: disqualifying vs. manageable**
  - **pathway-skeptic's position**: The pathway's only example being its own ratification PR is a "governance antipattern" warranting an explicit "audit note" in the governance log and a two-example requirement before "canonical" status (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 4, P2; also "Missed Opportunities" on "requiring multiple real-world examples before canonicity").
  - **pathway-defender's position**: The self-citation is acknowledged and manageable — label row 4 as "provisional — single example" and require two distinct PRs from different sessions before declaring it canonical (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 4, P2).
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews identify the same problem and propose similar remedies (provisional label, two-example requirement). The tension is in severity framing: the skeptic treats the self-citation as evidence that the pathway is premature and potentially illegitimate; the defender treats it as a documentation quality issue with a straightforward fix. The framing difference matters for the synthesis because the skeptic's severity framing strengthens the case for collapse (Rec 1) — if a pathway with one self-referential example is premature by definition, the natural response is to merge it into the established row rather than wait for a second example that may never come.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should decide whether provisional status is a sufficient response to self-citation or whether it merely defers the legitimacy question. If the defender's authorization-mechanism distinction is accepted as load-bearing (resolving Contradiction 1 in favor of retention), then provisional status is sufficient and the two-example requirement addresses the severity. If the skeptic's collapse recommendation is adopted (resolving Contradiction 1 in favor of collapse), then the self-citation discussion is moot.

- **Location of responsibility for the taxonomy's governance gap**
  - **pathway-skeptic's position**: The normative gap is structural — the taxonomy is in the wrong document (governance log, not constitution) and was established by the wrong mechanism (methodology note, not deliberation). The Rec 2 migration is necessary but not sufficient; the underlying problem is that methodology notes cannot create governance machinery regardless of where the result lives (Off-Base Assumptions, item 1).
  - **pathway-defender's position**: The normative gap is documentation quality — the taxonomy exists and the authorization mechanisms are real, but they need clearer expression (explicit authorization-mechanism statement, Rec 1) and a better home (migration to CONSTITUTION.md, Rec 2). The mechanism is sound; the presentation is not (Alignment section: "Verbatim-preservation contract discipline"; "Descriptive framing").
  - **Nature of tension**: The skeptic's diagnosis implies that migrating the taxonomy to CONSTITUTION.md without accompanying deliberation produces a taxonomy with the same structural legitimacy problem in a more authoritative document — potentially worse, not better. The defender's diagnosis implies that migration is straightforwardly curative because the taxonomy's content is correct and only its location is wrong. The synthesis must decide which diagnosis applies, because it determines whether migration alone is sufficient or whether a deliberation cycle on the taxonomy itself is required before migration.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis could split this tension by requiring a "lightweight verification" of the taxonomy (analogous to the single-PR pathway for verbatim-preservation amendments) before migration — not a full dual-deliberation, but a cross-reference audit confirming that the four pathways are mutually exhaustive, non-overlapping, and correctly anchored to verifiable triggers. This satisfies the skeptic's concern that some governance review is needed while not treating the taxonomy as if it were a new constitutional principle requiring full dual-deliberation. The defender's Rec 3 (gate conditions) could be drafted as part of this lightweight verification output.

- **The "validated" framing and what counts as canonical**
  - **pathway-skeptic's position**: The v3.1.2 entry's claim that the four-pathway taxonomy has been "validated" by the session's cycle sequence conflates exercising a process with ratifying a principle (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 7, P3).
  - **pathway-defender's position**: "The pathway's verbatim-preservation contract was exercised twice across v3.1.1 and v3.1.3. This is sufficient to treat the contract as a reliable mechanism" (Actionable Recommendations, Rec 7). The defender's framing accepts "exercised" as evidence of reliability, not of canonical status.
  - **Nature of tension**: These positions agree that "validated" is the wrong word but disagree on what replaces it. The defender accepts "reliable mechanism" as a replacement, grounded in two exercises. The skeptic requires explicit decoupling of "exercised" from "canonical" as a category. Neither framing is wrong, but they imply different documentation requirements — the skeptic's framing requires a definitional clarification in the governance log; the defender's framing requires only vocabulary precision in the methodology note.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should adopt the skeptic's category distinction (exercised ≠ validated ≠ canonical) while accepting the defender's calibration (two exercises of the verbatim-preservation contract is evidence of reliability, not of constitutional status). A single sentence in the revised methodology note — "Exercising a pathway twice demonstrates reliability; canonical status requires migration to CONSTITUTION.md under a verification-reviewed draft" — closes the gap without requiring either review to substantially revise its position.

### Safe Agreements

- **The taxonomy must move to CONSTITUTION.md before it can govern future amendments**
  - **Shared position**: Both reviews independently recommend migrating the pathway taxonomy (or a revised version of it) to CONSTITUTION.md's Governance section, where it carries normative force. Skeptic Rec 2 (P1): "Either (a) migrate the collapsed two-row table... into CONSTITUTION.md's Governance section... or (b) add an explicit disclaimer to the governance log entry: 'This table is a session observation, not a normative governance requirement.'" Defender Rec 2 (P1): migrate the taxonomy to CONSTITUTION.md "appended as a 'Verification pathway classification' sub-bullet under the Amendments bullet."
  - **Combined evidence**: The skeptic provides the authority argument (only CONSTITUTION.md content carries normative weight for future amendments); the defender provides the content argument (the taxonomy's authorization-mechanism structure is correct and worth preserving). Together, they establish both that migration is necessary (skeptic) and that the content is worth migrating (defender). Neither argument alone is sufficient — the skeptic's authority argument without the defender's content argument would recommend a disclaimer, not a migration; the defender's content argument without the skeptic's authority argument would leave a normatively ambiguous taxonomy in place.
  - **Confidence level**: High. This is the one structural recommendation where both reviews arrive at the same conclusion via independent analytical paths. The synthesis can treat migration to CONSTITUTION.md as settled and focus debate on what the migrated content should contain (the row-collapse vs. row-retention question) rather than whether migration should occur.

- **The verbatim-preservation contract is the operative verification test for single-PR pathways**
  - **Shared position**: Both reviews cite CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md L553 ("the pathway is determined by the verbatim-preservation contract, not the version classification") as correctly identifying the load-bearing mechanism. Skeptic Alignment section: "CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md, L553 correctly identifies that 'the pathway is determined by the verbatim-preservation contract, not the version classification.' This is a genuinely useful framing." Defender Alignment section: "the verbatim-preservation contract in precise, character-for-character terms for each affected bullet" is what gives the pathway mechanical verifiability.
  - **Combined evidence**: The skeptic values this framing because it separates verification cost from version classification — the key move that makes the taxonomy intellectually coherent rather than an ad-hoc version-number table. The defender values this framing because it establishes that the authorization mechanism (what licensed the single-PR pathway) is the operative axis, not the content provenance. Both reviews use this shared foundation to argue for their otherwise divergent conclusions, which means the foundation is more robust than either review in isolation: it survives the cross-review intact.
  - **Confidence level**: High. The synthesis should anchor the revised taxonomy explicitly in the verbatim-preservation contract framing, regardless of which row structure is adopted. Both reviews agree the contract is the right test; neither review recommends abandoning or modifying it.

- **The pathway's current documentation does not make the authorization mechanism explicit enough**
  - **Shared position**: Both reviews independently identify that the v3.1.2 methodology note fails to state the authorization mechanism in terms that distinguish it from v3.1.1's authorization mechanism. Defender Missed Opportunities (first item): "The entry describes the pathway's authorization through example... but never states the authorization mechanism abstractly. The entry would benefit from a one-sentence declaration: 'This pathway is authorized by the prior verification cycle's implicit validation of the deferred wording, not by the absence of new content.'" Skeptic Missed Opportunities (item on "implicit validation"): "An explicit criterion... would tighten this" — specifically, distinguishing SIR-author-written TODO without deliberation scope coverage from prior deliberation that actually evaluated the substantive claim.
  - **Combined evidence**: The defender identifies the problem as missing abstraction (no mechanism statement); the skeptic identifies the problem as insufficient falsifiability (the "implicit validation" concept is too loose). Both diagnoses point to the same documentation gap: the v3.1.2 entry cannot currently answer the question "did the prior deliberation validate THIS wording?" with a yes or no. The combined evidence from both reviews makes the case that adding a mechanism statement (defender) constrained by a scope criterion (skeptic) is necessary regardless of which row structure is adopted. Neither the two-row nor the collapsed-row version of the taxonomy is improved by leaving the authorization mechanism underspecified.
  - **Confidence level**: Medium. Both reviews agree on the diagnosis. They disagree on the prescription's severity (the defender's Rec 1 stops at abstraction; the skeptic's Rec 5 adds a scope constraint that the defender's Rec 3 gate condition (b) partially captures). The synthesis should adopt both: abstract mechanism statement plus scope criterion for the prior deliberation's coverage of the substantive claim.
