### Dangerous Contradictions Found

**Resolved Contradictions**

**1. Normative authority of a methodology note**

*What the contradiction was*: pathway-skeptic's Off-Base Assumption #1 held that CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md (the governance log) cannot carry normative weight for governance decisions — placing the taxonomy there assumes the log and the constitution carry equivalent normative weight, which they do not. pathway-defender's Phase 1 review treated the taxonomy as having sufficient legitimacy in the governance log to ground all enrichment recommendations without requiring prior deliberation.

*Who conceded*: Both agents modified their positions. pathway-skeptic revised the Off-Base Assumption in Phase 3: the problem is not that methodology notes are structurally incapable of establishing governance classification, but that the taxonomy's normative status is *ambiguous* until migration occurs. pathway-defender added migration to CONSTITUTION.md as a new P1 recommendation, acknowledging that gate conditions in the governance log are "records of intent" while the same conditions in CONSTITUTION.md are "rules that future amendments must satisfy."

*Resolution*: Before migration, the taxonomy is descriptive and non-normative. After migration to CONSTITUTION.md's Governance section under a verification-reviewed draft, it becomes normative. Migration is the act that converts the former into the latter. Both agents converged on this framing in Phase 3 revisions (defender revision new Rec 1; skeptic revision surviving Rec 2 with the before/after normative status accepted explicitly).

**2. Sufficiency of verbatim-preservation contract as a protection mechanism**

*What the contradiction was*: pathway-defender's original gate conditions (conditions (a)–(c): verbatim specification in prior SIR's TODO + produced during spec 067 cycle + character-for-character application) could be satisfied by an orchestrator who wrote a TODO sentence without the prior deliberation having evaluated the deferred wording's substantive correctness. pathway-skeptic's Rec 5 identified "implicit validation" as unfalsifiable and proposed replacing it with a scope-coverage requirement.

*Who conceded*: pathway-defender conceded fully. Original Rec 1 and Rec 3 formulations used "implicit validation" and "prior SIR's verification cycle" without specifying that the deliberation must have evaluated the claim's correctness. Both formulations were modified to incorporate the scope-coverage requirement: the prior deliberation must have evaluated whether the deferred wording's substantive claim is correct — not merely authorized the deferral.

*Resolution*: Scope-coverage replaces "implicit validation" throughout. It becomes condition (b) in the combined gate (defender's modified Rec 3 incorporating skeptic's modified Rec 5). The mechanism statement in modified PD-R1 replaces "implicit validation" with "prior deliberation scope coverage." Both agents converged on this formulation.

**3. Proliferation prevention: gate conditions vs. amendment requirement**

*What the contradiction was*: pathway-defender's original Rec 3 proposed gate conditions governing row use but left row creation ungoverned. pathway-skeptic's Rec 3 proposed an amendment requirement governing row creation but left row eligibility unconstrained. These were positioned as competing solutions but operated at different levels.

*Who conceded*: Both agents conceded the gap in their own position. defender conceded that gate conditions alone leave row creation ungoverned; skeptic conceded that an amendment requirement alone leaves the use-surface conditions unconstrained.

*Resolution*: Combined two-layer proliferation guard. Gate conditions govern row use (conditions (a)–(d) from defender's modified Rec 3, incorporating the scope-coverage condition). A MINOR amendment requirement governs row creation (condition (d) from defender's modified Rec 3 + skeptic's modified Rec 3). Both layers are necessary and non-redundant. (defender revision: modified Rec 3 condition (d); skeptic revision: modified Rec 3.)

**4. Extension to GitHub-issue-deferred wording vs. scope-coverage restriction**

*What the contradiction was*: pathway-defender's Rec 4 would extend recognized deferral forms to include GitHub issues filed during ratification. pathway-skeptic's Rec 5 would require scope coverage of the substantive claim. A GitHub issue authorizing deferral without deliberation scope coverage would pass Rec 4 (form recognition) while failing Rec 5 (scope coverage), producing contradictory eligibility outcomes for the same class of amendment.

*Who conceded*: Both agents adopted the conjunctive resolution surfaced in defender's cross-review of skeptic (Dangerous Contradictions, third item): form recognition and scope coverage are both necessary conditions — neither alone is sufficient.

*Resolution*: Gate condition (a) covers form recognition (SIR TODO block, GitHub issue filed during ratification with verbatim wording, or verification cycle acceptance criteria entry). Gate condition (b) requires scope coverage regardless of which form was used. The two conditions conjoin: both must hold. A GitHub issue authorizing deferral without deliberation scope coverage fails condition (b) regardless of satisfying condition (a). (defender revision: modified Rec 4 integrated into modified Rec 3 gate condition (a); skeptic revision: modified Rec 5.)

### Systemic Contradictions

- **Taxonomy location mismatch with normative function**
  - **Manifests in**: Dangerous Contradiction #1 (normative authority of methodology note); "enrich in place vs. relocate" tensions in both cross-reviews; PD-R2's sequencing dependence on migration; PS-R9 absorbed into PS-R2 because both address the same location problem.
  - **Root cause**: The methodology note entries in CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md accumulated governance classification decisions that future amendments will treat as normative guidance, but the governance log is designed as a descriptive record — an index of what happened, not a specification of what must happen. The spec's structure does not distinguish between "what occurred in this session" and "what future amendments must do." Every normative claim embedded in a methodology note inherits this ambiguity.
  - **Implication for spec**: A structural separation is needed between descriptive session entries and normative governance rules. The minimum interim fix is the "pending constitutional incorporation" disclaimer (absorbed from PS-R9) applied to the taxonomy table. The complete fix is migration to CONSTITUTION.md's Governance section (PD-NR1/PS-R2). Until migration occurs, every enrichment recommendation — however precisely worded — produces records of intent rather than enforceable rules.

- **Self-referential pathway creation**
  - **Manifests in**: PS-R1's collapse argument (a taxonomy that classifies itself into its own lowest-cost pathway); Dispute 1 (the authorization-mechanism distinction matters most precisely in the self-referential case); PS-R3's amendment requirement (designed to prevent future self-referential row additions); PD-R3's gate conditions (particularly condition (b), which the canonical example may not satisfy for itself per PD-NR2).
  - **Root cause**: The v3.1.2 pathway was created by the same PR that needed it to justify its own amendment pathway. Every governance classification mechanism faces this bootstrapping problem at first instantiation: the mechanism cannot pre-exist its own creation, so the first use is always self-referential. The tension between "a pathway needs a prior example to be credible" and "a pathway can only be created by its first use" is inherent to governance bootstrapping.
  - **Implication for spec**: The self-referential bootstrapping problem cannot be eliminated, but it can be managed by: explicit provisional status for first-use pathways (PS-R4), explicit acknowledgment of the canonical-example self-consistency gap (PD-NR2), and a prospective-only clause preventing retroactive application (PD-R6/PS-NR1). These measures together contain the problem without resolving it. Future governance mechanisms should include all three controls at creation time.

- **Authorization-chain completeness vs. decision-routing simplicity**
  - **Manifests in**: Dispute 1 (classification axis); PD-R2 (authorization-basis column serves the authorization-chain function); PS-R1 (collapsed row serves the routing-simplicity function); both agents' scope-coverage requirement (makes the authorization chain more complex but more precise).
  - **Root cause**: The taxonomy serves two distinct functions — routing (which pathway applies?) and authorization audit (what licensed this pathway?). These functions have opposite optimal structures. Routing prefers fewer, simpler rows with the same answer for equivalent outcomes. Authorization audit prefers distinct rows with distinct authorization chains so that conditions are unambiguously sub-case-specific. The v3.1.2 entry conflated these functions by treating the authorization-mechanism distinction as a routing criterion, without acknowledging that the two functions can be served simultaneously by a single table that names both.
  - **Implication for spec**: The taxonomy's header should declare both functions explicitly and acknowledge that the row structure is optimized for the authorization-audit function at a modest cost to routing simplicity. A "Deliberation required?" summary entry in each row (all single-PR rows: "no, when verbatim preservation holds") would serve the routing function without requiring routing-optimized structure.

- **Retrospective coherence vs. prospective governance value**
  - **Manifests in**: Skeptic's Off-Base Assumption #2 (the pathway appears to rationalize what already happened rather than govern what will happen); PD-R5 (prospective use-case scenario needed to demonstrate forward value); PD-R6/PS-NR1 (prospective-only clause prevents backward reach); PD-NR2 (self-consistency gap — canonical example may not satisfy its own gate conditions).
  - **Root cause**: The pathway taxonomy was built across four amendments in a single session, with each amendment's methodology note contributing a row and the final entry creating a row whose sole canonical example is itself. This sequential, self-justifying construction makes it structurally difficult to distinguish between "a taxonomy that accurately describes what happened" and "a taxonomy designed to make what happened look correct." Both may be simultaneously true, but the structural similarity to retrospective rationalization creates a credibility gap that prospective controls must address.
  - **Implication for spec**: The governance log entry for v3.1.2 needs the prospective-only clause (prevents backward application), the self-consistency gap acknowledgment (names the canonical example's limitation precisely), and a forward-looking use case (demonstrates the pathway has value beyond rationalizing this session's amendments). These three controls together are the minimum credibility foundation for the taxonomy's prospective governance role.

- **Bounded proliferation without a prior bounding precedent**
  - **Manifests in**: PS-R1 (collapse reduces row count as a proliferation control); PS-R3/PD-R3 condition (d) (amendment requirement bounds future row additions); PS-R8 (deliberation-bypass prevention); PS-R7 (RFC/CVE correction removes false authority for proliferation); the fact that no governance mechanism in the current spec constrains governance log entries from creating taxonomy rows.
  - **Root cause**: The taxonomy grew from one implicit pathway (MAJOR with full deliberation) to four explicit rows in a single session. There is no precedent in the governance log for bounding a classification mechanism's growth. The amendment process defined in spec 067 governs verification methodology changes; the constitutional amendment process governs CONSTITUTION.md changes. Governance log methodology notes occupy neither category — they are unbound by any formal process until the taxonomy is migrated.
  - **Implication for spec**: The migration to CONSTITUTION.md (PD-NR1/PS-R2) is the structural prerequisite that brings the taxonomy under the amendment process. Until migration, the taxonomy is unbound. After migration, the MINOR amendment requirement for new rows (PS-R3 modified, condition (d)) closes the gap. The migration must precede the proliferation guard having normative force — this is the same prerequisite structure as all other enrichment recommendations.

### Remaining Disputes

- **Dispute: Classification axis — authorization-mechanism vs. deliberation-requirement**
  - **Positions**: pathway-defender argues the taxonomy's operative axis is "what preconditions authorize skipping deliberation?" (authorization-mechanism), which requires separate rows for rows 3 and 4 because gate conditions are sub-case-specific and only enforceable when attached to a specific row. (defender disputes.md, first dispute.) pathway-skeptic argues the operative axis is "does this amendment need deliberation?" (deliberation-requirement), under which both rows answer identically ("no, when verbatim preservation holds"), and the authorization-mechanism distinction belongs in a sub-note. (skeptic disputes.md, first dispute.)
  - **Arguments**:
    - *Defender's strongest argument*: Gate condition (b) (scope coverage) applies to sub-case (b) — pre-ratified deferred wording — but not to sub-case (a) — structural rearrangement. In a collapsed row, an amendment author must determine which sub-case applies before knowing which conditions to satisfy. This determination is logically equivalent to having two rows, but less visible. Governance mechanisms that depend on visibility should be maximally visible; sub-notes communicate history, not mandatory requirements. A collapsed row with a sub-note cannot enforce condition (b) — it can only inform.
    - *Skeptic's strongest argument*: An orchestrator routing a new amendment consults the taxonomy to learn whether deliberation is required, not to understand historical authorization provenance. Separate rows imply separate decision trees. If the deliberation-requirement answer is identical for both rows, separate rows create false complexity — the orchestrator must classify into one of two rows with identical process consequences. The authorization-mechanism distinction is an audit fact, not a routing decision.
  - **Synthesizer assessment**: **The defender's position is stronger on gate-enforcement grounds.** This synthesis explicitly declares the operative question as PS-NR2 requires: the taxonomy answers both "does this amendment need deliberation?" (routing function — all single-PR rows: no, when verbatim preservation holds) AND "what preconditions authorize skipping deliberation?" (gate-enforcement function — varies by row). For the routing function, the deliberation-requirement axis is adequate. For the gate-enforcement function, the authorization-mechanism axis is necessary. The gate conditions formalized in both agents' revised Rec 3 are sub-case-specific: conditions (a)–(c) apply only to the pre-ratified deferred wording sub-case. In a collapsed row, these conditions appear attached to a sub-note rather than to a row — which means an amendment author sees them as historical annotations, not as mandatory requirements. The difference between a row-level requirement ("this row requires condition X") and a sub-note annotation ("this sub-case historically satisfied condition X") is precisely the governance gap that the proliferation guard was designed to close. Separate rows make the gate-enforcement function visible; a collapsed row with a sub-note obscures it. The synthesis resolves Dispute 1 in favor of row retention. The taxonomy's header should state both functions explicitly to prevent future disputes about which axis is operative.
  - **Recommended resolution**: Retain rows 3 and 4 as separate rows. Add an "Authorization basis" column (PD-R2) making the distinguishing property of each row visible at a glance. Add a note below the table: "For routing: all single-PR rows answer 'no deliberation required when verbatim preservation holds.' Authorization basis governs what conditions must be satisfied to invoke each single-PR pathway." Gate conditions from PD-R3/PS-R3 attach to row 4 specifically. Row 3 retains the verbatim-preservation contract as its verification mechanism; the additional conditions (a)–(c) do not apply to row 3's structural-rearrangement use case.

- **Dispute: Provisional label vs. self-consistency-gap acknowledgment**
  - **Positions**: pathway-defender proposes an explicit acknowledgment note naming the self-consistency gap with precision: condition (b) may not be satisfied by v3.1.2's canonical example because the v3.0.0 deliberation's scope coverage of the deferred sentence's substantive claim is not determinable from the governance log as written. (defender disputes.md, second dispute.) pathway-skeptic proposes a "provisional" label for row 4 with a two-example canonicity requirement, as a separate and complementary mechanism. (skeptic disputes.md, Tensions, self-citation severity.)
  - **Arguments**:
    - *Defender's strongest argument*: The acknowledgment note addresses a specific, falsifiable concern. The "provisional" label addresses a different concern — pathway maturity rather than canonical-example self-consistency. A pathway whose canonical example may fail its own gate conditions creates a self-defeating structure: the conditions are strict enough to exclude future uses but not strict enough to require the canonical example to justify itself. Only the acknowledgment note closes this loop with the required precision.
    - *Skeptic's strongest argument*: A pathway with a single self-referential example has not been empirically tested. Provisional status is a forward-looking maturity signal. Both mechanisms address different concerns and should coexist — both cross-reviews explicitly noted this. Implementing only the acknowledgment note without the provisional label leaves future amendment authors without a clear signal that the pathway's canonical example is unverified by an independent second use.
  - **Synthesizer assessment**: Both measures should be implemented. The cross-reviews from both agents explicitly noted these mechanisms address different concerns and can coexist (defender cross-review of skeptic, Tensions, second item; skeptic cross-review of defender, Tensions, fourth item).
