### Position Summary

The cross-review process produced four modifications to original recommendations (Recs 1, 3, 4, 7), three surviving recommendations (Recs 2, 5, 6), zero withdrawals, and two new recommendations both drawn from migration-soundness's findings. No original recommendations are withdrawn: all seven identified genuine concerns, but four required scoping adjustments based on cross-review evidence.

The most significant change in my thinking came from migration-soundness's structural criticism of my characterization of the CONTRIBUTING.md → docs/output-conventions.md cross-reference as "coherent." In Phase 1 I treated the existence of a navigational chain as sufficient evidence of adequate discoverability — once I confirmed the link existed, I logged it as a positive finding and moved on. The distinction migration-soundness articulates — "reachable in principle" versus "encountered in practice" — is concrete and correct: a cross-reference placed after a section break will be missed by readers who stop reading at that break. I was wrong to treat chain existence as a quality assessment of chain placement. This same audit gap explains why I did not discover the mkdocs.yml absence: I confirmed the file existed on disk and stopped there, rather than tracing the file through the documentation build infrastructure to verify it would appear in the rendered site. Both failures share a root cause — I verified existence but not traversability — and both are now corrected via the two new recommendations.

A secondary significant change is the narrowing of my alignment finding on the path (c) attestation. I characterized the SIR's attestation as "accurate," which removal-rigor-skeptic correctly identified as potentially foreclosing their substrate analysis requirements. My endorsement is now narrowed to "procedurally correct in form": the SIR's formal statement that path (c) does not apply to removals is accurate as a definitional matter, but I make no claim that the SIR adequately demonstrates why no substrate analysis was needed for VI and X. This narrowing propagates into Recs 3 and 4, which are now explicitly conditioned on the synthesis arbiter sustaining the analytical validity of the removals — if removal-rigor-skeptic's comparative-principles asymmetry argument succeeds, the navigation fixes that depend on VI's removal being sound should not be cemented in ratified Governance text.

My remaining highest-priority finding is the co-equal P1 pair: fix L1589 (modified Rec 1) and add the mkdocs.yml nav entry (new Rec 1). Both must be resolved before tagging v3.0.0 and neither gates on the other. L1589 makes the Governance section semantically incoherent for every future amendment author who reads it without parsing SIR comment blocks, directing them toward path (c) for principles that were removed via a different mechanism. The mkdocs.yml gap makes docs/output-conventions.md invisible in the primary contributor documentation surface, effectively orphaning the Principle X migration for the entire contributor population that uses the built docs site rather than browsing GitHub directly. These are equally concrete, equally verifiable, and equally correctable before ratification.
