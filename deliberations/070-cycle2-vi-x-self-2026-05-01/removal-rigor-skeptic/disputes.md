### Final Position Statement

**Non-Negotiables**

1. The SIR rationale must be updated to correctly identify VI's failure as Criterion 2 (not Criterion 1), with the explicit conclusion that even correct Criterion 2 identification does not require wording refinement when no viable refinement exists for "drives behavior" — and migrate-out stands independently on that analysis (Rec 3). This is non-negotiable because the current SIR establishes "Criterion 1 failure → migrate-out" as the template in a document that will be cited as governance precedent. Future amendment authors encountering qualitative judgment calls in constitutional language need the complete reasoning chain; the mislabeled shortcut creates systemic precedent debt that compounds across every subsequent deliberation.

2. The SIR must contain a documented Criterion 3 analysis of X's "one clear purpose per output file" sub-bullet before the migrate-out precedent is established, showing the sub-bullet is or is not an independent constitutional claim (Rec 2 modified, option (a)). This is non-negotiable because the v2.6.0 path (c) amendment created the analytical obligation that substrate analysis precede migrate-out decisions — it is precisely what distinguishes migrate-out from premature removal. A migrate-out precedent established without showing the analytical work is not a governed precedent; it is a decision that bypassed the gate it was required to satisfy.

**Flexibility**

1. Comparative-principles asymmetry documentation (Rec 6): I am firm on the requirement that the distinction between "judgment calls with a CI-detectable default class" and "irreducibly judgment-dependent qualifiers" must be documented and accessible to future amendment authors. I am flexible on where — this could appear in the SIR rationale, in the arbiter's note in CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md's governance log entry for v3.0.0, or as a parenthetical in the Governance body text's worked example. The non-negotiable is accessibility to future deliberators; the document is flexible.

2. MUST→SHOULD normative downgrade justification (Rec 4): I prefer option (a) — SIR paragraph explaining that the gate text governs location, not normative weight, and that SHOULD is appropriate because the judgment-call qualifier makes MUST unenforceable in migrated form. I am flexible on whether the justification appears in the SIR or in the governance log entry. What must be preserved: a documented explanation preventing future authors from treating this downgrade as establishing that all constitutionally-migrated guidance automatically weakens to SHOULD.

3. Sequencing dependency (new Rec): I am firm on the principle that the analytical track's conclusions must be stable before the operational completeness verdict is ratified as final. I am flexible on the mechanism — a sequencing note in the synthesis instructions, a staged ratification approach, or an explicit caveat in MS's operational verdict. What must be preserved: the arbiter's ratification record must not present MS's "substantially sound" operational characterization as closing the amendment integrity question while RRS's analytical P1 items remain open.
