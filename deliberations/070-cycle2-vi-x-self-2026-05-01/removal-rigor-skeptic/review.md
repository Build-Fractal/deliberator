### Executive Summary

The v3.0.0 amendment removes Principles VI (Scripts Over Markdown) and X (Zen of Python Output) from the constitution on the grounds that each fails Constitutional Inclusion Criterion 1 (mechanical verification capability). The migration deposits the substantive guidance into CONTRIBUTING.md and docs/output-conventions.md respectively, with normative weight downgraded from MUST to SHOULD. The amendment's formal structure is sound — the SIR is well-documented, the version classification is correct, the receiving documents are named, and the path (c) inapplicability is properly attested.

However, three defects undermine the amendment's integrity. First, the SIR's cross-reference audit produces a false assurance: a live body-text reference to "Principles VI and X" persists in the Governance section at L1589 — not in any SIR comment block — and is now semantically stale in a way that could mislead future amendment authors about which remediation path applies to those principles. Second, the SIR conflates Criterion 1 (mechanical verification capability) with Criterion 2 (falsifiable scope) throughout its rationale for both removals, invoking the wrong criterion to justify the migrate-out outcome while the surviving constitution contains multiple principles with equivalent qualifier-interpretation demands that were not subjected to the same standard. Third, Principle X contained at least two mechanically-checkable sub-bullets ("focused content per file" and "shallow hierarchies") not already subsumed by existing constitutional principles — the same path (c) analysis that saved XVI should have been applied to X's structural substrate before migrate-out was chosen.

**Most important recommendation**: correct the false claim in the SIR's cross-reference audit and repair the stale body-text reference at L1589, which says path (c) is the precedent for "future remediation" of principles that have already been removed via a different mechanism — it is the single error most likely to corrupt a future amendment author's understanding of the remediation-path options.

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **"No substrate exists in X — entire content is judgment guidance" (SIR L121-122)**: The SIR assumes that because "readability counts" is the headline sentence and is subjective, the entire principle body inherits that subjectivity. This is false. Constitutional bodies survive their headlines — the path (c) precedent established for XVI explicitly separates the load-bearing substrate from the animating intent. X's "one clear purpose per output file" sub-bullet does not inherit subjectivity from "readability counts" any more than XVI's "parameter pinning" invariant inherited the subjectivity of "user MUST understand the math." The SIR applies path (c) analysis rigorously to XVI and then declines to apply it to X with no principled distinction between them. The correct understanding is that the presence of a subjective headline sentence is not itself disqualifying — it's the absence of a structural substrate in the body that disqualifies; and that absence must be demonstrated, not asserted.

- **"The qualifier 'when the artifact drives behavior' fails Criterion 1" (SIR L17-21)**: The SIR asserts this is a Criterion 1 failure (mechanical verification). The accurate characterization is a Criterion 2 failure (falsifiable scope). Criterion 1 asks whether a check CAN be built; the answer for VI is yes — a lint detecting non-YAML, non-SKILL.md files in template directories or preset directories is concretely sketchable. Criterion 2 asks whether the wording flags a hypothetical PR without interpretation; the answer is borderline, since "drives behavior" is the judgment-laden qualifier. The SIR uses the Criterion 2 definition ("reviewer must reason 'well, X might be okay if Y'") while attributing it to Criterion 1. This matters because Criterion 1 and Criterion 2 failures have different remediation paths: Criterion 2 failures are candidates for wording refinement under path (c); Criterion 1 failures are not.

- **"Cross-reference audit found zero body-text references to VI or X" (SIR L80-81)**: The SIR asserts that all references to VI/X are confined to SIR comment blocks. This is false. The Governance body text at L1589 contains "path (c) precedent for future remediation of Principles VI and X" — a live, non-comment-block reference. The SIR's regex search for "Principle VI", "Principle X", "Scripts Over Markdown", "Zen of Python" would not catch the phrase "Principles VI and X" (plural, with "and") that appears at L1589. The audit missed the plural form. The claim of completeness is therefore incorrect.
