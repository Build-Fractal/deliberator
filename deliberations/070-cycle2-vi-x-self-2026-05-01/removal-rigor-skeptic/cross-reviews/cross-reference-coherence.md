### Dangerous Contradictions

- **Path (c) attestation: validated versus analytically deficient**
  - **cross-reference-coherence claims**: "Path (c) inapplicability attested (SIR L62-78): The SIR correctly distinguishes path (c) (restructure-and-retain) from migrate-out (remove-and-house-elsewhere). (cross-reference-coherence, Alignment section, 3rd bullet)
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic claims**: "Path (c) for Principle X was not seriously evaluated... The path (c) analysis that identified parameter pinning as XVI's structural substrate should have been applied to X's one-purpose-per-file sub-bullet before the migrate-out decision was made." and "Path (c) for Principle VI not seriously evaluated... if XVII survived the same analysis, VI's dismissal requires a comparative justification the SIR does not provide." (removal-rigor-skeptic, Missed Opportunities, items 1 and 2)
  - **Why this is dangerous**: cross-reference-coherence's validation endorses the path (c) attestation as procedurally and substantively sound. If that endorsement is adopted by synthesis, it forecloses removal-rigor-skeptic's P1 Recommendation 2 (requiring the SIR to demonstrate substrate-ineligibility for X and VI). The result would be a migrate-out precedent established without documented substrate analysis — the exact analytical step that the v2.6.0 XVI amendment made mandatory for path (c) decisions. Every future amendment author who cites this precedent would inherit the shortcut without knowing it was a shortcut.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The two positions can coexist if the synthesis distinguishes procedural accuracy (the attestation is formally correct: path (c) does not apply to removals) from analytical completeness (the attestation does not demonstrate why path (c)'s analytical step — substrate evaluation — was inapplicable to VI and X). cross-reference-coherence should narrow its endorsement from "the attestation is accurate" to "the attestation's procedural form is correct"; removal-rigor-skeptic's substrate analysis requirement then applies to the SIR's rationale, not to the attestation paragraph itself. This preserves both reviews without conflict.
