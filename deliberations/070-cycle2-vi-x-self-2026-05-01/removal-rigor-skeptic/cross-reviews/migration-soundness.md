### Dangerous Contradictions

- **MUST→SHOULD normative downgrade: defect vs. confirmed-clean**
  - **migration-soundness claims**: Recommendation 6 ("Confirm no inadvertent MUSTs in migrated bullet bodies") explicitly states "No change required. Document as confirmed-clean in the v3.0.0 ratification record." The section concludes: "this is a clean finding, documented here for completeness." migration-soundness frames the MUST→SHOULD reduction as a verified-correct outcome of the migration, not a second independent change.
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic claims**: Missed Opportunity 4 and Recommendation 4 argue that the normative downgrade is "unjustified by the gate failure" and constitutes "a second independent change bundled into the migration without justification in the SIR or deliberation record." The gate text determines WHERE guidance lives, not HOW STRONG the obligation is; a MUST-level requirement can live in operational guidance while retaining MUST weight.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: The arbiter receives a "confirmed-clean" finding and a "unjustified bundled change requiring correction" finding on the identical question. If both are adopted, the synthesis simultaneously ratifies the downgrade as correct AND flags it as an unexamined defect. Any implementation directive derived from this synthesis is internally contradictory: a ratification record cannot say both "normative downgrade is confirmed clean" and "normative downgrade requires justification before ratification."
  - **Suggested resolution**: migration-soundness's Recommendation 6 should be scoped narrowly: it verified that no inadvertent MUST-strength language slipped into the migrated bullet *bodies* — a check for unintended MUST usage, not an endorsement of the wholesale MUST→SHOULD policy. That narrower scope does not conflict with removal-rigor-skeptic's argument that the policy itself was unjustified. The arbiter should read Rec 6 as confirming the absence of *accidental* MUSTs, while treating the *deliberate* MUST→SHOULD policy as an open question removal-rigor-skeptic's Rec 4 raises. removal-rigor-skeptic's framing is analytically stronger — the gate text does not require normative downgrade — and migration-soundness should concede the scope of its clean finding.

- **Whether the Principle X migrate-out decision was properly grounded**
  - **migration-soundness claims**: The "Alignment" section accepts the migrate-out decision for X at face value and evaluates only whether the migration was executed faithfully: "Four of the five X bullets survive as main Conventions in docs/output-conventions.md; the fifth — 'errors should never pass silently' — has been repositioned into a separate 'Recommended implementation patterns' subsection." The review's concern is *how* the migration was done, not *whether* migrate-out was the right choice.
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic claims**: Missed Opportunity 1 and Recommendation 2 argue that X's "one clear purpose per output file" sub-bullet is a mechanically-checkable structural invariant that was not evaluated for path (c) elevation before migrate-out was chosen. The SIR's claim "no such substrate exists" (SIR L121-122) is asserted without the analytical step the path (c) precedent makes mandatory.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: migration-soundness's verdict that "The migration is substantially sound" is premised entirely on accepting the migrate-out decision as valid. removal-rigor-skeptic challenges that decision's analytical foundation. If the substrate question overturns the migrate-out decision for X, migration-soundness's execution-quality findings (structural demotion note, mkdocs.yml entry, provenance footer accuracy) become moot for X — they describe how well a migration was executed that should not have happened in its current form. The arbiter cannot ratify the migration's execution quality without first resolving whether the migrate-out decision was correctly reached.
  - **Suggested resolution**: These questions are genuinely separable. The arbiter should rule on the substrate question (Recommendation 2 from removal-rigor-skeptic) first. If that ruling goes against migrate-out, the execution-quality findings are superseded. If the ruling upholds migrate-out (perhaps finding the substrate argument unpersuasive), migration-soundness's execution findings carry full weight. Frame them as sequentially dependent, not mutually contradictory.

- **Overall amendment integrity verdict**
  - **migration-soundness claims**: Executive Summary: "The migration is substantially sound." The review characterizes its most important recommendations as operational (nav entry, explanatory note, cross-reference placement) with a P1 finding centered on discoverability, not constitutional integrity.
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic claims**: Executive Summary: "Three defects undermine the amendment's integrity." The review characterizes its most important finding as a false SIR audit claim (L1589) combined with analytical gaps in the substrate evaluation and criterion-conflation — all constitutional integrity issues that precede operational quality.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: Two reviewers with opposite top-line verdicts will produce paralysis in synthesis if the arbiter does not explicitly choose between "proceed with operational fixes" and "requires analytical rework before ratification." A synthesis that adopts both produces an incoherent action list: "make the nav entry AND redo the criterion analysis AND document the downgrade justification AND accept the amendment as sound." The critical-path ordering is different under each verdict.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The contradictions at the first two items above are the root cause of this meta-contradiction. If those are resolved (substrate question ruled on; normative downgrade policy addressed), the overall verdict naturally follows. The arbiter should not attempt to resolve the meta-verdict directly but instead adjudicate the underlying disputes and derive the verdict from those rulings.

### Tensions

- **L1589 fix scope and framing**
  - **migration-soundness's position**: Recommendation 1 proposes updating L1589 to distinguish the two remediation paths and frames this as a defect in both the body text and the SIR audit claim. The fix is scoped to one sentence replacement in L1589 plus correcting the SIR claim.
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic's position**: Recommendation 1 proposes a similar L1589 update but frames the SIR audit claim failure as a methodological defect in the audit procedure itself — the regex missed plural forms — and Recommendation 8 proposes extending the standard cross-reference audit methodology to prevent recurrence.
  - **Nature of tension**: migration-soundness treats L1589 as a one-off fix; removal-rigor-skeptic treats it as evidence of a systematic audit methodology gap requiring a procedural correction to the standard methodology used in future amendments. Both fixes are additive but scope differently: one corrects the symptom, the other corrects the procedure.
  - **Coordination needed**: The arbiter should adopt both: migration-soundness's L1589 text correction AND removal-rigor-skeptic's methodology extension (Rec 8). These are not in tension operationally, only in framing. The synthesis recommendation should make explicit that the fix covers both the specific instance and the recurring procedure.

- **"Errors should never pass silently" repositioning: explanatory note vs. migration-decision validity**
  - **migration-soundness's position**: Missed Opportunities and Recommendation 2 frame the errors/warnings bullet's placement in Recommended patterns (rather than main Conventions) as a "silent structural demotion" needing an explanatory note. The fix is one sentence in docs/output-conventions.md.
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic's position**: The SIR explicitly documents this repositioning ("the mechanically-checkable parts (e.g., 'warnings for malformed output') flagged as RECOMMENDED implementation patterns rather than constitutional requirements"). removal-rigor-skeptic does not separately challenge the within-document positioning but instead argues that the mechanically-checkable nature of this sub-bullet is precisely why X should have been evaluated for path (c) — "warnings for malformed output" is a CI-lint candidate.
  - **Nature of tension**: migration-soundness accepts the migration decision and asks for internal document transparency about a structural choice. removal-rigor-skeptic uses the mechanically-checkable nature of that same sub-bullet as evidence that the migration decision was premature. The tension is: if removal-rigor-skeptic's substrate argument succeeds, the "explanatory note" fix is insufficient — the sub-bullet belongs back in constitutional scope, not in a Recommended patterns section with an explanatory note. If migration-soundness's framing governs, the note is the right fix.
  - **Coordination needed**: The arbiter's ruling on the substrate question (removal-rigor-skeptic Rec 2) determines which fix is appropriate. If migrate-out stands, migration-soundness's explanatory note is the right operational fix. If migrate-out is challenged, the note addresses a symptom of a larger problem and should not be framed as a sufficient resolution.

- **Criterion 1 vs. Criterion 2 conflation — present or absent in review scope**
  - **migration-soundness's position**: Does not address the Criterion 1 vs. Criterion 2 distinction at all. The review takes the SIR's "failed Criterion 1" framing at face value and evaluates migration quality, not diagnosis validity.
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic's position**: Missed Opportunity 3 and Recommendation 3 identify the SIR's conflation of Criterion 1 (mechanical verification capability) with Criterion 2 (falsifiable scope) as a major analytical defect. The wrong criterion diagnosis produces the wrong remediation: Criterion 2 failures are candidates for wording refinement under path (c); Criterion 1 failures are not.
  - **Nature of tension**: These reviews operate at different analytical levels. migration-soundness audits migration fidelity (assuming the diagnosis is correct); removal-rigor-skeptic audits diagnostic validity (before evaluating migration fidelity). Neither is wrong about its chosen scope, but the scope difference means migration-soundness's "substantially sound" verdict may be evaluating execution quality of a decision built on an incorrect diagnosis.
  - **Coordination needed**: The arbiter should acknowledge that migration-soundness's execution-quality findings are conditionally valid — valid IF the migrate-out decision was correctly diagnosed. Removal-rigor-skeptic's Rec 3 should be addressed as a prerequisite to ratifying migration-soundness's "substantially sound" finding, not as a parallel concern of equal priority.

- **Operational discoverability findings vs. constitutional integrity scope**
  - **migration-soundness's position**: P1 finding (Recommendations 1 and 7) centers on mkdocs.yml nav entry absence and docs/developer-guide/contributing.md cross-reference gap. These are operational discoverability defects.
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic's position**: Does not address mkdocs.yml or the docs site navigation at all. Scope is constitutional integrity — SIR analytical validity, criterion conflation, normative downgrade, cross-reference audit completeness.
  - **Nature of tension**: The two reviews have different scope boundaries, not conflicting conclusions. migration-soundness's discoverability findings are additive to removal-rigor-skeptic's constitutional findings. However, if the constitutional findings from removal-rigor-skeptic require rework before ratification, the discoverability findings from migration-soundness may need to be re-evaluated against a revised document (a different docs/output-conventions.md structure, for instance, if X's content is reorganized).
  - **Coordination needed**: The arbiter should sequence these: constitutional integrity defects (removal-rigor-skeptic's scope) as gating; operational discoverability fixes (migration-soundness's scope) as non-blocking but required before the distribution surface is complete. Principle XXII (Distribution Surface Integrity) applies to both — the constitutional defects do not eliminate the discoverability obligations.

- **Comparative-principles asymmetry argument — raised or deferred**
  - **migration-soundness's position**: Does not address whether VI's "drives behavior" qualifier is the same kind of judgment-laden language present in surviving principles (XV "core artifacts," XXIV "safety-critical," IX "prefer pure functions").
  - **removal-rigor-skeptic's position**: Missed Opportunity 6 and Recommendation 6 argue that without documented rebuttal of the comparative asymmetry, the amendment creates a precedent inconsistency — future deliberations can invoke the VI/X removal as grounds to remove XV or XXIV on identical criteria.
  - **Nature of tension**: migration-soundness's review is silent where removal-rigor-skeptic's is explicit. The silence is not a contradiction but it means migration-soundness provides no defense against the asymmetry argument and no reinforcement for removal-rigor-skeptic's recommendation. The arbiter has only one-sided evidence on this question.
  - **Coordination needed**: The arbiter should treat removal-rigor-skeptic's asymmetry finding as uncontested. migration-soundness's silence on this point is not rebuttal. Recommendation 6 (document the principled distinction between "judgment-laden but structurally anchored" vs. "irreducibly judgment-dependent") should be adopted without requiring further cross-review resolution.
