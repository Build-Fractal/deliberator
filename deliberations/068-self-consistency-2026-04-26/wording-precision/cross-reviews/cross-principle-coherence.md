# Cross-Review of cross-principle-coherence — by wording-precision

## Executive Framing

cross-principle-coherence (CPC) reads XVI as broadly compatible with the constitution's reproducibility commitments but flags coherence drift between XVI's narrowed determinism claim and VII/VIII's unqualified language. My own review (wording-precision, WP) reads XVI as locally honest about the three-stage taxonomy but normatively weak — descriptive prose where MUST verbs are needed, undefined operational terms ("deliberation run", "fully-pinned", "cross-run variance"). The two reviews are largely complementary: CPC catalogues the cross-principle reference graph that XVI does not draw; WP catalogues the within-principle verbs that XVI does not use. Where we touch the same surface (the VII↔XVI tension, the cache-as-state question, the solver-equivalence claim) we agree on the diagnosis but propose different fix layers — CPC at cross-references, WP at normative verbs. That layered overlap is mostly safe but creates two genuine integration risks if both reviews are adopted naively.

### Dangerous Contradictions

1. **Where the VII↔XVI exception should be authored.** CPC's Recommendation #1 ("Explicitly scope Principle VII's 'structurally identical output' claim," Actionable Recommendations) and Missed Opportunity bullet 1 push the fix into **Principle VII** — narrow VII to acknowledge XVI's carve-out. WP's Recommendation #3 ("Resolve the VII ↔ XVI cross-run determinism tension explicitly", Actionable Recommendations) puts the fix into **the v2.3.2 clarification block in XVI**, leaving VII's text untouched. Why dangerous: an editor adopting both could end up with the exception authored in *two* places (VII narrows itself; XVI declares the carve-out), which is exactly the failure mode Principle XI prohibits ("If you find yourself writing the same fact in two places, stop"). Suggested resolution: pick one home for the exception. WP's location is lower-risk because v2.3.2 is the amendment in flight; CPC's is more honest because VII is the principle being narrowed. Recommend co-locating: put the carve-out in XVI's clarification (WP) and add a single back-reference parenthetical in VII (e.g., "(narrowed by Principle XVI for LLM gap-filling output)") — never the full carve-out text in both.

2. **Whether the parameter cache is "ambient state" or "sanctioned persistence."** CPC's Missed Opportunity bullet 2 says XVI's cache "*is* persisted state that conditions later behavior" and asks XVI to cross-reference VII line 167 ("No ambient state or hidden context") and explicitly distinguish the cache from prohibited ambient state. WP did not raise the cache-vs-VII-167 angle; WP's Recommendation #6 only asks for storage location and lifetime ("persisted to the run's output directory and reused for the lifetime of that directory"). These do not strictly contradict, but **CPC characterizes the cache as VII-prohibited-by-default-needs-explicit-sanction; WP characterizes it as benign-needs-only-location-disclosure.** If a future editor adopts WP's location-only fix without CPC's sanction language, a careful reader can still cite VII line 167 to argue the cache is forbidden ambient state. Suggested resolution: combine — WP supplies *where* the cache lives, CPC supplies *why* its existence does not violate VII ("the cache is keyed and explicit; ambient state would not be"). Both sentences belong in the v2.3.2 clarification, not split across reviews.

3. **What stage 3's "bit-for-bit" claim actually covers re: solver substitution.** CPC's Off-Base bullet 4 and Recommendation #10 push back on lines 488-489 ("Changing solvers MUST NOT change what is being optimized") on numerical-stability grounds — the *result* of optimization may differ across solvers even when the objective is identical, so the constitution should not present solver-equivalence as definitional. WP's Alignment bullet 3 *praises* "bit-for-bit" (line 470) as "the right level of precision for a determinism claim about mechanical assembly" without distinguishing the assembled-objective claim (line 470) from the solver-equivalence claim (line 489). Why dangerous: CPC tightens the solver claim; WP implicitly endorses it as already precise. An editor reading both could leave line 489 alone (citing WP) while accepting CPC's other recommendations — preserving the strong claim CPC actually wants weakened. Suggested resolution: WP should clarify that its "bit-for-bit" praise applies only to **stage 3 mechanical assembly of the objective function**, not to solver output. CPC's Recommendation #10 stands; WP's Alignment bullet 3 needs the scope qualifier.

4. **No additional contradictions identified.** The remaining overlaps are tensions, not contradictions.

### Tensions

1. **Fix layer: cross-references vs normative verbs.** CPC's recommendations are dominantly cross-reference additions (XI, XXIV, V, II, III, X — eight of ten). WP's recommendations are dominantly verb upgrades and operational definitions (MUST/SHOULD insertions, "run" defined, agent named, "fully-pinned" defined — eight of ten). Nature of tension: both fix the same underlying weakness — XVI is descriptive where it should be normative — but at different layers. If both are adopted in isolation the clarification block becomes congested (a paragraph of MUSTs *and* a paragraph of cross-references). Coordination needed: sequence the edits — adopt WP's verb upgrades first inside the existing 3-stage breakdown (lines 460-470), then add CPC's cross-references as parentheticals or a "Cross-references" subsection at the end of XVI rather than inline. Avoids paragraph bloat.

2. **"Cross-run variance is acceptable" — bounding mechanism.** WP's Recommendation #5 bounds it by **what varies** (parameter values may vary; objective shape, parameter names, template selection MUST NOT vary). CPC's Missed Opportunity bullet 1 bounds it by **when it applies** (VII's "structurally identical" applies post-pinning; not on first run). Both are valid; they bound different axes. Coordination: combine into one sentence in the clarification — "Cross-run variance in resolved parameter *values* is acceptable; cross-run variance in the assembled objective function's *shape* (parameter names, template selection, gap-identifier set) is prohibited. Once parameters are pinned, subsequent runs MUST reproduce the assembled objective bit-for-bit (Principle VII applies)."

3. **Whether the "future PR that re-resolves parameters" prohibition needs a test layer.** CPC's Missed Opportunity bullet 8 routes the prohibition through **Principle XXIV** (Safety-Critical Defense-in-Depth — "Contract test reproducing the failure scenario") and recommends XVI cross-reference XXIV. WP's Recommendation #1 promotes the prohibition to a MUST inside XVI itself but does not assign test-layer enforcement. Tension: CPC wants enforcement codified via XXIV; WP wants the rule made enforceable via verb strength. Both are needed (a MUST without a test obligation is a paper rule; a test obligation without a MUST has nothing to test). Coordination: WP's verbs land first; CPC's XXIV cross-reference is a separate parenthetical ("Enforcement: Principle XXIV applies") at the end of the clarification.

4. **"Mechanical" vs "deterministic" terminology drift.** CPC's Alignment bullet 2 and Recommendation #8 note that "mechanical assembly" (XVI line 458/470) borrows VIII's umbrella term, but VIII (lines 178, 188-190) treats "mechanical" and "deterministic" as roughly co-extensive — XVI is now drawing a finer distinction VIII does not articulate. WP did not surface this terminology drift; WP took XVI's stage labels at face value as "carrying their determinism property in-line" (Alignment bullet 2). Tension: CPC's framing exposes a Principle VIII coherence gap that WP missed; WP's framing implicitly assumed VIII would be amended in lockstep. Coordination needed: agree that VIII either gets a parenthetical in v2.3.2 ("note: 'mechanical' subsumes the post-pinning determinism XVI describes") or accept the drift as deliberate and document it once in XVI's clarification. Punting silently is the worst option.

5. **Stage 2 splitting (question generation vs answer extraction).** WP's Recommendation #7 calls for explicit disambiguation — "stage 2 encompasses both (a) translating gap identifiers into natural-language questions for the user, and (b) extracting parameter values from the user's natural-language answers. Both substeps are stochastic; both are pinned together." CPC does not raise this split at all — its "LLM gap-filling" treatment is monolithic. Tension: WP's split adds precision but increases the principle's surface area; CPC's silence implies one stochastic step suffices. Coordination: WP's split is the more defensible position (the prose at lines 473-475 names two distinct LLM activities) but the fix is local — a single parenthetical inside stage 2 — and does not require CPC to amend its review.

### Safe Agreements

1. **The cache needs an explicit storage location.** Confidence: **high.** WP Recommendation #6 ("persisted to the run's output directory and reused for the lifetime of that directory") and CPC Missed Opportunity bullet 4 ("XVI should at minimum note that pinned parameters land in a deterministic location in the output tree, e.g., next to `summary/final.md`") converge on the same fix. Combined evidence: a reviewer auditing a PR cannot verify pinning behavior without knowing where the cache lives; both reviews independently flagged this gap. The shared position is strong enough to land in v2.3.2 without further deliberation.

2. **The v2.3.2 amendment is correctly scoped and does not creep.** Confidence: **high.** WP's Scope Creep Check section explicitly states "No scope creep detected" — the candidate touches only XVI's first bullet and the appended clarification. CPC's review confines itself to XVI and its cross-references and never argues other principles need amendment as part of v2.3.2 (only that they should be cross-referenced from XVI). Combined evidence: the amendment's surface area is honest about what it changes; the integration concerns are about coordination with neighboring principles, not about hidden scope.

3. **The clarification block uses "claims" where it should use normative verbs.** Confidence: **high.** WP Recommendation #4 explicitly calls out "Principle XVI claims..." (line 491) and proposes "requires." CPC's Off-Base bullet 2 makes the same observation in different words: "The parenthetical reads like a reference but is functionally a unilateral redefinition" — i.e., XVI is doing normative work in non-normative language. Combined evidence: both reviews independently identify the soft-language pattern as a precision failure. This is a one-line fix and should land in v2.3.2.

4. **The pinning behavior needs a verifiable artifact (spec/test reference).** Confidence: **medium.** WP Recommendation #8 asks the Origin block to cite a specific FR and contract test ("Pinning behavior is specified in spec 013 FR-{N} and verified by test {path}"). CPC Off-Base bullet 1 makes the structurally same argument: "XVI's wording presents 'cached values are reused' as a property when it is actually a policy that has no enforcement principle backing it elsewhere in the document." Combined evidence: both reviews flag that XVI is making claims it cannot back up from constitutional text alone. Confidence is medium rather than high because CPC frames the fix as adding enforcement language inside XVI, while WP frames it as citing an external spec FR — both are valid but require slightly different edits. The shared diagnosis is robust.
