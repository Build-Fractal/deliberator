# Cross-Review of cross-principle-coherence by pr-evidence-grounding

## Framing

Cross-principle-coherence (hereafter "coherence") and I converged from different angles on the same suspicion: the v2.3.2 amendment to Principle XVI is well-intentioned but underspecified at its boundaries. Coherence approaches it from inside the constitution's text — which principles XVI silently narrows, which cross-references it should make. I approach it from the codebase — what the pinning discipline actually corresponds to in `conversus/schemas/construction.py` and spec 014. The two views are largely complementary; the points below are where they reinforce, push against, or safely agree.

### Dangerous Contradictions

1. **Coherence treats XVI's pinning discipline as a textual property to be reconciled with VII; my review treats it as an unimplemented behavior to be hedged or grounded.** Coherence's recommendation to "explicitly scope Principle VII's 'structurally identical output' claim" (Actionable Rec #1) and to add an XI cross-reference (Rec #2) presumes the pinning discipline is real and just needs better internal wiring. My review's finding — that no cache layer exists in `conversus/schemas/construction.py:139-171` and the actual mechanism is artifact-based persistence via `objective.yml` (spec 014 FR-012) — would make those textual reconciliations premature. If the principle's mechanism is misnamed at its core (cache vs. artifact), tightening its cross-references entrenches the misnaming. The two reviews need to be sequenced: ground the mechanism first, then wire the cross-references.

2. **Coherence's "off-base assumption" about caching policy lacking enforcement (its lines 33-34) goes further than my review and may understate the issue.** Coherence says "no other principle currently mandates this caching behavior" — true, but the deeper issue I surfaced is that *no code* implements it either. Coherence's framing ("the cache *is* persisted state … keyed and explicit") implicitly treats the cache as existing. It does not. Treating a nonexistent cache as "sanctioned persistence" (Coherence Rec #4) would write a constitutional sanction for infrastructure that does not yet exist — which Principle XII (No Dead Infrastructure) prohibits. Coherence's own framework cuts against its recommendation here.

3. **Coherence's solver-substitution observation (its lines 37, 50) and my "optimizer layer is partly aspirational" finding describe the same gap from opposite sides.** Coherence flags that lines 488-489 ("Changing solvers MUST NOT change what is being optimized") makes a numerical-stability claim the constitution doesn't otherwise discuss. I noted that no `conversus/optimizer/`, `nashopt/`, or `ampl/` directory exists yet. Both observations point at the same hole: XVI is asserting properties of a runtime that is partly speced and partly built. Coherence wants the wording tightened; I want the aspirational status declared. These can compose — declare aspirational status AND tighten the equivalence claim — but if only one happens, the principle still claims more than the codebase can deliver.

### Tensions

1. **Coherence's recommendation to cross-reference Principle XXIV for re-resolution enforcement (its Rec #3) presumes a contract test exists or can be written today.** XXIV requires "Contract test reproducing the failure scenario" (constitution lines 730-733). My review found no within-run pinning test in `engine/tests/`. So the XXIV cross-reference would currently land on an empty enforcement layer. The two reviews could combine here — coherence's recommendation plus my "file a separate spec or follow-up issue to add a CI lint detecting re-entrant `GapFiller.fill()` calls" (my Rec #10) — but only if the cross-reference is paired with a follow-up obligation, not asserted as if XXIV already covers it.

2. **Coherence's observability recommendation (Rec #6, invoking Principle V) is a stronger version of my "provenance auditability" suggestion (my Missed Opportunity #5).** Coherence wants a phase report line ("parameters resolved: 7 pinned"). I pointed at `SourceProvenance.filled_by` (`construction.py:262-279`) as the existing hook. These are compatible but layered: V's phase reporting is the user-facing surface; `filled_by` is the underlying data. If only one is added, V's reporting line without the `filled_by` data underneath is performative; `filled_by` without surfacing in V is invisible.

3. **Coherence's "mechanical assembly" terminology observation (its line 14, treating it as locally coherent with VIII) and my finding that `construction.py:8-14` still calls stage 3 "deterministic" disagree on whether the rename is settled.** Coherence reads VIII's "mechanical template-driven behavior" (line 178) as already covering "mechanical assembly." I read the live docstring in code as evidence that the rename has not propagated. Both readings are defensible — VIII's umbrella does sanction "mechanical" — but the constitution and the source file now disagree on the stage-3 label. Coherence's "Acknowledge the … terminology shift in Principle VIII" (Rec #8, P3) and my "Reconcile … terminology drift" (my Rec #3, P1) are the same recommendation at different priorities. The priority gap is the tension worth flagging.

4. **Coherence's framing of pinned parameters as a "stable interface" needing II classification (Rec #5) collides with my finding that the artifact is `objective.yml`, not a runtime cache.** If the pinning artifact is `objective.yml` (spec 014 FR-012), the stable-interface surface already exists — `objective.yml`'s schema is the contract, and II governs it. Coherence's recommendation reads more naturally if the artifact is named correctly. Without grounding the mechanism, the II classification floats.

### Safe Agreements

1. **Both reviews independently flag that XVI's silence on Principle VII's scope is a problem.** Coherence calls this its "largest coherence risk" (its line 7). I called it a Principle VII reconciliation gap (my Rec #9). Neither of us thinks the current parenthetical "(Principle VII applies)" is sufficient. This is the highest-confidence shared finding.

2. **Both reviews independently flag that the v2.3.2 clarification block is structurally well-formed but underspecifies enforcement.** Coherence praises the clarification as "the strongest part of the amendment from a cross-principle perspective" (its line 9) while noting it doesn't designate which test layer enforces the prohibition. I called the clarification's "testable invariant" well-formed but flagged that it has no current enforcement. We agree on both the strength and the gap.

3. **Both reviews independently point at the LLM gap-filling step as the locus of the wording problem, not the symbolic-parsing or mechanical-assembly stages.** Coherence's whole argument circulates around stage 2's stochastic admission. My grounding work confirmed stages 1 and 3 are testably present; stage 2 is the unimplemented one. Convergence on which stage the conversation should focus on is itself useful for the orchestrator's synthesis.

4. **Both reviews implicitly endorse the candidate's structure while disputing specific claims.** Neither of us recommends scrapping the three-stage taxonomy or the v2.3.2 clarification approach. Coherence calls the inversion ("pin and cache its output" rather than "make the LLM deterministic") a strength; I called the testable invariant well-formed. The disagreements are about scope, mechanism naming, and cross-references — not about the amendment's premise.
