- **VI and X Migration Verdicts Are Opposed**

  - **Why this is dangerous**: If both positions enter the synthesis without resolution, the governance log will contain a direct verdict contradiction for VI and X. Any SIR that cites both reviews as support for the migration will be factually incoherent — the reviews produce opposite outcomes on the same criteria. A synthesis agent trying to integrate both would have to either silently suppress one verdict or produce an irresolvable split record, which is precisely the audit-trail failure spec 070 §9 AC #5 was designed to prevent.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The synthesis must pick a lane on the IX/XV/XXIV distinction. gate-faithfulness's Missed Opportunities section (IX asymmetry item) provides the structural argument that rescues the migration: IX's "prefer" clause accompanies a dense block of mechanical MUST requirements; VI's scope-qualifier is the only trigger for VI's sole prescription with no accompanying mechanical MUSTs. The synthesis should adopt gate-faithfulness's structural distinction and explicitly refute the contrarian's "incorrectly decided" conclusion — not merely note the tension — because leaving it open falsifies gate-faithfulness's "verdicts are sound" claim.

### Tensions

- **Operational Guidance as Adequate Home for Mechanically-Checkable Requirements**
  - **gate-faithfulness's position**: The migration's goal is reclassification, not enforcement downgrade. The mechanically-checkable sub-requirements of VI and X — load-trigger lint, summary/final.md parity test — should be preserved in the receiving operational guidance documents at full normative force, making the migration a net-neutral enforcement move (gate-faithfulness, Actionable Recommendations 1-2: "Discard it during migration downgrades enforcement without a compensating benefit... Operational guidance is the correct home for 'judgment calls'... it can carry MUST requirements for judgment-laden behavior").
  - **contrarian-vi-x-xvi-defender's position**: The constitution is the home for invariants precisely because invariants verified by CI belong at constitutional level. If a sub-bullet is independently CI-verifiable, that is the argument for constitutional retention, not for migration with a preservation note (contrarian, Actionable Recommendation 2: "Retain Principle X under renamed headline 'Output Structure Invariants'" and Recommendation 1: "Retain Principle VI with directory-resolution clarification").
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews agree that mechanically-checkable requirements should survive migration at full normative force. They disagree on where that enforcement lives — constitutional vs. operational guidance. gate-faithfulness treats constitutional level as reserved for invariants whose *full text* passes the gate; the contrarian treats CI-verifiability as sufficient justification for constitutional retention regardless of whether surrounding text is non-mechanical. These represent genuinely different theories of what the constitution is for.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should make explicit which theory the gate adopts. If the gate is "the whole principle passes or the whole principle migrates" (gate-faithfulness's reading), the enforcement-preservation recommendations are a migration hygiene requirement. If the gate is "any principle with at least one CI-verifiable claim passes Criterion 1" (contrarian's reading), the gate becomes easier to satisfy. The gate's own text — "at least one form of automated check MUST be feasible" — supports the contrarian's reading for Criterion 1 specifically, which gate-faithfulness's Alignment section does not address.
