### Final Position Statement

**Non-Negotiables**

1. **The XVII-classification table with normative-strength columns is a required appendix in every migration SIR and the corresponding CONSTITUTIONAL_CONVERSATIONS.md deliberation entry.** The routing errors identified for VI (execution-logic sub-bullet routed to CONTRIBUTING.md) and the normative-strength concern applicable to X are structurally undetectable without this table. Making it mandatory converts what was a Phase 2 discovery in a specific deliberation into a durable process safeguard. All three agents converge on this at P1 priority. (CPC revision, Recommendation 8 modified; GF new recommendation at P1; CVXD new recommendation at P1.)

2. **X's error-handling sub-bullet must not be deleted.** The textual analysis confirms it has distinct scope from V: "missing documents" and "edge cases" are independent coverage categories V does not enumerate. Deleting this sub-bullet removes genuine enforcement content with no substitute in any destination document. If X migrates, the sub-bullet must be routed to a runtime-consumed document; if X is retained via path-(c) refactoring, the sub-bullet remains as constitutional content pending the specialization verification CVXD's revised Recommendation 2 requires. GF's deletion instruction is the only outcome that permanently eliminates the coverage; CPC cannot accept that without a credible argument that V's text already covers it — which the texts do not support. (CPC revision, Recommendation 3 modified.)

**Flexibility**

1. **The exact format and column structure of the XVII-classification table.** The table must capture XVII classification, required destination document, and normative-strength disposition for each sub-bullet. The precise column labels, ordering, whether disposition and classification are combined or separate, and the validation mechanism (CI lint vs. reviewer checklist) are negotiable. The non-negotiable is that all three dimensions appear in a single auditable artifact — not scattered across prose — so future reviewers can verify routing decisions without reconstructing the analysis.

2. **The timing requirement for the XVI grandfathering-disclosure annotation.** GF's revised Recommendation 4 requires the annotation to land in the same amendment as the migration SIR to prevent a window where the disclosure misleads reviewers about XVI's gate status. CPC's revised Recommendation 1 specifies composite content without prescribing same-amendment timing. If same-amendment sequencing is GF's mechanism for closing the misleading-disclosure window, CPC accepts that constraint. What is not flexible is the composite annotation's content: it must accurately reflect XVI's full compliance history — that the pre-cycle-1 body's three numbered rules were Criterion-2-compliant before the cycle-1 refactor, and that v2.3.2 further strengthened Criterion 1 via SourceProvenance.filled_by — rather than a uniform gate-failure characterization that overstates the current deficiency to the plain-language clause alone.
