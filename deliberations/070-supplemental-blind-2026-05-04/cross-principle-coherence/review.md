### Executive Summary

This review audits the v2.5.0 pre-migration constitution snapshot for cross-principle coherence, specifically examining whether the v2.4.0 gate's Constitutional Inclusion Criteria were applied consistently to Principles VI, X, and XVI, and whether spec 070's migration routing decisions hold up against the gate's own worked examples and Principle XVII's content-classification taxonomy.

The gate text and its grandfathering disclosure are internally honest: they identify VI, X, and XVI as failures before specifying their destinations. The cross-principle coherence problem is not in the gate logic itself but in three downstream gaps: (1) XVI's v2.3.2 extension silently transformed a gate-failing principle into a partially Criterion-1-compliant one without revisiting the grandfathering judgment; (2) the migration routing decisions for VI and X were not tested against Principle XVII's execution-logic versus contribution-guideline taxonomy, producing detectable stream-crossing in both cases; and (3) the gate's worked examples calibrate only failures, leaving future amendment authors without a positive-anchor case for borderline principles.

The most important recommendation is to separate XVI's plain-language explanation sub-requirement (which genuinely fails Criterion 1) from XVI's determinism-scope content (which the v2.3.2 extension made mechanically verifiable), and to route them independently rather than treating XVI as a uniform gate-failure.

---

### Alignment

- **Gate worked-examples as calibration anchors** (Governance section, "Constitutional Inclusion Criteria," paragraphs 1–4): The gate's two worked examples — "code should be readable" (Criterion 1 failure, no automated check feasible) and "all template variables MUST be lowercase" (Criterion 3 failure, composes from IX + XI) — function correctly as calibration instruments. They occupy opposite ends of the failure spectrum, which means reviewers can triangulate borderline principles by asking which example a candidate principle more closely resembles. The gate text correctly flags these as examples of content that "belongs in operational guidance," not as errors to be deleted.

- **Grandfathering disclosure specificity** (Governance section, grandfathering paragraph): The disclosure names the failing sub-requirements precisely: VI's "judgment-laden 'when the artifact drives behavior,'" X's "irreducibly subjective 'readability counts,'" and XVI's "plain-language explanation requirement is unverifiable." This specificity is correct gate application — it isolates the failing clause rather than condemning the entire principle. The problem (addressed below) is that this precision was not carried forward into migration routing.

- **XVI carve-out in Principle VII** (Principle VII, first bullet): The parenthetical "(narrowed by Principle XVI for LLM gap-filling output)" codifies that VII and XVI have a bilateral relationship, not a composability relationship. Two principles that compose reduce to one; two principles that carve out each other's scope remain distinct. This bilateral carve-out is the structural evidence that XVI is not composable with VII — which has direct bearing on whether path (c) for XVI was the right move.

- **XVII's content-classification taxonomy** (Principle XVII, first three bullets): XVII's execution-logic versus contribution-guideline distinction is the correct analytical framework for migration routing. The gate text in the Governance section cross-references XVII ("The gate inherits XVII's vocabulary; routing decisions to operational guidance follow XVII's execution-logic vs. contribution-guidelines distinction"). This cross-reference is correctly placed but, as shown below, was not applied to the actual routing of VI and X.

- **XXVII/XV bracketing language** (Principle XXVII, "Coordination with Principle XV" paragraph): The explicit bracketing — "plugins extend the registry; operators filter the registered set" — demonstrates the constitution's established pattern for principles that are complementary but distinct: they are retained separately with cross-references, not merged. This pattern is directly relevant to the XVI/VII/VIII question: XVI's relationship to VII and VIII follows the same complementary-but-distinct structure.

### Missed Opportunities

- **No partial-compliance status for upgraded grandfathered principles**: The grandfathering disclosure assigns VI, X, and XVI a uniform gate-failure status. But XVI received a v2.3.2 extension that introduced mechanically verifiable requirements: `objective.yml` persistence, MUST NOT re-invoke LLM within a run, `SourceProvenance.filled_by` audit hook, XXIV enforcement cross-reference, and the explicit falsification clause. These requirements pass Criterion 1 under the same standard that qualifies XXIV's "contract test reproducing the failure scenario." The constitution has no mechanism to reflect "grandfathered but partially upgraded by subsequent extension," which means XVI's gate status is invisibly more favorable than the disclosure suggests. A `**Partial upgrade note (v2.3.2)**` annotation on the grandfathering disclosure entry for XVI would close this gap. Impact: **high** — without this annotation, spec 070 cycle 2 may migrate XVI's mechanically-verifiable content along with the genuinely failing plain-language clause.

- **XVII taxonomy not applied to migration routing**: The gate text says "routing decisions to operational guidance follow XVII's execution-logic vs. contribution-guidelines distinction," but neither VI's routing to CONTRIBUTING.md nor X's routing to docs/output-conventions.md is validated against XVII's three-bullet classification test. Per XVII: "A rule that is enforced at runtime is execution logic even if it also serves as a contribution guideline." VI's sub-bullet "Orchestration logic belongs in SKILL.md (executable spec) and templates" is a runtime enforcement rule (SKILL.md-directed content is consumed by the agent runtime). Routing this to CONTRIBUTING.md violates XVII. Impact: **high** — the stream-crossing is structural, not editorial, and produces invisible runtime guidance.

- **X's error-handling sub-bullet orphaned from Principle V**: X's third bullet — "Errors should never pass silently — warnings are emitted for malformed output, missing documents, and edge cases" — is substantively identical to V's third bullet: "Output validation emits warnings for malformed output but does NOT block file writes." Migrating X to docs/output-conventions.md strands a runtime-enforcement rule (XVII: execution logic) in a contribution-guideline document and creates a duplicate of V's content in a separate file, violating XI (Single Source of Truth). The correct migration for this sub-bullet is deletion (it is already in V) rather than relocation. Impact: **medium** — this creates future drift between docs/output-conventions.md and V's canonical wording.

- **Gate worked examples are failure-only**: Both worked examples ("code should be readable," "all template variables MUST be lowercase") demonstrate failures. Future amendment authors have no positive anchor case illustrating a borderline principle that narrowly passes the gate. Principle XXVIII's own SIR provides one such case (diff-shape consistency check passing Criterion 1 "with acknowledged residual"), but it is not cited in the gate text as a calibration example. Adding a passing borderline example to the gate's worked-examples paragraph would reduce the risk of over-migration (discarding principles that narrowly pass). Impact: **medium**.

- **Criterion 1 vs. Criterion 2 conflation in the XVI grandfathering rationale**: The grandfathering disclosure says XVI fails on "plain-language explanation requirement is unverifiable." This is a Criterion 2 failure (the requirement is not falsifiable enough to flag a violating PR without interpretation) as much as a Criterion 1 failure. The distinction matters because Criterion 1 failure means no automated check is feasible, while Criterion 2 failure means the scope is too vague — these point to different remediation paths (remove the clause vs. tighten the wording). The disclosure conflates them. Impact: **low** — editorial, but creates ambiguity about whether tightening XVI's plain-language wording would rehabilitate it.

- **Migration stream-crossing for VI sub-bullets is undocumented**: VI's sub-bullet "Configuration belongs in YAML (`conversus.yml`, preset files), not in markdown tables or inline instructions" is an execution-logic constraint on configuration file format, not a contribution guideline. Migrating it to CONTRIBUTING.md routes execution logic to a non-runtime document, an XVII violation. The SIR for the migration should document this exception explicitly or split VI's sub-bullets into their correct destinations. Impact: **medium**.

- **No meta-test requirement for gate compliance**: Principle XXVI requires meta-tests that assert parametrize lists cover the full capability set. An analogous meta-test for the gate itself — asserting that every grandfathered principle has an entry in the grandfathering disclosure — does not exist. As principles are added and grandfathered principles are migrated, the disclosure will drift. A CI lint checking "every principle listed as grandfathered in the disclosure has a corresponding body in the constitution (or a migration SIR recording its removal)" would close this. Impact: **low**.

### Off-Base Assumptions

- **XVI is treated as a uniform gate-failure**: The grandfathering disclosure (Governance section, paragraph 2) treats XVI as failing the gate without distinguishing between the original plain-language clause and the v2.3.2 extension's mechanically verifiable content. The assumption is that a principle that had a failing clause remains a gate-failure after a subsequent extension adds passing-clause content. This is incorrect. The v2.3.2 extension introduced: (1) the explicit determinism-scope carve-out from VII with named artifact paths; (2) the `objective.yml` pinning MUST; (3) the `SourceProvenance.filled_by` audit hook; (4) the Principle XXIV enforcement cross-reference; and (5) the explicit falsification clause ("A future PR that re-resolves parameters mid-deliberation violates this principle"). Items 3, 4, and 5 satisfy Criterion 1 by the same standard XXIV's "contract test reproducing the failure scenario" satisfies it. The correct understanding is: XVI has a mixed status — one sub-clause (plain-language explanations) fails the gate, the remainder passes. Whole-principle migration treats the failing clause as a majority when it is a minority.

- **Principle VI's "drives behavior" predicate is comparably vague to "readable"**: The prompt's framing and the grandfathering disclosure implicitly categorize VI as failing Criterion 1 in the same way "code should be readable" does. This is not quite right. VI's operative predicate is "consumed by automation or agents to make decisions" — which is more concrete than "readable" because it admits a structural check: enumerate files consumed by the agent runtime, verify none are freeform markdown. The check requires knowing the consumer list, but that list is bounded (SKILL.md, conversus.yml, preset files, reference files, templates). "Readable" has no analogous bounded check. VI's Criterion 1 failure is real but is on the borderline between "no feasible check" and "feasible with known input set" — a distinction the gate text's worked examples do not draw. The correct understanding: VI fails Criterion 2 more cleanly than Criterion 1 (the "when the artifact drives behavior" trigger requires judgment, not just enumeration).

### Actionable Recommendations

1. **Annotate XVI's grandfathering entry with partial-upgrade status** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: The grandfathering disclosure (Governance section, paragraph 2) lists XVI as failing on "plain-language explanation requirement is unverifiable" with no distinction between the original clause and the v2.3.2 extension.
   - **Proposed change**: Insert after the XVI grandfathering line: "*Partial upgrade (v2.3.2)*: the determinism-scope content (objective.yml pinning, GapFiller.fill() protocol boundary, SourceProvenance.filled_by audit hook, XXIV enforcement cross-reference) satisfies Criterion 1 by the same standard as Principle XXIV. The plain-language explanation clause (second bullet of XVI, first paragraph) remains a Criterion 2 failure. Migration, if any, MUST target only the plain-language clause — not the determinism-scope content."
   - **Rationale**: Mixed-status grandfathered principles need explicit tracking to prevent wholesale migration of mechanically-verifiable content. This is the same precision the disclosure applies to individual failing clauses in the original grandfathering text.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Spec 070 cycle 2 migrates XVI's determinism-scope content alongside the plain-language clause, stranding XXIV's enforcement cross-reference and the VII bilateral carve-out in a non-constitutional document, orphaning enforcement contracts.

2. **Apply Principle XVII taxonomy to VI migration routing** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: VI is routed wholesale to CONTRIBUTING.md. VI's sub-bullets include "Orchestration logic belongs in SKILL.md" and "Configuration belongs in YAML" — both are runtime-enforcement constraints (Principle XVII: execution logic).
   - **Proposed change**: Split VI's content along the XVII taxonomy before migration: sub-bullets that constrain what goes into SKILL.md, conversus.yml, and templates are execution logic and belong in SKILL.md operational guidance (or the relevant reference file). Sub-bullets that guide format choice for new artifacts ("When a markdown artifact exists purely for human orientation, markdown is appropriate") are contribution guidelines and belong in CONTRIBUTING.md.
   - **Rationale**: XVII explicitly states "A rule that is enforced at runtime is execution logic even if it also serves as a contribution guideline. The authoritative source MUST be in SKILL.md/references; AGENTS.md MAY reference it but MUST NOT redefine it." CONTRIBUTING.md is AGENTS.md-equivalent here.
   - **Risk if ignored**: VI's execution-logic sub-bullets become invisible to the agent runtime, which reads SKILL.md but not CONTRIBUTING.md — the exact silent-failure mode XVII was created to prevent.

3. **Delete X's error-handling sub-bullet rather than migrate it** (Priority: P1)
   - **Current state**: X's third bullet ("Errors should never pass silently — warnings are emitted for malformed output, missing documents, and edge cases") is being migrated to docs/output-conventions.md.
   - **Proposed change**: Delete this sub-bullet from X during migration; do not include it in docs/output-conventions.md. Record in the migration SIR: "X bullet 3 deleted — substantively covered by Principle V bullet 3 ('Output validation emits warnings for malformed output but does NOT block file writes'). Migration would create XI (Single Source of Truth) violation."
   - **Rationale**: XI requires exactly one authoritative source per fact. V already owns this requirement. Two documents carrying the same rule creates drift, not coverage.
   - **Risk if ignored**: docs/output-conventions.md and V's text diverge over time; future amendments to one do not update the other; reviews produce conflicting verdicts on the same behavior.

4. **Add a passing borderline worked example to the gate text** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: The gate's worked-examples paragraph (Governance section, "Constitutional Inclusion Criteria," paragraph after the three criteria) contains only two failure examples.
   - **Proposed change**: Add a third example of a principle that narrowly passes: "A principle proposing 'every newly added pytest.skip() directive MUST cite a bug number and remediation timeline' passes Criterion 1 (lint matching `(issue|PR|#\d+)` plus a timeline cue is feasible), passes Criterion 2 (the scope is specific enough to flag an uncited skip without interpretation), and passes Criterion 3 (the skip-citation discipline is not addressable by composing IX, XXIV, or XXV). See Principle XXVIII for the ratified example."
   - **Rationale**: The gate's calibration is asymmetric — failure examples without a passing-borderline anchor cause reviewers to apply an overly strict standard and over-migrate principles with partial compliance. XXVIII's SIR already documents this exact case and can be cited.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future amendments on borderline principles either under-apply the gate (accepting principles that fail) or over-apply it (migrating principles that narrowly pass) without a positive anchor to calibrate against.

5. **Clarify that XVI is substantively distinct from VII and VIII** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: The grandfathering disclosure raises XVI as a gate-failure without addressing whether XVI composes with VII or VIII (which would trigger Criterion 3 failure and a different migration destination).
   - **Proposed change**: Add to the XVI grandfathering entry: "Criterion 3 status: PASS. XVI is not composable with VII or VIII. VII's bilateral carve-out (Principle VII, first bullet) demonstrates that XVI and VII are complementary-but-distinct, not composable. VIII's 'mechanical template-driven behavior' scope does not cover the 3-stage pipeline's LLM gap-filling protocol or objective.yml pinning discipline. If XVI is migrated, it MUST NOT be merged into VII or VIII's body."
   - **Rationale**: The VII bilateral carve-out ("narrowed by Principle XVI for LLM gap-filling output") is structural evidence of distinctness. Merged content in VII would require that carve-out to reference its own body, which is incoherent.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Spec 070 cycle 2 folds XVI's content into VII or VIII's operational guidance, creating a circular self-reference in VII's first bullet and losing XVI's domain-specific enforcement contracts.

6. **Distinguish VI's Criterion 1 and Criterion 2 failure modes** (Priority: P2)
   - **Current state**: The grandfathering disclosure characterizes VI as "judgment-laden 'when the artifact drives behavior'" — framing this as a Criterion 1 failure (no feasible automated check).
   - **Proposed change**: Update the VI grandfathering entry to: "Criterion 1 status: BORDERLINE. VI's operative predicate ('consumed by automation or agents to make decisions') admits a bounded check against the known consumer list (SKILL.md, conversus.yml, preset files, reference files, templates). Criterion 2 status: FAIL. 'When the artifact drives behavior' requires judgment to evaluate for novel artifact types not in the known consumer list." Route migration accordingly: the format-choice guidance (the Criterion 2 failure) belongs in CONTRIBUTING.md; the bounded-consumer-list enforcement could, in principle, be retained if tightened to name specific file types.
   - **Rationale**: Conflating Criterion 1 and Criterion 2 failures obscures remediation paths. A Criterion 1 failure is irredeemable (no check is feasible); a Criterion 2 failure is tightenable (narrow the scope to remove the judgment requirement).
   - **Risk if ignored**: VI's borderline-Criterion-1 content is discarded when tightening its wording could rehabilitate it, weakening the enforcement surface for execution-artifact format discipline.

7. **Add a CI lint for grandfathering-disclosure completeness** (Priority: P3)
   - **Current state**: The grandfathering disclosure is a prose paragraph with no mechanical completeness check. As principles are added or migrated, the disclosure drifts.
   - **Proposed change**: Add to the Governance section: "The grandfathering disclosure MUST enumerate every principle that fails any gate criterion. A CI lint (or equivalent manual check in the amendment process) MUST verify that every numbered principle either: (a) has no entry in the disclosure (implying it passes all three criteria), or (b) has a named entry explaining which criteria fail. Principles added after v2.4.0 MUST NOT appear in the disclosure — they must pass the gate or be routed to operational guidance at amendment time."
   - **Rationale**: This is the XXVI meta-test pattern applied to the gate itself. XXVI exists precisely to prevent silent coverage gaps in parametrized lists; the grandfathering disclosure is exactly such a list.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Future amendments add grandfathered principles informally (by omitting gate analysis) without disclosure, eroding the gate's authority.

8. **Record the migration SIR stream-crossing findings explicitly** (Priority: P3)
   - **Current state**: The spec 070 cycle 2 migration decisions for VI and X are not validated in this document against XVII's content-classification taxonomy. The migration SIR (wherever it lives) either validates them or does not.
   - **Proposed change**: Require that every migration SIR include a XVII-classification table for each migrated sub-bullet: `| Sub-bullet text | XVII classification | Migration destination | Validation |`. This table is the mechanical check for migration-routing correctness.
   - **Rationale**: XVII's taxonomy is the gate's own referenced framework ("The gate inherits XVII's vocabulary; routing decisions to operational guidance follow XVII's execution-logic vs. contribution-guidelines distinction"). Using it mechanically for each sub-bullet prevents stream-crossing by construction.
   - **Risk if ignored**: Migrations continue to route execution-logic sub-bullets to CONTRIBUTING.md and contribution-guideline sub-bullets to reference files, producing the exact silent-failure mode XVII was designed to prevent, now at migration time rather than amendment time.
