### Dangerous Contradictions

- **[XVI plain-language clause: Criterion 1 failure vs. Criterion 2 failure]**
  - **gate-faithfulness claims**: "XVI's plain-language sub-bullet is the only Criterion 2 failure (Principle XVI, lines 694–698): The plain-language requirement... No mechanical check can determine whether a given explanation satisfies this criterion" (gate-faithfulness, Alignment section, fourth bullet). gate-faithfulness frames this as a falsifiability/scope failure — the check cannot be automated because adequacy judgment is required.
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims**: "The disclosure conflates [Criterion 1 and Criterion 2 failures]. The distinction matters because Criterion 1 failure means no automated check is feasible, while Criterion 2 failure means the scope is too vague — these point to different remediation paths (remove the clause vs. tighten the wording)" (cross-principle-coherence, Missed Opportunities, fifth bullet).
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If the plain-language clause is a Criterion 2 failure (gate-faithfulness's frame), tightening its wording could rehabilitate it to constitutional status — e.g., "plugin recommendations MUST include an explanation matching the pattern `{N}% of agents are at their best possible position`" with grep-detectable structure. If it is a Criterion 1 failure (cross-principle-coherence's frame), no wording change rescues it because no automated check is feasible for explanation adequacy. A future amendment author reading the v3.0.0 SIR will apply whichever remediation path the SIR implies: if the SIR says Criterion 2, they will attempt tightening; if it says Criterion 1, they will not. These lead to different constitutional outcomes.
  - **Suggested resolution**: gate-faithfulness's substantive position is narrower than it appears. Gate-faithfulness acknowledges "No mechanical check can determine whether a given explanation satisfies this criterion" — that is a Criterion 1 observation. The synthesis should state that the plain-language clause fails both: Criterion 1 because no automated check can assess explanation adequacy, and Criterion 2 because the scope-qualifier ("alongside numerical outputs") does not bound the requirement to a greppable pattern. cross-principle-coherence's recommendation (Recommendation #6) to "clarify that XVI is substantively distinct from VII and VIII" and gate-faithfulness's Recommendation #8 ("preserve XVI's plain-language clause as MUST in receiving operational guidance") are both compatible once the dual-criterion failure is explicit.

- **[VI migration destination: CONTRIBUTING.md vs. SKILL.md references]**
  - **gate-faithfulness claims**: "The operational guidance document receiving VI's content MUST include a concrete, falsifiable rule: 'Any file referenced by a SKILL.md load trigger or config-conditional trigger MUST NOT be a freeform markdown file'" (gate-faithfulness, Recommendation #1). gate-faithfulness does not challenge CONTRIBUTING.md as the receiving document; it focuses on what enforcement content must survive migration there.
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims**: "Sub-bullets that constrain what goes into SKILL.md, conversus.yml, and templates are execution logic and belong in SKILL.md operational guidance (or the relevant reference file)... CONTRIBUTING.md is AGENTS.md-equivalent here" (cross-principle-coherence, Recommendation #2, "Apply Principle XVII taxonomy to VI migration routing").
  - **Why this is dangerous**: gate-faithfulness's Recommendation #1 places the load-trigger lint in CONTRIBUTING.md (the "receiving document" for VI). cross-principle-coherence establishes that CONTRIBUTING.md is read by coding agents during development, not by the agent runtime during deliberation. A lint rule placed in CONTRIBUTING.md is invisible to the runtime. If both positions are adopted, the load-trigger lint gets placed in a document where it cannot enforce the behavior it is designed to catch — exactly the silent-failure mode Principle XVII was created to prevent. The two reviews' combined output would produce a formally correct lint rule that is practically inert.
  - **Suggested resolution**: gate-faithfulness should yield on destination: the load-trigger lint belongs in SKILL.md references or a runtime-consumed reference file, not CONTRIBUTING.md. The content of gate-faithfulness's Recommendation #1 (the specific falsifiable rule text) is valuable and should be preserved; only the receiving document changes. cross-principle-coherence's Recommendation #2 provides the XVII-grounded rationale for this correction. These positions become compatible if gate-faithfulness's "receiving operational guidance document" is read as SKILL.md-adjacent rather than CONTRIBUTING.md.

- **[X's error-handling sub-bullet: migrate vs. delete]**
  - **gate-faithfulness claims**: Recommendation #2 promotes X's `summary/final.md` requirement to MUST in the receiving operational guidance document (docs/output-conventions.md). The implicit frame is that X's content generally migrates to that destination; gate-faithfulness does not address the error-handling sub-bullet separately.
  - **cross-principle-coherence claims**: "X's third bullet — 'Errors should never pass silently' — is substantively identical to V's third bullet: 'Output validation emits warnings for malformed output but does NOT block file writes.' Migrating X to docs/output-conventions.md strands a runtime-enforcement rule in a contribution-guideline document and creates a duplicate of V's content in a separate file, violating XI" (cross-principle-coherence, Missed Opportunities, third bullet). The proposed change is deletion, not relocation.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If gate-faithfulness's approach (migrate X to docs/output-conventions.md) is adopted without cross-principle-coherence's deletion of the error-handling sub-bullet, the migration creates an XI violation: two authoritative sources for the same rule (V in the constitution and docs/output-conventions.md). Future amendments to V will not automatically update docs/output-conventions.md. Reviews citing V will produce different verdicts than reviews citing docs/output-conventions.md if the two drift. This is precisely the failure mode XI was ratified to prevent.
  - **Suggested resolution**: gate-faithfulness's Recommendation #2 should be read as applying specifically to the `summary/final.md` sub-bullet of X — the one mechanically checkable requirement. cross-principle-coherence's Recommendation #3 (delete the error-handling sub-bullet) should be adopted for that specific sub-bullet. The two positions can coexist: migrate `summary/final.md` content, delete the error-handling content, and classify the remaining sub-bullets (flat is better than nested; sparse is better than dense; hard-to-explain = bad design) individually per the XVII taxonomy before routing them.

### Tensions

- **[Locus of XVI's mixed-status documentation: grandfathering disclosure vs. SIR]**
  - **gate-faithfulness's position**: The v3.0.0 SIR must document XVI's mixed status — specifically the clause-by-clause retention/migration map (Recommendation #4) and the path taxonomy definition (Recommendation #3). gate-faithfulness frames the SIR as the primary audit-trail vehicle.
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: "A `**Partial upgrade note (v2.3.2)**` annotation on the grandfathering disclosure entry for XVI would close this gap" (cross-principle-coherence, Missed Opportunities, first bullet). The grandfathering disclosure in the Governance section itself should be annotated to reflect that the v2.3.2 extension changed XVI's gate status from uniform failure to mixed.
  - **Nature of tension**: These address the same informational gap via different documents. The SIR is read during amendment review; the Governance section is read during ongoing compliance checks and future amendment drafting. If XVI's mixed status is documented only in the SIR, a future reviewer reading the constitution itself sees a principle labeled as grandfathered-failing without the partial-upgrade qualification — they may over-apply the gate and migrate XVI's mechanically-verifiable content unnecessarily. If it is documented only in the Governance section's grandfathering disclosure, a future SIR author may not find the precedent they need.
  - **Coordination needed**: Both documents should receive updates. The Governance section's grandfathering disclosure should carry the partial-upgrade annotation (cross-principle-coherence's proposal) and the v3.0.0 SIR should carry the clause-by-clause map (gate-faithfulness's proposal). These are not redundant — one is discovery-time context (the disclosure) and one is amendment-time audit trail (the SIR). The synthesis recommendation should specify both.

- **[Load-trigger lint as execution-logic enforcement vs. contribution-guideline guidance]**
  - **gate-faithfulness's position**: "The load-trigger lint is a valid partial check that SHOULD be carried into the receiving operational guidance document as a concrete enforcement mechanism" (gate-faithfulness, Missed Opportunities, first bullet). gate-faithfulness frames the lint as salvageable enforcement content from VI.
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: Routing VI's execution-logic sub-bullets to CONTRIBUTING.md is an XVII violation because those sub-bullets are "runtime-enforcement constraints... consumed by the agent runtime during deliberation" (cross-principle-coherence, Recommendation #2). CONTRIBUTING.md is not read by the runtime.
  - **Nature of tension**: gate-faithfulness values the lint's enforcement content; cross-principle-coherence values the lint's correct placement per XVII. These do not contradict at the level of principle — both agree the lint should exist. The tension is about where it lives. A lint placed in CONTRIBUTING.md satisfies gate-faithfulness's concern (the content survives migration) but not cross-principle-coherence's concern (it reaches the runtime). A lint placed in SKILL.md references satisfies both, but gate-faithfulness's Recommendation #1 does not make this placement explicit.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should clarify that "receiving operational guidance document" in gate-faithfulness's Recommendation #1 means a runtime-consumed document (SKILL.md reference), not CONTRIBUTING.md, which is where Spec 070 cycle 2 routed VI's content. This requires cross-principle-coherence's XVII routing critique to be acknowledged as a correction to the migration destination, and gate-faithfulness's lint content to be redirected accordingly.

- **[Criterion 1 borderline vs. definitive failure for VI]**
  - **gate-faithfulness's position**: "Criterion 2 kills VI via exact template match" (gate-faithfulness, Alignment). The framing treats VI's Criterion 1 status as secondary, with Criterion 2 as the decisive failure mode. gate-faithfulness does not characterize VI as borderline on Criterion 1.
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: "VI fails Criterion 2 more cleanly than Criterion 1. VI's operative predicate... admits a bounded check against the known consumer list (SKILL.md, conversus.yml, preset files, reference files, templates)" (cross-principle-coherence, Off-Base Assumptions, second bullet). cross-principle-coherence explicitly labels VI as borderline on Criterion 1.
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews agree VI should be migrated out of the constitution. But the borderline-vs.-definitive framing affects remediation options and future restoration criteria. If VI is definitively non-compliant on Criterion 1 (no feasible check), a future amendment can restore VI only by fundamentally changing what VI prescribes. If VI is borderline on Criterion 1 (feasible check for the known consumer list), a future amendment could restore VI by tightening the scope to the bounded consumer list and naming the check explicitly.
  - **Coordination needed**: The v3.0.0 SIR should state both criteria failure modes, with precision about their character: Criterion 2 is the definitive failure (scope-qualifier failure, not tightenable without restructuring the principle); Criterion 1 is borderline (a bounded check is feasible for the known consumer list but not for the principle's stated scope of "any artifact consumed by automation"). This framing gives future amendment authors accurate information about restoration paths without implying VI is easily rehabilitated.

- **[Gate's worked examples: calibration sufficiency vs. asymmetry risk]**
  - **gate-faithfulness's position**: The gate's worked examples function correctly as calibration instruments — they identify the failure spectrum and map Criterion 1 and Criterion 3 failures explicitly. gate-faithfulness does not propose adding a passing-borderline example.
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: "The gate's calibration is asymmetric — failure examples without a passing-borderline anchor cause reviewers to apply an overly strict standard and over-migrate principles with partial compliance" (cross-principle-coherence, Missed Opportunities, fourth bullet). Recommendation #4 proposes adding a passing borderline example citing XXVIII's SIR.
  - **Nature of tension**: gate-faithfulness accepts the existing calibration as adequate; cross-principle-coherence sees a gap that creates over-migration risk. These positions do not contradict about the gate's current failures — they diverge on whether the gate's calibration instruments are complete. The tension is between "the gate works as-is" and "the gate's asymmetry will cause systematic errors in future amendments."
  - **Coordination needed**: cross-principle-coherence's Recommendation #4 is additive and low-cost — it adds a worked example, not a criterion change. gate-faithfulness does not oppose it; it simply does not address it. The synthesis should include it as a standalone improvement recommendation that does not depend on resolving any of the contradictions above.
