**Rationale:** GF's core concern is well-founded: the rehabilitation path is the practically important piece of information. GF's annotation modification costs one phrase. CPC's substantive concern — that the annotation should be a discovery-time summary, not a lengthy analysis — is satisfied by the pointer to the SIR. **Rejected position:** CPC's "fails Criterion 2" annotation alone was not adopted as sufficient. CPC's strongest argument — that the annotation is a discovery-time tool and "fails Criterion 2" accurately records the failure without being affirmatively misleading — is technically correct but functionally insufficient. The annotation is routinely consulted during compliance checks without accompanying SIR context. An annotation that records a failure without identifying which failure is decisive creates a systematic navigation error: future amendment authors will routinely attempt the wrong rehabilitation path. CPC does not dispute this concern and does not oppose the addition; the ruling extends the annotation by the minimum phrase that forecloses the misleading inference.

**Required changes:** (a) The grandfathering disclosure entry for Principle XVI (in CONSTITUTION-v2.5.0-pre-migration.md Governance section) must be updated: for the plain-language explanation clause, the existing annotation phrase (currently stating only Criterion 2 failure) must be replaced with: "the plain-language explanation clause fails Criterion 1 and Criterion 2; Criterion 1 is the decisive barrier (see v3.0.0 SIR for rehabilitation path analysis and structured-schema rehabilitation warning)."
