### Position Summary

Of the nine original recommendations, zero are withdrawn, five are modified (Recommendations 1, 2, 4, 5, and 8), and four survive without modification (Recommendations 3, 6, 7, and 9). Two new recommendations emerged from the cross-review process: the XVII per-sub-bullet classification table (P1) and the cycle 2A procedural standing note (P2).

The most significant change in my thinking was caused by cross-principle-coherence's identification of the XVII destination error in Recommendation 1 and the XI violation in Recommendation 2. In both cases, my original recommendations were content-correct but destination-wrong: I correctly identified what enforcement content must survive migration but failed to apply Principle XVII's execution-logic vs. contribution-guideline taxonomy to determine where that content must live. The result was a recommendation that would have placed runtime-enforced rules in a document the runtime does not read (XVII violation) and migrated a duplicate of V's canonical text to a second authoritative location (XI violation). These are not minor formatting concerns — they are the exact silent-failure modes Principles XVII and XI were created to prevent. The modification to Recommendation 1 (route the lint to SKILL.md references, not CONTRIBUTING.md) and the modification to Recommendation 2 (delete the error-handling sub-bullet rather than relocating it) are the most substantive changes in this revision, and both are prompted by the cross-principle-coherence cross-review's structural analysis rather than by any dispute about the gate criteria themselves.

The highest-priority recommendation that should survive into the final synthesis is the new XVII per-sub-bullet classification table requirement. This is a structural safeguard that converts a routing error — which manifested in my original Recommendation 1 and would have manifested silently in the migration SIRs — into a mandatory, auditable deliverable. The classification table forces each sub-bullet to be explicitly assigned to an XVII category and a specific destination document before migration proceeds. It would have caught both the VI destination error and the X error-handling sub-bullet duplication before they were committed. Making it a required element of every migration SIR is the systemic fix that prevents the ad hoc destination decisions that produced these problems, and it is grounded in a principle — XVII — whose authority both cross-reviews independently confirmed as the governing routing framework for this migration.
