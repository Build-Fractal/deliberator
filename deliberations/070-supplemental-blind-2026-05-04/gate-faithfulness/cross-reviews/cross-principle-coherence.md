### Tensions

- **XVI's Criterion Failure: Criterion 2 Framing vs. Criterion 1/2 Conflation**
  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: the grandfathering disclosure conflates Criterion 1 and Criterion 2 failures for XVI's plain-language clause. The distinction matters: Criterion 1 failure is irredeemable (no check is feasible); Criterion 2 failure is potentially tightenable (narrow the scope). Without distinguishing them, the remediation path for a future amendment is unclear — it would not be known whether tightening the wording could rehabilitate the clause. (cross-principle-coherence, Missed Opportunities, "Criterion 1 vs. Criterion 2 conflation in the XVI grandfathering rationale.")
  - **gate-faithfulness's position**: the plain-language sub-bullet fails Criterion 2 specifically — a mechanical check can detect the presence of a numerical output but cannot assess whether an accompanying explanation constitutes adequate plain-language. This framing treats the clause as a Criterion 2 failure without addressing whether Criterion 1 also applies or whether the clause is rehabilitable. (gate-faithfulness, Alignment, "XVI's plain-language sub-bullet is the only Criterion 2 failure.")
  - **Nature of tension**: both reviews agree the plain-language clause fails the gate. But gate-faithfulness is satisfied with naming Criterion 2 as the failure mode, while cross-principle-coherence notes the conflation obscures whether the failure is Criterion 1 (irredeemable) or Criterion 2 (tightenable). The practical stakes: if the clause is a Criterion 2 failure only, a future amendment that specifies a structured output format (e.g., required JSON field pairing every numerical output with a text key) could rehabilitate it. If it is also a Criterion 1 failure, no tightening will help. The migration SIR's framing of the failure type shapes future amendment paths.
  - **Coordination needed**: the migration SIR should state explicitly that XVI's plain-language clause fails Criterion 2 (unfalsifiable adequacy scope) and that Criterion 1 may also apply to the extent no check can evaluate explanation quality even with tighter wording. The SIR should note that a future amendment proposing a structured output requirement (machine-checkable form) could address the Criterion 2 failure specifically. This preserves the current migration decision while keeping the rehabilitation path visible.

- **VI's Criterion Failure Degree: Unambiguous vs. Borderline**

  - **cross-principle-coherence's position**: the "Partial upgrade note (v2.3.2)" annotation should appear in the grandfathering disclosure itself — in the governance section of the constitution, immediately after the XVI entry. (cross-principle-coherence, Recommendation 1.)
