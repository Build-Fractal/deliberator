### Dangerous Contradictions

- **VI migration verdict: correct vs. incorrectly decided**
  - **contrarian-vi-x-xvi-defender claims**: "This review rates the VI migration as **incorrectly decided** unless the principled distinction is supplied." (Verdict-Relevant Summary — Principle VI). The argument rests on the asymmetry claim: retained Principles IX, XV, and XXIV carry analogous judgment-laden qualifiers, and the migration record articulates no principled distinction between those and VI's "when the artifact drives behavior."
  - **gate-faithfulness claims**: "The verdicts are sound. VI fails Criterion 2 unambiguously: the operative conditional 'when the artifact drives behavior' (line 351) requires semantic judgment that no automated check can substitute for, and the gate text's exact failure template… maps directly onto VI's 'when it's just for human orientation, markdown is appropriate' carve-out (line 360)." (Executive Summary). The asymmetry is addressed in Missed Opportunities — IX distinguishes itself via its accompanying mechanical MUST block; VI has no such block.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If both positions were adopted, the v3.0.0 SIR would simultaneously assert that the migration was correct (gate-faithfulness) and that it requires a principled-distinction justification before it can stand (contrarian). These commitments are logically exclusive: either the existing gate text is sufficient to distinguish VI from IX and the migration proceeds, or it is insufficient and the migration is suspended pending the distinction's articulation. Adopting both produces an SIR that authorizes and conditionally blocks the same migration in the same document.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The gate-faithfulness position contains the principled distinction the contrarian demands — it is just not foregrounded as a response to the asymmetry challenge. The resolution is for gate-faithfulness to yield on presentation: the v3.0.0 SIR MUST include an explicit Asymmetry Resolution section (as gate-faithfulness's Recommendation 5 already proposes) stating that IX's scope-qualifier is accompanied by a mechanical MUST block that independently satisfies Criterion 1, whereas VI's scope-qualifier is the only trigger condition for its sole prescription and carries no accompanying mechanical block. If that distinction is made explicit in the SIR, the contrarian's conditional ("unless the principled distinction is supplied") is satisfied, and the contradiction collapses. The contrarian should acknowledge that the distinction exists and was demonstrated in the gate-faithfulness review, even if it was absent from the prior SIR record.

- **X migration correctness: premature vs. sound**
  - **contrarian-vi-x-xvi-defender claims**: "The migration to operational guidance was **premature**; path (c) refactoring would have been the correct response." (Verdict-Relevant Summary — Principle X). The argument is that one verifiable sub-bullet (`summary/final.md` parity test) satisfies Criterion 1's "at least one form" standard, and that the migration conflated rhetorical scaffolding with substantive content.
  - **gate-faithfulness claims**: X "fails Criteria 1 and 2 simultaneously" (Executive Summary); "'readable' (line 487) is the gate's own worked example of a non-mechanical requirement" (Alignment). Gate-faithfulness does not dispute the `summary/final.md` sub-bullet's checkability — it explicitly recommends promoting it to MUST in the receiving document (Recommendation 2) — but does not concede that this sub-bullet rescues the principle from Criterion 1 failure.
  - **Why this is dangerous**: If both positions were adopted, the implementation would attempt to simultaneously (a) execute full migration of X to operational guidance (gate-faithfulness) and (b) retain the mechanically verifiable sub-bullets in the constitution under a renamed principle "Output Structure Invariants" (contrarian Recommendation 2). A synthesis document that says "migrate X but also retain its verifiable core as a renamed constitutional principle" produces two constitutional artifacts covering overlapping ground, violating Principle XI (Single Source of Truth) and creating the exact content-in-two-places pattern the constitution explicitly prohibits.
  - **Suggested resolution**: The disagreement is narrower than it appears. Both reviews agree that the `summary/final.md` constraint and flat-hierarchy constraint are valuable and should survive. The dispute is only about the vehicle: constitutional principle vs. operational guidance MUST. Gate-faithfulness should yield on the path-(c) option for X and evaluate it seriously: if the X principle is refactored to strip the "readability counts" headline framing, rename it "Output Structure Invariants," and elevate only the four sub-bullets as the principle's text with an explicit Verification block, would the refactored principle pass Criterion 1? Gate-faithfulness's own Recommendation 2 effectively performs this refactoring at the operational guidance level. If the refactored content passes all three criteria as a constitutional principle, the contrarian's path-(c) outcome is correct and migration was premature. The synthesis should evaluate the refactored text against the gate criteria rather than treating the original X framing as the only option.

- **Whether the IX/XV/XXIV asymmetry argument is decisive and unaddressed**
  - **contrarian-vi-x-xvi-defender claims**: "No such distinction appears in the available record." (Missed Opportunities — Principled-distinction gap). The claim is that the migration case for VI has a structural defect because no prior SIR articulated why VI's qualifier fails while IX's, XV's, and XXIV's analogous qualifiers pass.

(Missed Opportunities — IX asymmetry argument must be addressed proactively).   - **Why this is dangerous**: The contrarian treats the absence of the distinction in the prior record as evidence the distinction does not exist; gate-faithfulness treats the absence as a documentation gap that should be filled. If both positions are recorded without resolution, the audit trail will contain a finding that the migration was incorrectly decided (contrarian) alongside a finding that the migration was correctly decided and the distinction merely needs documentation (gate-faithfulness). A future reviewer cannot tell which finding governs. The appearance of a documentation gap and the appearance of a substantive logical failure look identical in an audit trail.
  - **Suggested resolution**: Gate-faithfulness should yield on the procedural point: the contrarian is correct that the distinction was not articulated in the prior SIR, and that absence is a real audit-trail defect. ### Tensions

- **Directory-context resolution as Criterion 1 sketch vs. Criterion 2 scope-qualifier failure**
  - **contrarian-vi-x-xvi-defender's position**: The "drives behavior" qualifier "resolves to directory membership in the project's architectural directories — a CI-detectable structural default." A lint flagging prose `.md` files in `presets/`, `templates/`, and `schema/` is "both feasible and directory-detectable without content analysis." This resolves Criterion 1 by supplying a concrete check. (Missed Opportunities — Directory-context resolution; Recommendation 1)
  - **gate-faithfulness's position**: The load-trigger CI lint "has genuine partial validity: it is a real mechanical check for the most common violation pattern. It does not rescue VI from Criterion 2 failure because VI's text covers all artifacts that 'drive behavior,' not only load-trigger-referenced files, and the exception clause at lines 359–362 ('when a markdown artifact exists purely for human orientation, markdown is appropriate') requires semantic judgment that the lint cannot make." (Missed Opportunities — Contrarian's load-trigger CI lint has genuine partial validity)
  - **Nature of tension**: Both reviews accept that directory-based linting provides a real mechanical check. They disagree about whether that check satisfies Criterion 1 (contrarian yes; gate-faithfulness no — partial only) and they reach different diagnoses of why the lint falls short. Gate-faithfulness argues the failure is Criterion 2 (scope-qualifier requires semantic judgment about the file's purpose), not Criterion 1. The contrarian argues that directory membership collapses the purpose-judgment into a structural check. These pull in different directions: the contrarian is narrowing the principle's scope to make it checkable; gate-faithfulness is reading the principle's text at face value and finding the scope remains semantically bounded. Neither position is frivolous.
  - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should determine whether VI's principle text, as written, supports the directory-membership reading of "drives behavior" or whether that reading requires amending the principle's text. If the directory-membership reading is a plausible textual interpretation, the contrarian's Criterion 1 sketch is valid and the migration decision turns on whether the exception clause at lines 359–362 still imports Criterion 2 failure independently. If the directory-membership reading requires amending the text to add "behavior-driving is defined as membership in `presets/`, `templates/`, or `schema/`," then the contrarian is implicitly proposing a path-(c)-style amendment rather than arguing the existing principle passes the gate as written. The synthesis should be explicit about which of these is the intended claim.

- **Sub-bullet independence as Criterion 1 rescue for X vs. headline-level framing as principle identity**
  - **contrarian-vi-x-xvi-defender's position**: "Criterion 1 requires 'at least one form of automated check.' The predictable-output-tree sub-bullet is independently CI-verifiable via parity test. Gate-faithfulness treats the principle as a unit: the headline's prescriptive claim is the operative requirement, and the sub-bullet is a supporting illustration.   - **Coordination needed**: The synthesis should determine which granularity the gate intends. If that clarification is adopted, it supports the contrarian's path-(c) argument for X directly.
