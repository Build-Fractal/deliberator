# Remaining Disputes

The revision process resolved most fundamental conflicts through productive convergence on governance priorities and constitutional methodology. However, two narrow disputes remain:

### Remaining Disputes

- **Dispute: Constitutional Analysis Conditional Value**
  - **My claim**: My original constitutional coordination analysis (Recommendations 1-5, now modified to be conditional) has substantive value for future properly-scoped amendments, even though governance validity must be established first. [From my revision: "The amendment may ultimately strengthen constitutional coherence as I originally argued, but that strengthening can only occur through constitutionally sound pathways."]
  - **Opposing position(s)**: Precedent-auditor's revision suggests that constitutional merit analysis should be subordinated entirely to procedural validity: "constitutional merit analysis without first confirming the amendment's procedural foundation is sound." Purist's revision treats governance validity as potentially invalidating technical constitutional work entirely.
  - **Why I will not concede**: Constitutional coordination principles don't become invalid due to governance process failures. The technical constitutional analysis identifies real coordination requirements that will apply to any future persistence-related amendment, regardless of tier placement.
  - **Counter-argument to their position**: Dismissing constitutional coordination analysis due to governance problems conflates procedural validity with substantive constitutional merit. These are separate analytical domains - governance process validity determines whether an amendment can proceed, while constitutional coordination analysis determines what coordination work is needed if it does proceed.
  - **Proposed resolution path**: Preserve the conditional constitutional coordination analysis as framework for future amendments while acknowledging that governance validity is the immediate blocking issue.

- **Dispute: Override Precedent Review Authority**
  - **My claim**: Override precedent application review (Priority P2 in my revision) should be conducted as a separate analytical track from tier placement analysis, since precedent scope affects all future amendments regardless of tier. [From my revision: "Override-with-rationale precedent has established scope limitations. Procedural validity is foundational to constitutional analysis."]
  - **Opposing position(s)**: Precedent-auditor's revision claims precedent methodology and tier placement are interdependent: "tier placement determines which precedent rules apply." They argue for coordinated treatment rather than separate tracks.
  - **Why I will not concede**: Precedent scope violations affect constitutional governance integrity across all tiers and amendment types. Deferring precedent review until tier placement creates a dependency that doesn't actually exist - precedent rules should apply uniformly regardless of tier.
  - **Counter-argument to their position**: Their coordination approach conflates two distinct governance issues. Tier placement affects which constitutional rules apply to content, but precedent methodology affects which governance procedures apply to process. These operate at different analytical levels.
  - **Proposed resolution path**: The synthesizer must choose whether precedent review proceeds independently or as part of coordinated governance analysis. Both approaches have constitutional merit, but they represent different methodological commitments.

### Final Position Statement

**Non-Negotiables**:
1. **Governance validity must be established before constitutional coordination work proceeds.** Tier placement, differentiated deadlines contradiction, and precedent scope issues are logically prior to technical constitutional analysis. Constitutional governance integrity requires valid foundations.
2. **Constitutional coordination analysis retains conditional value for future amendments.** Even with governance problems blocking this specific amendment, the coordination principles identified apply to any future persistence-related amendments and should be preserved as framework.
3. **Evidence sufficiency for universality claims must be constitutional prerequisite.** Universal principles require multi-product-family evidence or explicit provisional status before ratification, not post-ratification documentation.

**Flexibility**:
1. **Constitutional debt framing and scope.** Willing to accept compound formulation (tier-evidence mismatch + precedent-scope drift) or separate treatment, as long as the governance gaps receive systematic attention rather than ad-hoc resolution.
2. **Precedent review coordination methodology.** Can accept independent precedent analysis track or coordinated governance analysis approach, as long as precedent scope authority limitations are addressed and don't defer indefinitely.
3. **Technical recommendation implementation sequencing.** Willing to defer detailed constitutional coordination specifications until governance foundation is established, as long as the analytical framework is preserved for future application.
