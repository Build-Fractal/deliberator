## Dangerous Contradictions

### Differentiated Deadlines Constitutional Status

- **purist claims**: Modified recommendation that "if amendment remains at Tier 1, remove differentiated deadlines; if demoted to Tier 2, differentiated deadlines are constitutionally appropriate at suite tier" (Recommendation 2 disposition, lines 18-19)
- **tier-coherence-auditor claims**: Original position that differentiated deadlines violate universality regardless of tier placement, with no tier-conditional modification in my revision
- **Why this is dangerous**: If we accept purist's tier-conditional logic, it suggests that constitutional violations at one tier become acceptable accommodations at another tier. This undermines tier coherence by implying that what violates universal principles somehow becomes appropriate for suite governance. The differentiated deadlines either violate the principle of universal applicability or they don't—tier placement shouldn't change constitutional validity.
- **Suggested resolution**: purist should acknowledge that differentiated deadlines remain constitutionally problematic even at Tier 2, but are less damaging there since suite-tier can legitimately accommodate product-specific implementation details. The resolution is about damage containment, not constitutional validity.
